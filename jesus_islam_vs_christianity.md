## 1. What Christianity Claims About Jesus

Mainstream Christianity (since the Council of Nicaea, 325 CE) teaches:
- Jesus is **God** — the second person of a Trinity.
- Jesus is the **Son of God** in a literal, divine sense.
- Jesus was **crucified to die for the sins of humanity**.
- Jesus is the **savior**, and salvation comes through belief in his divinity and resurrection.

Below, we examine these claims against the Quran and the words of the scholars.

## 4. Where Did the Trinity Come From?

The word "Trinity" does not appear in the Bible. The doctrine was formally defined at:
- **The Council of Nicaea (325 CE)** — convened by the Roman Emperor Constantine, a politician, not a prophet.
- **The Council of Constantinople (381 CE)** — finalized the doctrine.

For the first three centuries after Jesus, the doctrine of the Trinity did not exist in its current form. The early followers of Jesus — including the disciples — believed he was the Messiah, a prophet, a servant of God. The doctrine of his full divinity was a later theological development, influenced by Greek philosophy and Roman politics.

The Quran addresses this directly:
> *"The Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, was but a messenger of Allah and His word which He directed to Mary, and a soul [created at a command] from Him. So believe in Allah and His messengers. And do not say, 'Three'; desist — it is better for you. Indeed, Allah is but one God. Exalted is He above having a son."* (Quran, Surah An-Nisa 4:171)

He taught that Allah is his Lord and our Lord. He prayed in prostration. He fasted. He did not claim divinity. He did not ask to be worshipped.

To follow Jesus is to follow his message: **worship Allah alone, obey His law, and submit to His will.** That is Islam.
