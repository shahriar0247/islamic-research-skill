# Missing Some Prayers (But Not Abandoning Salah Entirely) and Whether Seeking Knowledge Still Earns Good Deeds

## A Research Article Based on Verbatim Quotes from Salafi Scholars

---

## The Question

> *If someone does not pray four out of the five daily prayers — he did not really abandon salah, he intends to pray next time or soon, and he is regretting — will seeking knowledge give him good deeds?*

This question has two parts:

1. **The ruling on a person who misses most prayers but does not completely abandon salah** — is he still a Muslim? What is his state?
2. **Does seeking knowledge still earn him good deeds (hasanat)?**

Both questions require careful answers based on the statements of the Salafi scholars, because there is a genuine difference of opinion on the first issue, and the answer to the second issue depends on it.

---

## Part 1: The Ruling on One Who Prays Some Prayers and Misses Others

### The Critical Distinction: Complete Abandonment vs. Partial Negligence

The Salafi scholars distinguish between two situations:

- **Complete abandonment (tark as-salah)** — a person gives up prayer altogether, deciding not to pray any of the five daily prayers.
- **Partial negligence (tark ba'd)** — a person prays some prayers and misses others out of laziness or negligence.

There is consensus that **complete abandonment** of prayer is a grave matter. The difference of opinion is whether it constitutes disbelief (kufr) that exits one from Islam. But the case of one who **prays some and misses others** is where the scholars differ more sharply.

---

### Position A: One Who Prays Even One or Two Prayers Is NOT a Kaafir (Ibn 'Uthaymeen, Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Albani)

#### Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymeen (rahimahullah)

> "What appears to be the case from the evidence is that he does not become a kaafir unless he gives up prayer all the time, in the sense that he has decided not to pray, so he does not pray Zuhr, 'Asr, Maghrib, 'Isha' or Fajr. This is the one who is a kaafir. **But if he prays one or two obligatory prayers, then he is not a kaafir and he cannot truly be described as having given up prayer.** The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: 'Between a man and shirk and kufr stands his giving up prayer (al-salaah)' and he did not say 'salaah (a prayer).'"

**Source:** al-Sharh al-Mumti' (2/26)
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/52923

He also said:

> "It seems to me that he is not a kaafir unless he does not pray at all. As for one who prays sometimes, he is not a kaafir because the Messenger (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said: 'Between a man and kufr and shirk there stands his giving up prayer.' He did not say 'giving up a prayer' (tarku salaatin) rather he said 'giving up [the] prayer' (tark al-salaah). This implies not praying altogether. Similarly he said: 'The covenant that distinguishes between us and them is prayer; whoever does not pray is a kaafir.' Based on that we say: **The one who prays sometimes and does not pray sometimes is not a kaafir.**"

**Source:** Majmoo' Fataawa Ibn 'Uthaymeen, 12/55
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/83165

#### Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (rahimahullah)

> "The one who prays sometimes and does not pray sometimes, if he resolves in his heart to give up prayer altogether, then he is inwardly a kaafir, i.e., it is between him and Allah, may He be exalted."

**Source:** Majmoo' al-Fataawa, 22/49; Sharh al-'Umdah, 2/94
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/52923

> **Key point:** According to Ibn Taymiyyah, one who prays sometimes and leaves sometimes is **not a kaafir** unless he **resolves in his heart** to give up prayer altogether.

#### Shaykh Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani (rahimahullah)

> "But if there is a man who does not deny (the legislation) of the prayer and who acknowledges its legislation, but with respect to action, he does not perform it, he does not pray — maybe he does not pray at all and maybe he prays sometimes — so in this case, if we said that this man has disbelieved, this statement would not apply to him at all, because **kufr (disbelief) is denial, and he is not denying** the legislation of the prayer..."

> "...then if we said that this man is a disbeliever, we would be contradicting the reality, because **this man is a believer**, a believer in the legislation of the prayer and a believer in all of Islam, so how can we declare him a disbeliever?"

**Source:** Silsilat al-Huda wa an-Nur (The Series of Guidance and Light), tape no. 8, Question #5
**URL:** https://abdurrahman.org/2014/10/10/the-ruling-on-the-one-who-abandons-prayer-shaykh-al-albaani/

### Position B: Missing Even a Single Prayer Deliberately = Kufr (Ibn Baz, Standing Committee, al-Fawzan)

#### Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Baz (rahimahullah)

Shaykh Ibn Baz held that one who leaves the prayer deliberately out of negligence (tahawun) — even while affirming its obligation — commits major kufr:

> "As for one who left the prayer deliberately then repented to Allah, he does NOT make them up; because leaving it deliberately is kufr, and the kaafir does not make up [prayers]. This is the correct view regarding leaving the prayer — even if he left it out of negligence (tahawun) and not denial (juhood), he becomes a kaafir by that... the correct and preponderant view in this issue: he is a kaafir with major kufr, so there is no qada upon him for what he left deliberately out of negligence."

**Source:** Fatwa no. 8055 (حكم قضاء الصلاة على من تركها عمداً)
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/8055/حكم-قضاء-الصلاة-على-من-تركها-عمداً

#### The Permanent Committee (Lajnah Daa'imah) — signed by Ibn Baz

> "As for the Salah you intentionally abandoned while believing in it being obligatory, the most sound of the two views held by scholars is that such an act constitutes major Kufr (disbelief). You should not make up for it, but you have to repent, regret it, and ask Allah's forgiveness for abandoning it. You should observe the five daily prayers regularly."

**Source:** Fatwa no. 6196 (Part 6/10-11)
**Members:** 'Abdullah ibn Qa'ud, 'Abdul-Razzaq 'Afifi, 'Abdul-'Aziz ibn Baz
**URL:** https://fawaaids.com/2024/11/27/making-up-a-missed-obligatory-prayer-ibn-taymiyyah-ibn-al-qayyim-ibn-hazm-ibn-baz-al-albani/

#### Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan (hafizahullah)

> "The scholars have said that whoever abandons the Prayer out of laziness, he becomes a disbeliever, in the correct saying — even if he acknowledges its obligation because there is no benefit in acknowledging its obligation if he does not implement it and he does not act upon it..."

**Source:** Sharh Usool-ith-Thalaathah, Lesson 50 (Part B), translated by Dawud Burbank
**URL:** https://abdurrahman.org/2018/06/08/the-head-of-the-affair-is-al-islaam-its-supporting-pillar-is-the-prayer-its-highest-pinnacle-is-jihaad/

> **NOTE:** Al-Fawzan's quote here addresses complete abandonment. A specific quote from him on the case of one who prays SOME prayers and misses others was NOT FOUND FROM SALAFI SOURCE. However, based on the view he adopts (Imam Ahmad's view), missing even one prayer deliberately until its time ends would constitute kufr.

### Summary of the Difference of Opinion

| Issue | Position A (Ibn Uthaymeen, Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Albani) | Position B (Ibn Baz, Standing Committee, al-Fawzan) |
|-------|------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| One who prays some prayers and misses others | **Not a kaafir** — remains a Muslim, but commits a major sin | **Kaafir (major kufr)** — if he deliberately misses even one prayer until its time ends |
| Basis | The Prophet said "al-salaah" (the prayer as a whole), not "salaah" (a single prayer) | The view of Imam Ahmad and a group of the Sahabah — leaving even one prayer deliberately = kufr |

IslamQA summarizes:

> "The issue of the ruling on one who does not pray out of laziness and carelessness is one of the most well-known issues concerning which there is a difference of opinion among the scholars. There are two well-known scholarly views concerning this matter: The first view is that he is Muslim, but he is an evildoer and sinner. This is the view of the majority of fuqaha. [...] The second view is that he is a disbeliever in the sense of major kufr."

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 285977
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/285977

> **IMPORTANT:** Both positions agree that missing prayers deliberately out of laziness is **at minimum a grave major sin**. The difference is only whether it reaches the level of kufr that exits one from Islam. No scholar considers negligence in prayer to be a trivial matter.

### On Regret and Intention to "Pray Soon"

Regret is a component of repentance, but it does **not** substitute for the missed prayer by performing it outside its prescribed time. According to the correct view held by al-Albani, Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, Ibn Taymiyyah, and Ibn al-Qayyim, **deliberately missed prayers are not made up (qada)**. Rather, the person must:

1. **Repent sincerely** to Allah
2. **Regret** what was missed
3. **Resolve** never to repeat it
4. **Pray the current prayers on time**
5. **Do many voluntary good deeds** and prayers

IslamQA states:

> "If you missed prayers with no excuse, you have committed a major sin and you have to repent to Allah. It is not valid for you to make it up according to the more correct of the two scholarly opinions."

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 111783
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/111783

> "The correct view on the issue of one who fails to fast or pray with no excuse is that he does not have to make up what he did not do; rather what he must do is repent, and pray and fast regularly from now on. It is mustahabb for him to do a lot of naafil (supererogatory) acts of worship, fasting and prayers, in the hope that Allah will accept his repentance."

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 197247
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/197247

Shaykh al-Albani said:

> "...such individuals [who leave out the prayer intentionally] cannot compensate for the great sin they commit of neglecting the prayer and allowing it to lapse beyond its (prescribed) time by introducing a prayer of their own accord, such as performing the Fajr prayer during the day or the Dhuhr prayer at night, claiming it to be 'making up' the missed prayers."

**Source:** Silsilah al-Huda wa al-Nur 146; Jami' al-Turath 2/241-243
**URL:** https://fawaaids.com/2024/11/27/making-up-a-missed-obligatory-prayer-ibn-taymiyyah-ibn-al-qayyim-ibn-hazm-ibn-baz-al-albani/

> **The "intention to pray soon" must be followed by immediate action** — praying the current prayers on time and sincere repentance for the past. Delaying repentance or relying on "I'll pray soon" without actually doing so is not sufficient.

## Part 2: Does Seeking Knowledge Still Earn Good Deeds for Such a Person?

The answer to this depends on which scholarly position one adopts regarding the person's state (Muslim vs. kaafir).

### If the Person Is Still a Muslim (Position A — Ibn Uthaymeen, Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Albani, majority of fuqaha):

**Yes, seeking knowledge earns him good deeds, and his good deeds are accepted.**

#### Nothing Cancels Out All Good Deeds Except Kufr

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah stated:

> "**Nothing cancels out good deeds except disbelief (kufr)**, because the one who dies as a believer will inevitably enter Paradise, and he will be brought out of Hell if he does enter it, but if all his good deeds are cancelled out, he will never enter Paradise. Good deeds can only be cancelled out by that which is their opposite, and nothing can be the opposite of all good deeds except disbelief. This is well known from the principles of Sunnah."

**Source:** Ibn Taymiyyah, As-Saarim al-Maslool, p. 55
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/81874

IslamQA states:

> "One of the basic principles among Ahl As-Sunnah wal-Jama`ah is that good deeds are not accepted if they are accompanied by disbelief, and that **nothing cancels out all good deeds except disbelief**."

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 81874
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/81874

> "You should realize that what you have heard about repentance, prayer and fasting not being accepted from one who has committed major sins is false, and it is speaking about Allah without knowledge. There is a great deal of evidence in the Book of Allah and the sunnah of His Messenger (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) to indicate that **Allah accepts His slave's repentance from all sins, no matter how great they are**."

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 65621
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/65621

> The view of the Khawarij and Mu'tazilah — that major sins cancel out ALL good deeds — is a rejected innovation. Ahl al-Sunnah hold that only kufr cancels out all good deeds.

---

#### Seeking Knowledge Is Itself a Great Good Deed (Hasanah)

**Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal** said:

> "Seeking knowledge is the best of deeds for the one whose intention is sound."

**Source:** Reported via Muhanna, cited in IslamQA fatwa no. 262984
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/262984

The Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said:

> "Whoever follows a path in the pursuit of knowledge, Allah will make a path to Paradise easy for him."

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari, Kitaab al-'Ilm, 10
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/10471

> "Whoever follows a path to seek knowledge, Allah will make easy for him the path to Paradise. The angels beat their wings in approval of the seeker of knowledge, and those who are in the heavens and on earth pray for forgiveness for the scholar, even the fish in the water. The superiority of the scholar over the worshipper is like the superiority of the moon over all other heavenly bodies. The scholars are the heirs of the Prophets, for the Prophets did not leave behind dinars or dirhams, rather they left behind knowledge, so whoever gains knowledge has gained great good fortune."

**Source:** Narrated by Abu'd-Dardaa', reported by al-Tirmidhi (2606); classed as saheeh by al-Albani
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/20191

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** explicitly listed seeking knowledge alongside prayer and jihad as actions done for Allah's sake:

> "Sincerity to Allah is: that you intend His noble Face and what He has of good by your actions: **from seeking knowledge, prayer, jihad**, and other affairs of the religion."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 21215
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/21215/ما-سبيل-الاخلاص-في-تعلم-العلم

He also said:

> "Turning towards studying and understanding the religion is from the best of actions."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 19669
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/19669/مسألة-في-فضل-التفقه-في-الدين

#### Good Deeds Erase Bad Deeds

Allah says:

> "And perform As-Salah (establish the prayer), at the two ends of the day and in some hours of the night [i.e. the five compulsory Salah (prayers)]. **Verily, the good deeds remove the evil deeds** (i.e. small sins)."

**Source:** Quran, Hud 11:114
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/3365

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said about this verse:

> "It is for all of my Ummah."

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari (503) and Sahih Muslim (2763)
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/3365

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** explained:

> "The greatest of good deeds is Tawhid, for by it Allah erases evil deeds. And repentance is a great good deed — sincere repentance erases the sin that is repented from... And **all righteous actions are among the causes of erasing evil deeds**: his prayers, his zakah, his fasting, his Hajj, his charity, his remembrance (dhikr) — his remembrance of Allah, all of this is among the causes of erasing evil deeds. 'Verily, the good deeds remove the evil deeds' [Hud 11:114]. **All acts of obedience are good deeds (hasanaat).**"

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 1531
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/1531/ما-هي-الحسنات-التي-تمحو-السيئات

> Since seeking knowledge is an act of obedience, it is included in "all acts of obedience" that Ibn Baz described as good deeds (hasanaat) which erase evil deeds.

---

#### A Note on the Hadith of Annulment of Good Deeds

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Whoever does not pray 'Asr, his good deeds will be annulled."

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari (553)
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/49698

IslamQA explains this hadith:

> "It seems from the hadith that there are two types of those who do not pray: those who do not pray at all, which annuls **all** their good deeds, and those who do not offer a particular prayer on a particular day, which annuls the good deeds of **that day**. So annulment of all good deeds happens to those who forsake all the prayers, and annulment of the good deeds of a particular day happens to the one who omits a particular prayer."

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 49698
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/49698

> **Key point:** Missing a specific prayer may annul the good deeds of **that day**, but it does not annul ALL good deeds unless one abandons prayer entirely. So the seeking of knowledge done on other days — and the repentance and good deeds that follow — are still accepted and rewarded.

---

### If the Person Is Considered a Kaafir (Position B — Ibn Baz, Standing Committee, al-Fawzan):

Under this view, if the person deliberately missed even one prayer until its time ended (out of laziness, no excuse), he would be considered a disbeliever at the time of that negligence. In that state, good deeds are not accepted until he repents and returns to Islam.

However, **once he repents and returns to Islam**, all his previous sins are forgiven, and his good deeds from that point forward — including seeking knowledge — are accepted. Allah says:

> "Say to those who have disbelieved, if they cease (from disbelief) their past will be forgiven."

**Source:** Quran, al-Anfal 8:38
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/197247

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Do you not know that Islam erases what [came before it]?"

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 197247
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/197247

> **Under both positions, the path forward is the same:** sincere repentance, establishing the prayer, and doing abundant good deeds — including seeking knowledge.

## Part 3: Seeking Knowledge as a Means to Return to Prayer

One of the most important points for the questioner is that **seeking knowledge can itself be the means by which Allah guides a person back to establishing the prayer**.

#### Shaykh Ibn Baz on the Causes of Guidance

> "And among the causes of guidance: **attending the circles of knowledge** of the people of knowledge, the scholars known for good, sitting with them, listening to the circles of knowledge, and benefiting — all of this is among the causes of guidance."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 15387
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/15387/من-أسباب-الهداية

#### Shaykh Ibn Baz on Seeking Knowledge Even with a Bad Intention

Remarkably, Shaykh Ibn Baz encouraged seeking knowledge **even if one's intention is corrupt**, because knowledge itself can lead to guidance and correction of intention:

> "He may learn in order to get a job or so it is said of him: 'a scholar,' then Allah guides him and he returns to the truth, as some of the Salaf said: **'We sought knowledge for other than Allah, but it refused to be except for Allah.'** So he should seek knowledge even if his intention is bad — he should not abandon knowledge. He should learn, strive, attend with the scholars, and ask his Lord to correct his intention."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 5970
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/5970/وجوب-تصحيح-النية-في-طلب-العلم

> **This is directly relevant to the questioner's situation.** If a person is negligent in prayer but turns to seeking knowledge, that very knowledge can be the means by which Allah softens his heart, increases his fear of Allah, and leads him back to establishing the prayer.

#### Knowledge as Protection from Sin

TROID.org states:

> "Knowledge is the shield and protection from sin and transgression for the Muslim. When the Muslim knows the difference between the truth and misguidance he can then distinguish between the people of Paradise and the people of the hellfire."

**Source:** TROID.org, "Why We Should Seek Knowledge and From Whom"
**URL:** https://www.troid.org/why-we-should-seek-knowledge-and-from-whom/

Allah says:

> "Verily, this Quran guides to that which is most right."

**Source:** Quran, al-Isra 17:9
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/15387/من-أسباب-الهداية

## Direct Answer to the Question

### First: On the ruling of missing 4 out of 5 prayers

Missing four out of five daily prayers deliberately out of laziness is **a grave major sin** by the consensus of all scholars. The question of whether it constitutes kufr depends on which scholarly view one follows:

- **According to Ibn 'Uthaymeen, Ibn Taymiyyah, and al-Albani:** If the person prays even one or two of the five daily prayers, he is **not a kaafir**. He remains a Muslim but is committing a major sin. He must repent and establish all five prayers.
- **According to Ibn Baz, the Standing Committee, and al-Fawzan:** Deliberately missing even a single prayer until its time ends (out of laziness, no excuse) constitutes **major kufr**. The person must repent and return to Islam.

> **The statement "he didn't really abandon it" needs correction.** While the person may not have *completely* abandoned salah (which is the distinction Ibn 'Uthaymeen and Ibn Taymiyyah draw), missing four out of five prayers is an extremely dangerous state. No scholar considers this a minor matter. The fact that he regrets it and intends to pray soon is a good sign — but that intention must be translated into **immediate action**: repentance and establishing all five prayers from now on.

### Second: On whether seeking knowledge gives him good deeds

**If the person is a Muslim** (which is the position of Ibn 'Uthaymeen, Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Albani, and the majority of fuqaha for one who prays some prayers):

- **Yes, seeking knowledge earns him good deeds.** Seeking knowledge is from the best of actions (Ibn Baz, Imam Ahmad).
- **Nothing cancels out all good deeds except kufr** (Ibn Taymiyyah). Since he is still a Muslim, his good deeds — including seeking knowledge — are accepted.
- **Good deeds erase bad deeds** (Hud 11:114). All acts of obedience — including seeking knowledge — are hasanaat that erase evil deeds (Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 1531).
- **Seeking knowledge can itself be the means by which Allah guides him back to the prayer** (Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 15387 and no. 5970).

**If the person is considered a kaafir** (the position of Ibn Baz for one who deliberately misses even one prayer):

- Good deeds are not accepted in the state of kufr. However, **once he repents and returns to Islam, all previous sins are forgiven** (al-Anfal 8:38), and his good deeds from that point — including seeking knowledge — are accepted and rewarded.

### The Path Forward

Under both scholarly positions, the path forward is the same:

1. **Repent sincerely** to Allah for the missed prayers
2. **Establish all five daily prayers** on time from now on
3. **Seek knowledge** — it is a good deed that earns reward AND a cause of guidance that can strengthen one's commitment to prayer
4. **Do abundant voluntary good deeds** — dhikr, charity, fasting, supererogatory prayers — as these erase sins (Hud 11:114; Ibn Baz, fatwa no. 1531)

Allah says:

> "And verily, I am indeed Forgiving to him who repents, believes (in My Oneness, and associates none in worship with Me) and does righteous good deeds, and then remains constant in doing them, (till his death)."

**Source:** Quran, Ta-Ha 20:82
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/197247
