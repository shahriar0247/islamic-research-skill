# Research: Missed Prayers and Good Deeds - Salafi Scholar Perspectives

**Question:** If a person missed some prayers (e.g., 4 prayers) out of laziness but had prayed before and did other good deeds, do those good deeds still get recorded/accepted by Allah, or does missing prayers stop the recording/acceptance of good deeds?

**Sources Consulted:** islamqa.info, salafipublications.com, abdurrahman.org, troid.org, learnaboutislam.co.uk, fatwaislam.com, islamweb.net, binbaz.org.sa, alalbani.info, abukhadeejah.com

## 1. THE HADITH ABOUT SALAH BEING THE FIRST DEED JUDGED ON THE DAY OF RESURRECTION

### Hadith Text (English):
"The first of his deeds for which a person will be brought to account on the Day of Resurrection will be his prayer. If it is good, then he will have succeeded and prospered, but if it is lacking, then he will have lost and failed. If anything is lacking from his obligatory prayer, the Lord, may He be glorified and exalted, will say: Look and see, does My slave have any voluntary prayers, which could make up for what is lacking from his obligatory prayer? Then all of his deeds will be dealt with in a similar manner."

### Arabic:
"أول ما يحاسب به العبد يوم القيامة صلاته، فإن صلحت فقد أفلح ونجح، وإن فسدت فقد خاب وخسر. فإن نقص من فريضته شيء، قال الله عز وجل: انظروا هل لعبدي من تطوع فتكملوا بها فريضته؟ ثم سائر أعماله على ذلك"

### References:
- **Narrator:** Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him)
- **Source:** At-Tirmidhi (413)
- **Grading by al-Albani:** Classed as saheeh in Saheeh at-Tirmidhi

### Additional Version (al-Tabarani):
"The first matter that the slave will be brought to account for on the Day of Judgment is the prayer. If it is sound, then the rest of his deeds will be sound. And if it is bad, then the rest of his deeds will be bad."

- **Source:** Recorded by al-Tabarani
- **Grading by al-Albani:** Sahih in Sahih al-Jami', vol.1, p. 503

**Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/286728

## 3. THE DIFFERENCE OF OPINION AMONG SALAFI SCHOLARS ABOUT THE ONE WHO ABANDONS PRAYER OUT OF LAZINESS

### View 1: Imam Ahmad, Ibn Taymiyyah, and the Majority of the Salaf - He Becomes a Kafir (Disbeliever)

**Imam Ahmad (d. 241 AH):**
"Imam Ahmad (may Allah have mercy on him) said that the one who does not pray because of laziness is a disbeliever. This is the more correct view and is that indicated by the evidence of the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger, and by the words of the Salaf and the proper understanding."

- **Source:** Ash-Sharh Al-Mumti `ala Zad Al-Mustanqi`, 2/26
- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/5208

**Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (d. 728 AH):**
According to IslamQA: "This was also the view of Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyah (may Allah have mercy on him), although he also ruled to be a kaafir the one who misses some prayers and is called by the ruler to pray but does not pray."

- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/52923

**Consequence if this view is taken:** If the person is ruled to be a disbeliever, then his deeds are void based on Quranic verses (see section 4).

### View 2: Abu Hanifa, Malik, al-Shafi'i, and Some Scholars - He is a Sinful Muslim (Fasiq) but Not a Kafir

**Islamweb summarizes:**
"Muslim scholars agreed that whoever abandons the prayer (the obligatory prayer) while denying its obligation, goes out of the fold of Islam. But they differed in opinion about the person who acknowledged the prayer as obligatory, but he abandons it because of laziness:

- Abu Haneefah says that the person doesn't become a non-Muslim (does not go out of the fold of Islam), but he should be imprisoned until he starts to pray.

- Maalik and Ash-Shaafi'i are of the view that he is not a non-Muslim, but he should be executed by Hadd (i.e. corporal punishment determined by the Islamic Law) (because of abandoning the prayer)."

- **Source URL:** https://islamweb.net/en/fatwa/84235/neglecting-the-prayers

**Consequence if this view is taken:** His good deeds may still be accepted but he is in grave danger of punishment.

### View 3: Ibn Baz, al-Albani, al-Uthaymin, al-Fawzan - What Do They Say is the Strongest View?

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (d. 1420 AH):**
According to IslamQA: "We have previously discussed this ruling in more than one answer on this website, quoting the evidence for it, and stating that we favour the view that he is a disbeliever if he completely abandons the salah (ie, never prays)."

- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/285977

**Shaykh al-Albani (d. 1420 AH):**
Shaykh al-Albani held the view that the one who abandons prayer out of laziness (without denying its obligation) is NOT a disbeliever, but rather a rebellious and disobedient Muslim:

"This (man) is one who is rebellious and disobedient (to Allaah), and not a disbeliever. The daleel is that 'Whoever says laa ilaaha illAllaah (sincerely) will enter Parad...'"

- **Source:** alalbani.info/alalbany_eng_043.php
- **Source URL:** https://alalbani.info/alalbany_eng_043.php

**Shaykh al-Uthaymin (d. 1421 AH):**
"What appears to me to be the case is that he does not become a disbeliever unless he does not pray altogether, i.e., he never prays; as for the one who prays sometimes, he is not a disbeliever."

- **Source:** al-Sharh al-Mumti' (2/26)
- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/52923

**Shaykh al-Fawzan (may Allah preserve him):**
Islamweb states: "Whoever gives it up and neglects it or does not pray because of laziness; never praying it, is also, according to the chosen correct opinion of scholars, considered as an unbeliever."

- **Source URL:** https://www.islamweb.net/amp/en/fatwa/81555

**Summary of Contemporary Salafi Scholars:**
- Ibn Baz: Favors the view that complete abandonment = kufr
- al-Albani: Views the one who abandons out of laziness as a sinful Muslim, not a kafir
- al-Uthaymin: Does not become kafir unless he abandons prayer altogether (never prays)
- al-Fawzan: According to Islamweb, the chosen correct opinion is that complete abandonment = kufr

## 5. THE DISTINCTION BETWEEN MISSING SOME PRAYERS VS ABANDONING PRAYER ENTIRELY

### Key Distinction: Complete Abandonment vs. Occasional Missing

**IslamQA states:**
"The one who neglects prayer is one who does not pray at all, and he is a kaafir regardless of whether that is due to laziness or denying that the prayer is obligatory, according to the more correct of the two scholarly opinions."

"If a person is not omitting all prayers, rather he prays sometimes and does not pray sometimes, then those who say that the one who does not pray is a kaafir differed in this case."

- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/83165

### Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin on the Distinction:

"What appears to me to be the case is that he does not become a disbeliever unless he does not pray altogether, i.e., he never prays; as for the one who prays sometimes, he is not a disbeliever."

"But if he prays one or two obligatory prayers, then he is not a kaafir and he cannot truly be described as having given up prayer. The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: 'Between a man and shirk and kufr stands his giving up prayer (al-salaah)' and he did not say 'salaah (a prayer)'."

- **Source:** al-Sharh al-Mumti' (2/26)
- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/52923

### Islamweb on the Distinction:

"But the person who sometimes prays and some other times doesn't do so is not an unbeliever. He is, however, in a great danger."

- **Source URL:** https://www.islamweb.net/amp/en/fatwa/81555

### Summary:
- **One who abandons prayer completely (tarakah al-salah):** Most severe - many scholars rule this is kufr
- **One who misses some prayers occasionally out of laziness:** Sinful but still Muslim according to all scholars (though some scholars say missing even one prayer until its time ends can be kufr)

## 6. REPENTANCE (TAWBAH) FOR MISSED PRAYERS

### Can Missed Prayers Be Made Up?

**IslamQA states:**
"If you missed prayers for a reason, there is no sin on you but you have to make it up. If you missed prayers with no excuse, you have committed a major sin and you have to repent to Allah. It is not valid for you to make it up according to the more correct of the two scholarly opinions."

- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/111783

### View of Ibn Baz:

**From binbaz.org.sa (Arabic fatwa):**
"التوبة تجب ما قبلها، وليس عليه أن يعيد ما ترك، بل عليه التوبة إلى الله، والصدق في ذلك، بالندم على ما مضى من عمله، والعزم ألا يعود، ثم الاستكثار من العمل الصالح..."

Translation: "Repentance wipes out what came before it, and he does not have to make up what he missed; rather he must repent to Allah, be sincere in that, by regretting what has passed of his deeds, resolving not to return to it, then doing many good deeds..."

- **Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/13597/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81-%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%B6-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%83-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%83%D9%87

**Another fatwa from Ibn Baz:**
"أما إن ترك الصلاة عمدًا بلا شبهة فإنه لا يقضي؛ لأن تركها عمدًا كفر أكبر، وإن لم يجحد وجوبها في أصح قولي العلماء... وعليه التوبة مما سلف، والاستقامة على فعلها مستقبلاً."

Translation: "As for if he leaves the prayer deliberately without any excuse, then he does not make it up; because leaving it deliberately is major kufr, according to the more correct of the two scholarly opinions... He must repent from what has passed, and remain steadfast in performing it in the future."

- **Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/3906/%D9%87%D9%84-%D9%8A%D8%AC%D8%A8-%D9%82%D8%B6%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%84%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AA%D8%A9

### View of al-Uthaymin:

According to IslamQA: "This is also the view of 'Umar ibn al-Khattab and his son 'Abd-Allah, and of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Salman, Ibn Mas'ud, al-Qasim ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, Badil al-'Aqili, Muhammad ibn Sirin, Mutarrif ibn 'Abd-Allah and 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz. It was also the view of Dawud al-Zahiri and Ibn Hazm, and was the view favoured by Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyah and al-Shawkani. Among contemporary scholars it was regarded as more correct by al-Albani, Ibn Baz, Ibn 'Uthaymin and others."

- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/111783

### View of al-Albani:

As mentioned above, al-Albani is among the contemporary scholars who regarded the view that missed prayers are NOT made up as more correct.

### Summary:
The well-known view of al-Uthaymin, Ibn Baz, and al-Albani is that missed prayers (deliberately, without excuse) are NOT made up; rather one repents and increases in good deeds and voluntary prayers.

## 7. WHETHER GOOD DEEDS OF A SINFUL MUSLIM (FASIQ) ARE STILL ACCEPTED

### Ibn Taymiyyah on Good Deeds of Believers vs. Disbelievers:

**Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) said:**
"Nothing cancels out good deeds except kufr, because the one who dies as a believer will inevitably enter Paradise, and he will be brought out of Hell if he does enter it, but if all his good deeds are cancelled out, he will never enter Paradise. Good deeds can only be cancelled out by that which is their opposite, and nothing can be the opposite of all good deeds except kufr."

- **Source:** Al-Saarim al-Maslool (p. 55)
- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/81874

### Ibn al-Qayyim on Good Deeds Erasing Sin:

**Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) said:**
"The traces of sin may be erased by means of sincere repentance, heartfelt affirmation of the Oneness of Allah (Tawheed), good deeds that erase sin, calamities that expiate sin, the intercession of those who intercede among those who affirm the Oneness of Allah, and finally, if he is punished for whatever sins remain, he will be brought out of Hell by virtue of his Tawheed."

"Good deeds that erase sin include wudoo', the five daily prayers, and Hajj and 'Umrah."

- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/169682/he-is-asking-about-the-most-effective-good-deeds-in-expiating-sins

### al-Uthaymin on Good Deeds of Sinful Muslims:

**NOT FOUND** specific quote from al-Uthaymin on whether good deeds of a fasiq are accepted in this research session.

### IslamQA on Good Deeds of Sinful Muslims:

"Allah will accept whatever righteous deeds from one who commits Zina, such as praying, fasting, giving charity etc., and He will also accept his sincere repentance."

- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/1397

### Summary:
According to Ibn Taymiyyah, only kufr cancels out all good deeds. Sins (even major sins) do not cancel out a believer's good deeds entirely, though they may reduce the reward. The sinful Muslim's (fasiq) good deeds are still accepted by Allah, though he may be punished for his sins.

## 8. THE HADITH ABOUT THE FIVE DAILY PRAYERS EXPIATING SINS BETWEEN THEM

### Hadith Text (English):
"The five daily prayers, from one Jumu'ah to the next and from one Ramadan to the next is expiation for whatever (sins) come in between them, so long as you avoid major sins."

### Arabic:
"الصلوات الخمس، والجمعة إلى الجمعة، ورمضان إلى رمضان، مكفرات ما بينهن إذا اجتنبت الكبائر"

### References:
- **Narrator:** Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him)
- **Source:** Sahih Muslim, 1/209
- **Grading:** Sahih (Muslim)

### Additional Version:
"The five prayers and Jumu'ah are an expiation from one week to the next, so long as you do not commit major sins."

- **Source:** Muslim, 233
- **Source URL:** https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/3365

### Implication:
If a person misses prayers, then the sins between them are NOT expiated by those prayers, meaning the person remains accountable for those sins.

## CONCLUSION: ANSWERING THE ORIGINAL QUESTION

**Question:** If a person missed some prayers (e.g., 4 prayers) out of laziness but had prayed before and did other good deeds, do those good deeds still get recorded/accepted by Allah?

**Answer based on Salafi scholar sources:**

1. **If the person is considered a Muslim (not a kafir):** According to the majority of contemporary Salafi scholars (al-Albani, al-Uthaymin), a person who misses some prayers occasionally out of laziness is still considered a Muslim (fasiq/sinner), not a disbeliever. In this case:
   - His good deeds ARE still recorded and accepted by Allah
   - Only kufr (disbelief) cancels out all good deeds (Ibn Taymiyyah)
   - He must repent sincerely for the missed prayers
   - According to the stronger view (Ibn Baz, al-Uthaymin, al-Albani), he does NOT make up the missed prayers; rather he repents and increases in voluntary prayers and good deeds

2. **If the person is considered to have abandoned prayer completely:** According to some scholars (Imam Ahmad, Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Baz's favored view for complete abandonment), complete abandonment of prayer is kufr. In this case:
   - His deeds would be void based on Quranic verses (5:5, 24:39, 39:65, 47:1-2)
   - He would need to repent and return to Islam

3. **For the specific case mentioned (missing 4 prayers out of laziness, but having prayed before and done other good deeds):**
   - This appears to be a case of missing some prayers occasionally, not complete abandonment
   - According to al-Uthaymin: "if he prays one or two obligatory prayers, then he is not a kaafir and he cannot truly be described as having given up prayer"
   - Therefore, the good deeds done before and after would still be recorded and accepted
   - The person must repent for the missed prayers and strive to pray regularly going forward

**Final Verdict:** The good deeds of a person who misses some prayers out of laziness (but does not completely abandon prayer) are still recorded and accepted by Allah, according to the view of the majority of contemporary Salafi scholars. However, missing prayers is a grave sin that requires sincere repentance.
