# Research: Taking Notes When Seeking Knowledge — Salafi Sources

## Research Question
Is taking notes obligatory in seeking knowledge? How much should a student write?

---

## 1. Ruling: Obligatory, recommended, or permissible?

### Summary
- Taking notes is **not obligatory**.
- It is **strongly recommended (mustahabb/mandoob)**.
- It is **permissible (mubah)** at minimum, with consensus (ijmāʿ) that writing knowledge is allowed.
- For those with weak memory, it becomes more necessary.

### Permanent Committee
> "We advise you to fear Allah, to learn from the Islamic sciences what you establish your religious and worldly affairs with, to ask the people of knowledge about what is difficult for you, and to record from the sciences what you need to record; and to be keen on memorizing the Quran and what is easy from the Sunnah."
>
> Source: al-Fatawa.com, fatwa no. 111112  
> URL: https://al-fatawa.com/fatwa/111112

### IslamWeb
On how the Salaf preserved knowledge:

> "As for the meaning that the Salaf preserved knowledge by hearing alone, without needing to write it, this is not absolute. Some of them were given the gift of complete memorization, so they did not need to write. These were a small number. The majority used to rely on writing to preserve knowledge."
>
> Source: IslamWeb fatwa no. 136518  
> URL: https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/136518

## 2. Evidence from the Quran

### Surah al-ʿAlaq 96:1–4
> "Read! In the Name of your Lord, Who has created (all that exists). Has created man from a clot. Read! And your Lord is the Most Generous, Who has taught by the pen."

### Surah al-Baqarah 2:282
> "O you who have believed, when you contract a debt for a specified term, write it down."

Ibn al-ʿUthaymeen explained that the verse in al-ʿAlaq shows writing is a remedy for forgetting:

> "Allah said 'Read!' and then said 'Who has taught by the pen.' Meaning, write what you memorized. Allah explained to us how this sickness can be cured if knowledge is not in our hearts. The sickness is being forgetful and it is cured by writing."
>
> Source: Trying to Follow the Salaf  
> URL: https://tryingtofollowthesalaf.com/2014/01/11/the-remedycure-for-forgetting-knowledge-shaykh-ibn-al-uthaymeen/

## 3. Evidence from the Sunnah

### Hadith: "Bind knowledge with the book" (qayyidū al-ʿilma bil-kitāb)

IslamWeb fatwa no. 119595 explains:

> "The phrase 'Qayyidū al-ʿilma bil-kitāb' (Bind knowledge with the book) is authentic (sahih). It was narrated through other paths from Anas that he said: 'The Prophet said: Bind knowledge with the book.' It was narrated by al-Khatib, Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, al-Ramhurmuzi, Ibn 'Asakir, Abu Nu'aym, al-Qada'i, and others. And al-Albani authenticated it in Sahih al-Jami'."
>
> URL: https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/119595

Note: The wording "bil-kitābah" (with writing) is weak as a marfūʿ hadith; "bil-kitāb" (with the book) is authentic as a saying of the Prophet.

### Hadith of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr

> "I used to write everything I heard from the Messenger of Allah, wanting to memorize it. Quraysh forbade me... So I stopped writing, and I mentioned that to the Messenger of Allah. He pointed to his mouth with his finger and said: 'Write, for by the One in whose hand my soul rests, nothing but truth comes from it.'"
>
> Source: Sunan Abu Dawud (3646), authenticated by al-Albani. Also in IslamWeb 136518.  
> URL: https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/151146

### The prohibition and its abrogation

IslamQA explains the hadith "Do not write anything from me...":

> "Al-Qaadi said: there were many disputes among the Sahaabah and Tabi'een concerning the writing down of knowledge. Many of them regarded this as being makrooh, but most of them regarded it as permissible. This dispute is no longer an issue."
>
> "It was said that the hadeeth forbidding writing down ahaadeeth was abrogated by these ahaadeeth. The prohibition was in effect when there was the fear that the words of the Prophet might be mixed with the Quran. When that danger was no longer present, permission was given to write down ahaadeeth."
>
> URL: https://islamqa.info/amp/ar/answers/22394

### Hadith of Abu Shah

> The Prophet said: "Write it down for Abu Shah."
>
> Source: Sahih al-Bukhari. Cited in IslamQA.  
> URL: https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/22394

## 5. Scholarly statements

### Ibn al-ʿUthaymeen
> "It is befitting for the student of knowledge to record what he has learned, especially the principles, guidelines, and rare issues, lest they escape him... So record the issues, especially the rare ones, or the principles or guidelines, so that you have a reserve."
>
> Source: al-Fatawa.com, fatwa no. 129509  
> URL: https://al-fatawa.com/fatwa/129509

### Ibn Bāz
> "The student of knowledge attends, listens attentively, and brings the book from which the lesson is being delivered so that he may derive the fullest benefit and record important points that might otherwise be forgotten. Writing down beneficial knowledge is of tremendous value, for a person may hear something and later forget it, whereas writing preserves knowledge and safeguards its benefit."
>
> Source: DuSunnah.com  
> URL: https://dusunnah.com/article/the-blessings-of-the-circles-of-knowledge-shaykh-ibn-baz/

### Shaykh Saalih Aali Shaikh
> "A student of knowledge should maintain (a list of) selected points of benefit for each of his books. This means that if someone reads a book and he doesn't feel that he will remember or be able to recall what he read later — even if he is young — he should select points of benefit from this book and write them down in a special notebook."
>
> "Understanding is contingent — it comes and goes, whereas writing is recorded."
>
> Source: AbdurRahman.org  
> URL: https://abdurrahman.org/2014/03/01/etiquette-four-recording-the-points-of-benefit-found-in-books-shaikh-saalih-bin-abdil-azeez-aali-shaikh/

### Ibn al-Qayyim
> "There are six stages to knowledge: asking questions, remaining quiet and listening, understanding, memorizing, teaching, and acting upon the knowledge."
>
> Source: AbdurRahman.org  
> URL: https://abdurrahman.org/2014/01/15/faawaid/

### IslamWeb on note-taking and recording
> "We advise you to focus in listening to the Shaykh during his lecture in a study circle so that you would fully understand what he says, and find a way to record the lesson so that you can write down the benefits afterwards... The means of recording have become widely available nowadays, praise be to Allah."
>
> URL: https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/249955

## 6. How much to write?

### Select key points of benefit
- Record rare issues, principles, guidelines, evidences.
- Do not write everything the teacher says.
- If you cannot remember something later, write it down.

### Balance listening and writing
- Listening and understanding come before writing.
- Writing supports memorization, not replaces it.
- If writing prevents understanding, reduce it.

### Organize notes
- Use a dedicated notebook for each science.
- Create an index of benefits at the beginning of a book.
- Use margins for brief notes.
- Write clearly.
- Review regularly.

### Record immediately
- When a benefit arises, record it at once.
- Do not delay, as it may be forgotten.
