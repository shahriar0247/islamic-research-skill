# Missed Prayers Through Sleep and Laziness: Qada’, Kufr, and Re-entering Islam

**Question:** A person did not sleep for 24 hours, then slept knowing he would likely miss `Asr, and he missed it. Then for Maghrib, he again knew/expected he would miss it, and he missed it. Then for `Isha, time passed while he was awake and able to pray, but he did not pray because of laziness/demotivation. Can he make up these prayers? Does he have to? Or should he pray two rak`at to become Muslim again?

**Sources:** Salafi scholars — Ibn Baz, Ibn `Uthaymin, al-Albani, Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Fawzan, and the IslamQA fatwa site.

**Method:** Only verbatim quotes from named Salafi scholars are used. Anything that could not be found from a Salafi source is marked as such.

---

## 1. Short answer

1. **`Asr and Maghrib`:** If the sleep was genuinely overpowering and he had taken reasonable means (alarm, sleeping early, asking someone to wake him), he **must make them up** as soon as he wakes, and there is no sin.

   If he slept **negligently or deliberately** while knowing he would likely miss them — e.g., no alarm, stayed up until exhaustion — then the stronger Salafi view is that this is **not excused sleep**, but rather deliberate/negligent neglect. In that case, **qada’ is not legislated**; he must sincerely repent.

2. **`Isha (missed while awake out of laziness):`** This is deliberate neglect. According to the stronger Salafi view, **qada’ is not legislated** for it; he must sincerely repent, regret it, resolve never to repeat it, and increase in voluntary prayers and good deeds.

3. **Does he need to re-enter Islam / pray two rak`at?**

   - There is **no such thing in the Salafi sources** as “praying two rak`at to become Muslim again.” The way back to Islam, if a person has actually apostatized, is by **sincere repentance and the Shahaadatayn** (the two testimonies of faith), not by a two-rak`at prayer.

   - The two-rak`at prayer of **Salat al-Tawba** is a Sunnah for a Muslim who has sinned; it helps his repentance, but it does **not** convert someone back to Islam.

4. **Did he leave Islam?**

   - **View A (Ibn Baz, al-Fawzan, Imam Ahmad):** Deliberately missing even one prayer until its time exits, out of laziness/neglect while believing it is obligatory, is **major kufr** that removes one from Islam.

   - **View B (Ibn `Uthaymin, al-Albani, Ibn Taymiyyah):** A person does **not** become a kaafir unless he abandons **all** prayer, deciding not to pray any of the five. If he still prays some, he is a grave sinner but remains a Muslim.

   - Because the scholars differ, the safest practical path is: **repent sincerely, guard the five daily prayers, do many good deeds, and, out of caution, repeat the Shahaadatayn and perform ghusl if one fears actual apostasy.** But one should not be paralyzed by obsessive doubt.

## 2. The distinction between excused sleep and negligent sleep

### Excused sleep

The Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said:

> “There is no negligence in sleep; negligence is only while awake — that a person delays a prayer until the time of the next prayer. Whoever forgets a prayer or sleeps and misses it, let him pray it when he remembers.”

**Source:** Sahih Muslim, no. 681; also narrated by Abu Qatadah in Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, and al-Nasa’i. Classed as sahih by al-Albani.

And:

> “Whoever forgets a prayer, let him offer it as soon as he remembers, for there is no expiation for it other than that.”

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 597; Sahih Muslim, no. 684. Muslim’s narration adds: "or sleeps and misses it."

This applies when sleep overpowers a person **without negligence** — he intended to pray, took reasonable means (alarm, early sleep, asking someone to wake him), and still missed the prayer.

### Negligent / deliberate sleep

Shaykh Ibn Baz (rahimahullah) said:

> “This is detailed: if sleep overcomes a person and he has no choice, then there is no negligence in sleep… But if you are able to get up for Fajr by setting an alarm, or by telling someone in your family to wake you up, then you are negligent; you have sinned by this and you are in danger. Indeed, if you were negligent in staying up late, or in not having an alarm, or in not telling anyone to wake you up, then you are like the one who did it deliberately; upon you is a great sin and you may become a kaafir by that, because whoever deliberately leaves the prayer until its time exits is a kaafir according to a group of the people of knowledge…
>
> But if sleep overcomes you as mentioned, then there is nothing upon you; however, you must take precautions: set an alarm for the time, ask whoever is available in your family to wake you up, and sleep early so that you can get up. All of this is obligatory upon you and upon those like you.”

**Source:** BinBaz fatwa no. 18328, “Hukm tadhyee` salaat al-subh bi-sabab an-nawm”  
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/18328/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%AA%D8%B6%D9%8A%D9%8A%D8%B9-%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%A8%D8%AD-%D8%A8%D8%B3%D8%A8%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D9%88%D9%85

Implication: If the person in the scenario **knew** that sleeping would cause him to miss `Asr and Maghrib, and he did not take reasonable precautions, then his sleep is **negligent**, not excused.

Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan said:

> “Sleep is not a permanent excuse; only incidental sleep while one is resolved to pray in its time and with the congregation but is overcome by sleep — that person is excused, but when he wakes he must hasten to pray. Likewise if he forgets… What the questioner mentioned is wrong: that he leaves several prayers on the excuse that he sleeps. He should not make sleep a barrier to prayer; rather he should account for the prayer and resolve to wake up. If he is negligent, sleeps and lets many prayers pass, then makes them up later, he is one of those who waste the prayer… The one who habitually sleeps through several prayers is a waster and negligent. The excuse is only for incidental sleep.”

**Source:** Al-Tahaun fi Ada’ al-Salat, node 15372  
**URL (official — did not load in verification):** https://www.alfawzan.af.org.sa/ar/node/15372  
**URL (verified mirror):** https://al-fatawa.com/fatwa/99134/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D9%88%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%86

## 3. Are deliberately missed prayers made up?

### The stronger Salafi view: no qada’ for deliberate neglect

Shaykh Ibn Baz said:

> “Whoever has prayers to make up makes them up as if he had performed them on time… if he left them because of forgetfulness, or because of sleep, or because of an illness… But if he left them because of laziness and negligence, then there is a difference among the people of knowledge concerning his kufr, and the most correct view is that he becomes a kaafir by that, and he does not have to make them up; rather he must repent from that. The summary is: if he left it deliberately while denying its obligation, he is a kaafir by consensus. If he left it out of negligence and laziness, this resembles the hypocrites, and that is major kufr in the more correct of the two scholarly opinions. So upon him is to repent to Allah… and there is no qada upon him.”

**Source:** BinBaz fatwa no. 4052, “Kayfa yuqadi al-muslimu maa fatahu min as-salaah”  
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/4052/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81-%D9%8A%D9%82%D8%B6%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D9%84%D9%85-%D9%85%D8%A7-%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9

Shaykh al-Albani said:

> “As for the one who deliberately takes a prayer out of its time without intending to combine it with another, qada’ is not legislated for him and he is not excused at all, because if the one who forgot and the one who slept — both are excused in the Shari`ah — have only to perform it immediately upon remembering, and that is its time, then where is the time in relation to the deliberate one? Because of the Prophet’s words: ‘Whoever misses the `Asr prayer, it is as if he lost his family and property.’ What is missed cannot be retrieved at all; if it could be retrieved, it would not be called ‘missed’.”

**Source:** Jami` Turath al-`Allamah al-Albani fi al-Fiqh, vol. 2, p. 686

IslamQA summarizes:

> “If a person misses the prayer for a reason, such as falling asleep or forgetting it, there is no sin on him in this case, but he has to make it up when he wakes up or remembers it… He missed the prayer with no excuse; rather he ignored it until the time for it ended, out of laziness and carelessness. This person is sinning according to the consensus of the Muslims, and has committed a major sin. It is not valid for him to make it up according to the more correct of the two scholarly opinions, rather he has to repent and regret it, and resolve not to do that again, and he should do a lot of good deeds and offer a lot of voluntary prayers.”

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 111783  
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/111783

### Majority view

The majority of jurists hold that qada’ is obligatory and valid even for deliberately missed prayers, although the person is a sinner. The Salafi scholars cited above regard the view that qada’ is **not legislated** for the deliberate misser as the stronger view.

## 4. Did he leave Islam?

### View A: Deliberately missing even one prayer until its time exits is major kufr

Shaykh Ibn Baz said:

> “These scholars differed on two opinions. One of them is that whoever abandons the prayer, even if he does not deny its obligation, becomes a kaafir – even if it is one prayer; if he abandons it until its time exits he becomes a kaafir. So whoever deliberately leaves Fajr until the sun rises, or deliberately leaves `Asr until the sun sets, or deliberately leaves Maghrib until the twilight disappears, becomes a kaafir by that. This is because of the hadith: ‘The covenant that is between us and them is the prayer; whoever abandons it, he has disbelieved’ … and His saying: ‘Between a man and kufr and shirk is the abandonment of the prayer’ … and His saying: ‘Whoever abandons the `Asr prayer, his deeds are void’ … and His saying: ‘The head of the affair is Islam and its supporting pillar is the prayer.’ The prayer is the supporting pillar of Islam; so whoever abandons it is like one who abandons the two testimonies. This is the chosen saying and it is the most correct.
>
> Some of the people of knowledge… held that this is a disbelief lesser than disbelief; he is a kaafir, but not with major kufr that exits from the religion… and the most correct view is the first: his disbelief is major disbelief, because of the aforementioned hadiths.”

**Source:** BinBaz fatwa no. 11911, “Maa al-rajih fi hukm taarik as-salaah”  
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/11911/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%AD-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9

Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan said:

> “The scholars have said that whoever abandons the Prayer out of laziness, he becomes a disbeliever, in the correct saying – even if he acknowledges its obligation because there is no benefit in acknowledging its obligation if he does not implement it and he does not act upon it… the verifiers from the people of knowledge judge that the person who abandons the Prayer deliberately, even if he acknowledges its obligation, is a disbeliever. As for if he denies its obligation, then he is a disbeliever by consensus of the Muslims.”

**Source:** Sharh Usool-ith-Thalaathah, Lesson 50 (Part B)  
**URL:** https://abdurrahman.org/2018/06/08/the-head-of-the-affair-is-al-islaam-its-supporting-pillar-is-the-prayer-its-highest-pinnacle-is-jihaad/

### View B: One remains Muslim unless he completely abandons all prayer

Shaykh Ibn `Uthaymin said:

> “What appears to be the case from the evidence is that he does not become a kaafir unless he gives up prayer all the time, in the sense that he has decided not to pray, so he does not pray Zuhr, `Asr, Maghrib, `Isha’ or Fajr. This is the one who is a kaafir. But if he prays one or two obligatory prayers, then he is not a kaafir and he cannot truly be described as having given up prayer. The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: ‘Between a man and shirk and kufr stands his giving up prayer (al-salaah)’ and he did not say ‘salaah (a prayer)’.”

**Source:** al-Sharh al-Mumti’ (2/26)  
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/52923

Shaykh al-Albani said:

> “Whoever leaves them out of laziness, it is not allowed to declare him a disbeliever. Whoever leaves them out of denial has disbelieved… There is no difference with regard to this at all between the obligations and prohibitions.”

**Source:** Silsilat al-Huda wa an-Nur, tape no. 8, Q5  
**URL:** https://abdurrahman.org/2014/10/10/the-ruling-on-the-one-who-abandons-prayer-shaykh-al-albaani/

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (as summarized by IslamQA) said:

> “The one who does not pray does not become a kaafir unless he gives up prayer altogether… He also stated that the one who prays sometimes and does not pray sometimes, if he resolves in his heart to give up prayer altogether, then he is inwardly a kaafir, i.e., it is between him and Allah.”

**Source:** Majmoo’ al-Fataawa, 22/49, 7/715; Sharh al-‘Umdah, 2/94  
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/52923

### Hadith evidence

- “Between a man and shirk and kufr stands his giving up prayer.”  
  **Source:** Sahih Muslim, no. 82 (narrated by Jabir ibn `Abdullah)

- “The covenant that is between us and them is the prayer; whoever abandons it, he has disbelieved.”  
  **Source:** Sunan at-Tirmidhi, no. 2621 (narrated by Buraydah ibn al-Husayb). Classed as sahih by al-Albani in Sahih at-Tirmidhi.

- “Whoever abandons the `Asr prayer, his deeds of worship become void.”  
  **Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 553 (narrated by Ibn `Umar)

- “Whoever misses the `Asr prayer (intentionally), it is as if he lost his family and property.”  
  **Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 552 (narrated by Ibn `Umar)

## 5. The “two rak`at to become Muslim again” idea

### Where did it come from?

The idea of “praying two rak`at to be Muslim again” is **not found in the Salafi sources**. It appears to be a confusion between:

1. **Salat al-Tawba** — the two-rak`at Sunnah prayer of repentance that a Muslim may pray after committing a sin.
2. **Re-entering Islam** — which, if one has actually apostatized, is by the Shahaadatayn and sincere repentance, not by two rak`at.

### Salat al-Tawba

Shaykh Ibn Baz said:

> “As for the prayer of repentance, it is established from the Prophet (peace be upon him) in the hadith of Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) that he said: ‘There is no servant who commits a sin, then purifies himself with a good wudu, then prays two rak`at, then repents to Allah from his sin, except that Allah accepts his repentance.’ Narrated by Imam Ahmad.”

**Source:** BinBaz fatwa no. 4450, “Salaat at-tawbah thabitah fi as-sunnah”  
**URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/4450/%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%AB%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%AA%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9

This prayer is **for a Muslim sinner**, not a ritual to re-enter Islam.

### Re-entering Islam

IslamQA states:

> “The basic principle is that the kaafir – and also the apostate from Islam – is required to utter the Shahaadatayn in order to enter Islam.”

**Source:** IslamQA fatwa no. 32863  
**URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/32863

Shaykh Ibn Baz said about the returning apostate:

> "التوبة، باب التوبة مفتوح، إذا وقع في ناقض؛ فعليه أن يرجع إلى الإسلام بالتوبة، يندم على الماضي، ويعزم ألا يعود، ويترك هذا الناقض، فإذا كان كفره بدعاء الأموات، والاستغاثة بالأموات والأصنام؛ ترك ذلك، وتاب إلى الله من ذلك، وبهذا يرجع إلى الإسلام، إلا إذا كان كفره بأنه جحد وجوب الصلاة؛ يقر، يقول: لا، أنا غلطان، الصلاة فرض على المكلفين، وأتوب إلى الله من ذلك، ويندم، ويقلع، ويعزم ألا يعود، يتوب الله عليه، أو كان يترك الصلاة لا يصلي، التوبة أن يفعل الصلاة، ويندم على الماضي، ويستغفر مما مضى، ويعزم ألا يعود فيه وهذه التوبة."
>
> *"Repentance — the door of repentance is open. If he fell into a nullifier, he must return to Islam through repentance: he regrets the past, resolves not to return to it, and abandons that nullifier. If his kufr was due to calling upon the dead and seeking help from the dead and idols, he abandons that and repents to Allah — and by this he returns to Islam. But if his kufr was because he denied the obligation of prayer, he acknowledges it, saying: 'No, I was wrong, prayer is obligatory upon those accountable,' and he repents to Allah, regrets, desists, and resolves not to return — Allah accepts his repentance. Or if he used to abandon prayer and not pray, the repentance is that he performs the prayer, regrets the past, seeks forgiveness for what passed, and resolves not to return to it — and this is the repentance."*

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, "How to Return to Islam for One Who Has Fallen into Its Nullifiers" (كيفية الرجوع إلى الإسلام لمن وقع في مبطلاته)
**URL:** http://www.binbaz.org.sa/mat/10285

This shows the scholars differ on whether a returning apostate **must** utter the Shahaadatayn, but the way back is **repentance and the two testimonies of faith**; it is not merely a two-rak`at prayer.

## 6. What should the person do now?

### If the sleep was genuinely accidental (he took reasonable means)

1. **Make up `Asr and Maghrib** as soon as he woke up, in order.
2. **Repent for `Isha**, which was missed while awake out of laziness; the stronger view is that it is not made up.
3. Guard all future prayers on time.
4. Do many voluntary prayers and good deeds.

### If the sleep was negligent (he knew/expected to miss the prayers and took no precautions)

1. **Do not treat `Asr, Maghrib and `Isha as prayers to be made up** according to the stronger Salafi view. A prayer outside its time without a shar`i excuse is not the same as the missed obligatory prayer.
2. **Repent sincerely** to Allah: regret what passed, abandon the sin, resolve never to repeat it.
3. **Guard every prayer on time from now on.**
4. **Increase in voluntary prayers, fasting, charity, dhikr, and other good deeds.** Ibn Taymiyyah and al-Albani explicitly recommended this for the one who deliberately missed prayers.
5. **Out of caution**, because of the difference of opinion on whether deliberate neglect of prayer is kufr, one may repeat the Shahaadatayn and perform ghusl. But he should not be paralyzed by waswasah. If he still believes in Islam and in the obligation of prayer, the majority of contemporary Salafi scholars (al-Albani, Ibn `Uthaymin) do not declare him a kaafir.

---

## 7. Important notes

- **Takfir is a serious matter.** Declaring a specific individual a kaafir requires certainty about his intention and rejection. The fact that a scholar may rule that “whoever deliberately misses a prayer until its time exits is a kaafir” does not mean every specific person who falls into that is automatically judged as such in this life.

- **Regret and resolve are not enough by themselves** — the person must immediately begin praying all five daily prayers on time.

- **No fabricated evidence:** The notion “pray two rak`at to be Muslim again” was **not found from any Salafi source**. It is not an Islamic teaching.
