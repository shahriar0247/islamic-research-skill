# Is Allah Above His Throne? And Is "Allah Exists Without Place" an Opinion or Deviancy?

## A Research Article Based on the Statements of the Salafi Scholars

---

## Introduction

Two questions are frequently asked in the science of Aqeedah (Islamic creed):

1. **Is Allah above His Throne?** (العلو والاستواء — *al-'uluww* and *al-istiwaa'*)
2. **Is the statement "Allah exists without any space/place" (الله موجود بلا مكان) an acceptable difference of opinion, or is it a deviancy?**

This article answers both questions using **only verbatim quotes** from the Salafi scholars — Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn Kathir, al-Dhahabi, Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Wahhab (via al-Sa'di's commentary), Ibn Baz, al-Albani, al-Uthaymin, al-Fawzan, and as-Sa'di (may Allah have mercy on them). Every quote below is sourced to a named scholar, book, and URL. Where a quote could not be sourced to a Salafi scholar, it is explicitly marked `NOT FOUND FROM SALAFI SOURCE`.

---

## Part 1: Is Allah Above His Throne?

### The Answer in Brief

**Yes.** The Salafi scholars are unanimously agreed that Allah is above His Throne (*fawqa al-'arsh*), above the seven heavens, separate and distinct from His creation, with His Essence (*bi-dhatihi*), while His knowledge encompasses everything everywhere. This is established by:

- **Seven Quranic verses** explicitly stating Allah's *istiwaa'* (rising over) the Throne
- **Authentic hadiths**, including the hadith of the slave girl (graded **Sahih by al-Albani**)
- **Consensus (ijma')** of the Companions, the Tabi'un, and the Imams of the Salaf
- **The innate disposition (fitrah)** and sound reason

---

### 1.1 The Quranic Evidence — Seven Verses of Istiwaa'

Allah mentions His *istiwaa'* (rising over) the Throne in seven places in the Quran:

| # | Surah | Ayah | Verse (translation) |
|---|-------|------|---------------------|
| 1 | Al-A'raf (7) | 54 | "Indeed, your Lord is Allah, Who created the heavens and the earth in Six Days, and then He rose over (Istawaa) the Throne (really in a manner that suits His Majesty)." |
| 2 | Yunus (10) | 3 | "Indeed, your Lord is Allah, Who created the heavens and the earth in Six Days and then rose over (Istawaa) the Throne…" |
| 3 | Ar-Ra'd (13) | 2 | "Allah is He Who raised the heavens without any pillars that you can see. Then, He rose above (Istawa) the Throne." |
| 4 | Ta-Ha (20) | 5 | "The Most Gracious (Allah) rose over (Istawaa) the (Mighty) Throne (in a manner that suits His Majesty)." |
| 5 | Al-Furqan (25) | 59 | "He Who created the heavens and the earth and all that is between them, in six Days, and then rose over (Istawaa) the Throne…" |
| 6 | As-Sajdah (32) | 4 | "Allah it is He Who has created the heavens and the earth, and all that is between them in six Days. Then He Istawa over the Throne." |
| 7 | Al-Hadid (57) | 4 | "He it is Who created the heavens and the earth in six Days and then rose (Istawa) over the Throne." |

(Quran, Surah Al-A'raf 7:54; Surah Yunus 10:3; Surah Ar-Ra'd 13:2; Surah Ta-Ha 20:5; Surah Al-Furqan 25:59; Surah As-Sajdah 32:4; Surah Al-Hadid 57:4)

**Additional verses affirming Allah's aboveness (*al-'uluww*):**

- (Quran, Surah Al-Mulk 67:16) — "Do you feel secure that He, Who is over the heaven (Allah), will not cause the earth to sink with you?"
- (Quran, Surah An-Nahl 16:50) — "They fear their Lord above them."
- (Quran, Surah Fatir 35:10) — "To Him ascend (all) the goodly words, and the righteous deeds exalt it."
- (Quran, Surah An-Nisa 4:158) — "But Allah raised him ['Eesa (Jesus)] up (with his body and soul) unto Himself."
- (Quran, Surah Aal 'Imran 3:55) — "Verily, I am going to take you and raise you to Myself."

### 1.2 The Hadith Evidence

#### The Hadith of the Slave Girl (Hadith al-Jariyah)

> Mu'awiyah ibn al-Hakam al-Sulami said: "I said: 'O Messenger of Allah, I have a slave girl whom I slapped.' This grieved the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. I said to him: 'Should I not emancipate her?' He said: 'Bring her to me.' Then I brought her. He asked: **'Where is Allah?'** She replied: **'In the heaven.'** He said: 'Who am I?' She replied: 'You are the Messenger of Allah.' He said: **'Emancipate her, she is a believer.'**"

- **Source**: Sahih Muslim, Hadith 537; Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 328
- **Grading**: **Sahih (authentic) — graded Sahih by al-Albani** (Sunan Abi Dawud 328)

This hadith is decisive evidence that the Prophet ﷺ affirmed Allah is above the heaven, and that this belief is a condition of faith (iman). The Prophet ﷺ declared her a believer *because* she knew Allah is in the heaven.

#### The Hadith of Allah's Descent

> Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Our Lord descends every night to the lowest heaven when the last third of the night remains, and He says: 'Who will call Me that I might answer him, who will ask of Me that I might give him, who will ask My forgiveness, that I might forgive him?'"

- **Source**: Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 6940; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1262

#### The Statement of Sulayman al-Taymi

> Sulayman al-Taymi said: "If I were asked, 'Where is Allah?' I would say, 'In (fi) the heaven.' And if it were said, 'Where was the Throne before the Heaven?' I would say, 'Over the water.' And if it were said, 'Where was the Throne before the water?' I would say, 'I do not know.'"

- **Reported by**: Imam al-Bukhari in *Khalq Af'al al-Ibad*

### 1.3 The Statement of Imam Malik (d. 179H)

When asked about the meaning of {Al-Rahman 'ala al-'Arsh istawa} (Quran, Surah Ta-Ha 20:5), Imam Malik said:

> **"Al-Istiwa' is known, the how (kayf) is unknown, belief in it is obligatory, and asking about it is an innovation."**

- **Attributed to**: Imam Malik ibn Anas
- **Source**: Quoted by Ibn Baz in *Bayan Madhhab Ahl al-Sunnah fi al-Istiwa'* (https://binbaz.org.sa/articles/43/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%85%D8%B0%D9%87%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%87%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%A1); also quoted by al-Dhahabi in *al-'Uluww* (spubs.com)

This statement became the foundational principle of the Salaf regarding Allah's attributes: **affirm the meaning, deny the "how" (kayfiyyah), believe it is obligatory, and reject asking about the modality.**

### 1.5 The Meaning of Istiwaa' — The Salafi Position Summarized

The Salafi position, as articulated by all the above scholars, is:

1. **Istiwaa' means "to rise above / ascend over" (ista'laa)** — this is the apparent (dhaahir) meaning in the Arabic language.
2. **It is a real attribute (haqiqah)**, not metaphorical (majaaz) — as al-Qurtubi (quoted by al-Dhahabi) said: "Not a single one of the Salaf denied that his ascending (istawaa) the Throne was real and true (haqeeqah)."
3. **It is affirmed without takyif (asking "how")** — the kayfiyyah (modality) is unknown.
4. **It is affirmed without tamthil (likening)** — there is nothing like unto Allah (Quran, Surah Ash-Shura 42:11).
5. **It is an attribute of action (sifat fi'liyyah)** — connected to Allah's will and choice.

### 1.6 Refutation of the "Istawla" (Conquering) Interpretation

The Jahmiyyah, Mu'tazilah, and later Ash'ari theologians reinterpreted *istiwaa'* (rose over) as *istawla* (conquered/seized control of). The Salafi scholars unanimously rejected this as a baseless innovation (*bid'ah*).

#### Ibn Taymiyyah — On the Linguistic Impossibility of Istawla:

> "If this was known in the language: that 'Istawa' on something means that He directed towards its action, and **this is not known at all in the language, neither in reality nor metaphorically, neither in poetry nor in prose.**"

- **Book**: Majmu' al-Fatawa, 5/521
- **Source**: https://kutub.io/en/book/7289/2381

#### Ibn Taymiyyah — On the First Innovator of the Istawla Interpretation:

> "The first one I know of who said such a saying in Islam, i.e., that Allah the Almighty is not on the Throne in a real sense and that Istawa (rising over) means Istawla (conquer) and the like is **al-Ja'd ibn Dirham. Then al-Jahm ibn Safwan followed his footsteps and spread the idea.** So the sect of al-Jahmiyyah was named after him."

- **Source**: https://www.knowingallah.com/en/articles/nations-that-went-astray-regarding-the-names-and-attributes-of-allaah-the-almighty/

#### Ibn 'Abd al-Barr (d. 423H) — Refutation of Istawla:

> "And as for their taking istawaa metaphorically, and in ta'wil to mean istawlaa (conquering) then this is not a meaning of it because it is not clear (ghayru dhaahir) in the language. **And the meaning of istawlaa in the language is conquering/overcoming, and Allah the Exalted does not (need to) overcome or overwhelm anyone. And He is the One, the Eternal.** And from the right of the Words (of Allah) is that they be taken upon their literal meanings ('alaa haqeeqatihi), until the ummah is agreed that what is meant is the metaphorical meaning."

- **Book**: At-Tamheed (commentary on Muwatta')
- **Source**: https://spubs.com/sps/sp.cfm?articleID=AQD050002&articlePages=1&subsecID=AQD05

#### Ibn al-A'rabi (the linguist) — Refutation of Istawla:

> A man came to Ibn al-A'rabi and said: "O Abu Abdullah, what is the meaning of Allah's saying: {Ar-Rahmanu upon the Throne Istawa}?" He said: "He is on His Throne like He informed — 'Azza wa Jal." ## Part 2: Is "Allah Exists Without Place/Space" an Opinion or a Deviancy?

### The Answer in Brief

This question requires **important nuance**, which the Salafi scholars — especially Ibn Taymiyyah and al-Albani — carefully articulate:

1. **The phrase itself** ("Allah exists without place" / *bi-la makan*) is **vague, innovated terminology** not found in the Quran, Sunnah, or the speech of the Salaf.
2. **If the speaker means** that Allah is not contained/surrounded by His creation, and that He existed before any place existed — **this is true and correct.**
3. **If the speaker means** that Allah is NOT above the Throne, NOT in any direction, neither inside the universe nor outside it — **this is deviant (*dalal*), it is the way of the Jahmiyyah and Mu'tazilah, and it opposes the Quran, Sunnah, and consensus of the Salaf.**
4. **The ruling on the speaker**: Ibn Taymiyyah and al-Albani both emphasize that **the speaker's intent must be investigated before judging the phrase**, because the same words can carry a true or false meaning.

Therefore, the phrase is **not a mere "difference of opinion" (khilaf)** when it is used to negate Allah's *'uluww*. In that sense, it is a **deviancy**. But the specific words, in isolation, require investigation of intent before declaring the speaker a deviant or disbeliever.

### 2.7 The Critical Nuance — Abu Hanifa and "Kan Allah wa la Makan"

This is one of the most important nuances in this topic, discussed by Ibn Taymiyyah and other Salafi scholars.

#### Abu Hanifa's Statement in *al-Fiqh al-Absat*:

Abu Hanifa (d. 150AH) is reported to have said:

> "**Allah existed and there was no place (kan Allah wa la makan). He existed before He created creation. He existed and there was no 'where,' no creation or anything else. He is the Creator of everything.** So the one who says, 'I do not know about my Lord, is He in the sky or on earth,' is a disbeliever. Likewise, the one who says, 'Verily He is over the Throne, but I do not know whether the Throne is in the sky or on earth,' [is also a disbeliever]."

- **Attributed to**: Imam Abu Hanifa
- **Book**: al-Fiqh al-Absat
- **Source**: https://spubs.com/sps/sp.cfm?articleID=AQD050004&articlePages=7&subsecID=AQD05

#### Al-Dhahabi — On Abu Hanifa's Statement:

> "Abu Muti' Al-Balkhi reported: 'I asked Imam Abu Hanifah about a person who says, "I do not know whether my Rubb is, above the heavens or on earth?" Abu Hanifah, may Allah grant him His mercy, said: "**A person who makes such a statement becomes an apostate because Allah, the Exalted says, 'The Merciful has ascended above the 'Arsh, and the 'Arsh of Allah is above His heavens'**.'"

- **Scholar**: Al-Dhahabi (citing Abu Hanifa)
- **Book**: Al-'Uluw
- **Source**: https://spubs.com/sps/sp.cfm?articleID=AQD050004&articlePages=7&subsecID=AQD05

#### The Salafi Explanation of Abu Hanifa's Statement:

The Salafi scholars explain that Abu Hanifa's statement "Allah was and there was no place" was said in a **specific, correct sense** — meaning that Allah existed *before* He created place/space, and He is not in need of a place. This is **NOT** the same as the Jahmiyyah/Ash'ari usage which negates Allah's *'uluww* (elevation above the Throne).

The key distinction:

1. **Abu Hanifa's usage**: "Allah was and there was no place" = Allah existed before creation, before any place existed; He is not contained by place. **Yet Abu Hanifa ALSO affirmed Allah is above the Throne, above the heavens** — as shown in the same statement where he declares a disbeliever the one who denies Allah is above the heavens.

2. **Jahmiyyah/Ash'ari usage**: "Allah exists without place" = Allah is NOT above the Throne, NOT in any direction, NOT separate from creation — **this negates Allah's 'uluww entirely**, which is deviancy.

#### Ibn Taymiyyah — On the Creed of Abu Hanifa:

> "The established creed from Abu Hanifah in tawhid and qadar and similar matters is in agreement with the creed of these [other Imams] — and **their creed is what the Companions and the Tabi'un were upon, and it is what the Book and the Sunnah have spoken with.**"

### 2.8 The Principle of Halting at Vague Terms (Tawaqquf)

#### TROID.org — On the General Principle:

> "**To halt with regards to vague terms which are not found to be affirmed or denied textually; in wording or meaning. So further explanation is sought.** Then, if something false is meant by it, then we declare Allah free of that and reject it. If, however, it is something that is true and something that is not to be denied for Allah, then it is accepted and the correct terminology — as found in the Book and the Sunnah — is to be made clear."

> "An example of this is the term *jihah* (direction). We halt — neither immediately affirming, nor denying it, and we ask the one who says it: What do you mean by the term *jihah* (direction) for Allah? **If he says that he means a place which contains Him, then we say that this is something false and rejected, and Allah is to be declared free from this. But if he says that it means that Allah is unrestrictedly above, then we say that this is true; and it is not to be denied for Allah.**"

- **Source**: https://www.troid.org/general-principles-regarding-the-names-and-attributes-of-allah/

## Part 3: Summary and Conclusion

### On Question 1 — Is Allah Above His Throne?

**Yes, absolutely.** This is established by:

1. **Seven explicit Quranic verses** (7:54, 10:3, 13:2, 20:5, 25:59, 32:4, 57:4) stating Allah's *istiwaa'* over the Throne
2. **The hadith of the slave girl** (Sahih Muslim 537; Sunan Abi Dawud 328 — **graded Sahih by al-Albani**), where the Prophet ﷺ declared her a believer because she knew Allah is in the heaven
3. **The consensus (ijma')** of the Companions, Tabi'un, and Imams — documented by al-Dhahabi in *al-'Uluww* (with al-Albani's tahqiq), Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Baz, and others
4. **The statements of the Salaf** — Imam Malik, Imam Ahmad, al-Awza'i, Ibn al-Mubarak, al-Bukhari, Abu Nu'aym, Ibn Khuzaymah, and many others
5. **The innate disposition (fitrah)** and sound reason — as Ibn 'Uthaymeen noted, Highness for Allah is affirmed by fitrah, intellect, and revelation

The *istiwaa'* means **"to rise above / ascend over" (ista'laa)** — a real attribute (haqiqah), not metaphorical. It is affirmed **without takyif** (asking how) and **without tamthil** (likening Allah to creation). The interpretation of *istiwaa'* as *istawla* (conquering) is an innovation of the Jahmiyyah, rejected by all the Salafi scholars.

As Ibn Baz (may Allah have mercy on him) stated bluntly:

> "**The one who says that Allah is not in the heaven, or is not above the Throne, is a disbeliever, astray, a filthy Jahmi.**"

### On Question 2 — Is "Allah Exists Without Place" an Opinion or Deviancy?

**It is not a valid difference of opinion (khilaf) when used to negate Allah's 'uluww. In that sense, it is a deviancy.** However, the ruling requires nuance:

| The speaker's intent | The ruling |
|---------------------|------------|
| Allah is not contained/surrounded by creation; He existed before any place existed | **Correct and acceptable** — this is what Abu Hanifa meant |
| Allah is NOT above the Throne, NOT in any direction, neither inside nor outside the universe | **Deviant (dalal)** — this is the way of the Jahmiyyah, Mu'tazilah, and later Ash'aris/Maturidis |
| The phrase is used as a vague slogan without clarifying meaning | **The speaker must be asked to clarify** before judging — this is the principle of Ibn Taymiyyah and al-Albani |

**Key conclusions from the scholars:**

1. **The phrase itself is innovated terminology** — not found in the Quran, Sunnah, or speech of the Salaf (Ibn Taymiyyah, *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 5/264-265).

2. **When used to negate Allah's 'uluww, it is deviancy** — al-Dhahabi called the "third saying" (Allah not in any place, not in any direction, neither inside nor outside the universe) "something that cannot be comprehended nor understood" with "opposition to the verses and the narrations," and said: "Flee with your religion and beware of the opinions of the philosophers."

3. **Ibn 'Uthaymeen's decisive point**: Those who deny all directions for Allah effectively describe non-existence — "If we were asked to describe non-existence, we would not find a more precise description than this."

4. **Al-Albani's principle**: "It is not permissible to negate or affirm these expressions until we investigate the meaning intended by the one who affirms or negates these expressions."

5. **The Abu Hanifa nuance**: The early Salaf (including Abu Hanifa) used "kan Allah wa la makan" (Allah was and there was no place) to mean Allah existed before creation and is not dependent on place — **while ALSO affirming Allah is above the Throne**. The later Jahmiyyah/Ash'aris took the same phrase and used it to negate Allah's 'uluww entirely — a different, deviant meaning.

6. **It is NOT a valid "difference of opinion" in the sense of acceptable ikhtilaf** — Ibn al-Qayyim identified the Jahmiyyah's tawhid (which negates Allah being above His Throne) as derived from philosophy, not revelation, and inherited by the Mu'tazilah, Kullabiyyah, Ash'airah, and Maturidiyyah "to varying degrees."
