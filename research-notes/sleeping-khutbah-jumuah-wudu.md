# Sleeping in the Jumu'ah Khutbah: Jumu'ah Validity and Wudu

A common question from brothers and sisters: you sat through the Friday khutbah, but sleep overcame you. You are not sure if it was "real" sleep, and you are certain you did not fart, touch your private parts, or do anything else that breaks wudu. You want to know:

- Is my Jumu'ah still valid?
- Did my wudu break?
- What are the different scenarios?

This note answers the question using the statements of the Salafi scholars — Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymin, the Permanent Committee, al-Albani, and Islam Q&A — and the authentic Sunnah.

---

## 1. Short Answer

- **Jumu'ah prayer is not automatically invalidated merely because you fell asleep.** If you were present in the congregation and prayed with valid wudu, your Jumu'ah counts as a valid prayer.
- **The special reward of Jumu'ah (expiation of sins between two Fridays) is tied to listening attentively and remaining silent.** Sleeping through the khutbah means you did not fulfill that condition, so the *extra reward* may be lost — but the *prayer itself* is still Jumu'ah, not Zuhr.
- **Your wudu may or may not have broken, depending on the depth of the sleep.**
  - **Light dozing / nu'as:** wudu is still valid.
  - **Deep sleep that removes awareness:** wudu is broken and must be renewed before praying.
  - The decisive test, according to Ibn Uthaymin, is: *if you had passed wind while asleep, would you have felt it?* If yes, wudu is still valid. If no, it is broken.

---

## 2. Is My Jumu'ah Invalid Because I Slept in the Khutbah?

### The Reward Is Conditional on Listening

Allah says:

> **"O you who believe! When the call is proclaimed for the Salah on Friday, come to the remembrance of Allah and leave off business. That is better for you if you did but know!"**  
> (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah 62:9)

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> **"He who took a bath and then came for Jumu'ah prayer and then prayed what was fixed for him, then kept silence till the Imam finished the sermon, and then prayed along with him, his sins between that time and the next Friday would be forgiven, and even of three days more."**  
> (Sahih Muslim 857a)

This hadith shows that the **special reward** of Jumu'ah is for the one who:

1. performs ghusl,
2. comes to Jumu'ah,
3. remains silent,
4. listens until the khutbah finishes,
5. then prays with the imam.

If a person sleeps through the khutbah, he has not listened, so he has missed the condition for that extra reward. But the **prayer** he prays with the imam is still the Jumu'ah prayer, as long as he was present and his prayer was valid.

### Idle Action Makes Jumu'ah "Like Zuhr" in Reward, Not Invalid as a Prayer

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> **"Whoever washed himself on Friday ... and does not indulge in idle talk during the sermon, that will atone (for his sins) between the two Fridays. But he who indulges in idle talk ... that (Friday) will be for him like the noon prayer."**  
> (Sunan Abi Dawud 347; al-Albani graded it hasan)

Shaykh al-Munajjid (Islam Q&A) explained:

> **"The meaning of the Prophet's words, 'There is no Jumu'ah for him,' concerning the one who engages in idle action during the Friday khutbah, is that it will be recorded for him as Zuhr, and he will be deprived of the reward for Jumu'ah prayer."**  
> — [Islam Q&A #119636](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/119636)

This means the **reward** is reduced to that of Zuhr; it does **not** mean the physical prayer is invalid or that he must repeat it as four raka'at of Zuhr. His two-raka'at prayer behind the imam still count as Jumu'ah.

### The Permanent Committee: Sleeping Is a Distraction From Listening

The Permanent Committee (Ibn Baz, Ibn Ghudayyan, al-'Afeefee) said:

> **"It is obligatory for a Muslim to listen to the Khutbah and stay away from distractions like talking or sleeping."**  
> — [al-Feqh.com, "Those who sleep during Friday Khutbah"](https://www.al-feqh.com/en/those-who-sleep-during-friday-khutbah)

They also quoted the hadith in Muslim about the one who speaks during the khutbah being "like the donkey carrying books," and the one who tells others to listen having "no Jumu'ah." This shows the seriousness of inattention, but it refers to the **loss of reward**, not the invalidity of the prayer itself.

### Bottom Line on Jumu'ah Validity

- If you were physically present and prayed behind the imam with valid wudu, your Jumu'ah is **valid**.
- If you slept through the entire khutbah, you missed the **extra reward** tied to listening.
- If you did not wake up for the prayer at all and missed it, then you must pray **Zuhr** instead.

## 3. Did Sleeping Break My Wudu?

The answer depends on the **kind** of sleep, not only the position.

### The Salafi Principle: Sleep Is a "Place" Where Nullifiers May Occur

Sleep itself is not a direct nullifier of wudu in the strongest Salafi view. Rather, it is a state in which a person may lose awareness and a nullifier (like passing wind) may occur without him knowing. This is the view of the Hanbali madhhab and the later Salafi scholars such as Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Baz, and Ibn Uthaymin.

### Scenario A: Light Dozing / Nu'as While Sitting — Wudu Remains Valid

The Companions used to wait for 'Isha until their heads would nod from drowsiness, then they would pray **without renewing wudu**:

> **"The Companions during the lifetime of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to wait for the night prayer so much so that their heads were lowered down (by dozing). Then they offered prayer and did not perform ablution."**  
> (Sunan Abi Dawud 200; al-Albani graded it sahih)

This hadith is the proof that **light dozing while sitting does not break wudu**, because the Prophet ﷺ did not rebuke them and they prayed without making a new wudu.

Shaykh Ibn Baz said:

> **"النعاس لا ينقض الوضوء، إذا كان نعاس على اسمه ليس فيه نوم ثقيل يذهب معه الشعور."**  
> "Drowsiness does not invalidate wudu, if it is drowsiness by its name and does not contain heavy sleep in which awareness is lost."  
> — [Ibn Baz](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/3775/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D9%88%D9%85-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%82%D8%B6-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D8%B0%D8%A7-%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%BA%D8%B1%D9%82%D8%A7)

The Permanent Committee said:

> **"Indeed the light sleep in which consciousness is not fully lost does not nullify the wudhoo. For it is indeed established that the Prophet ﷺ occasionally used to delay the 'Isha prayer until the heads of the Companions would be nodding off with sleep, then they would pray and they would not make the wudhoo."**  
> — [Permanent Committee, Fatwa 12885](https://www.fatwaislam.com/fis/index.cfm?ID=647&scn=fd)

### Scenario B: Deep Sleep That Removes Awareness — Wudu Is Broken

Shaykh Ibn Baz said:

> **"متى نام نومًا مستغرقًا زال معه شعوره، سواء كان جالسًا أو مضطجعًا، بطل وضوؤه."**  
> "Whenever one sleeps a deep sleep in which his awareness is removed — whether sitting or lying down — his wudu is invalidated."  
> — [Ibn Baz](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/13871/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%BA%D8%B1%D9%82-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D9%88%D9%85)

He supported this with the hadith of Safwan ibn 'Assal:

> **"The Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to tell us, when we were travelling, to wipe over our Khuffs and not take them off for three nights in the event of defecating, urinating or sleeping; only in the case of Janabah."**  
> (Sunan an-Nasa'i 127; hasan)

This hadith lists **sleep** alongside stools and urine as a reason to remove the khuffs / make wudu, which shows that sleep that nullifies purity is the sleep that is like those things — i.e., sleep in which the person is not aware.

Also, 'Ali reported:

> **"The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'The eye is the leather strap (that ties up) the anus, so whoever falls asleep, let him perform ablution.'"**  
> (Sunan Abi Dawud 203; al-Albani graded it hasan)

### Scenario C: Lying Down / Reclining — Wudu Is More Likely Broken

The hadith "The eye is the strap of the anus" and the saying "Ablution is necessary for one who sleeps while lying down, because when he lies down his joints relax" indicate that **lying down makes it far more likely** that a nullifier has occurred and that awareness is lost. So if you sleep lying down or reclining, you should renew wudu.

Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin said:

> **"النَّوم: إذا كان كثيرًا بحيث لا يشعُر النَّائِمُ لو أحدث، فأمَّا إذا كان النَّومُ يَسيرًا يشعُر النَّائم بنفسه لو أحدَث، فإنَّه لا ينقُضُ الوضوءَ، ولا فرْق في ذلك أن يكون نائمًا مضطجعًا، أو قاعدًا معتمِدًا، أو قاعدًا غير معتمِدٍ."**  
> "Sleep: if it is much such that the sleeper would not feel if he passed wind; but if it is light such that the sleeper would feel himself if he passed wind, then it does not invalidate wudu. There is no difference whether he is lying down, sitting with support, or sitting without support."  
> — [Ibn Uthaymin, Majmu' Fatawa 11/195, quoted in dorar.net](https://dorar.net/feqhia/417)

**The key factor is the loss of awareness, not the posture alone.** However, lying down is usually accompanied by a complete loss of awareness, so wudu is usually broken.

## 4. The Decisive Test for Your Own Situation

Use this simple test, which is based on Ibn Uthaymin's explanation:

1. **If you only nodded off slightly and would have felt any wind passing or any other nullifier, your wudu is valid.** Your Jumu'ah is also valid.
2. **If you fell into real, deep sleep such that you would not have felt if wind passed from you, your wudu is broken.** You must make wudu before praying. If you already prayed without renewing wudu, that prayer was invalid and you must repeat it.
3. **If you are genuinely unsure** whether it was deep sleep or light dozing, and you prayed without renewing wudu, the safer course is to **renew wudu and repeat the prayer** if you can. If repeating is not possible, the scholars differ, but the strong view is that you rely on what you are certain of: if you are certain you had wudu and are uncertain whether it was broken, your original state of purity remains.

---

## 5. Different Scenarios Summarized

| Scenario | Jumu'ah Prayer | Wudu | What to Do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light dozing while sitting; you remained aware | Valid | Still valid | Nothing; continue. Try to change your place if you feel drowsy. |
| Deep sleep while sitting; awareness was lost | Valid **only if** you renewed wudu and prayed | Broken | Renew wudu before the prayer. If you already prayed, repeat it. |
| Sleeping while lying down or reclining | Likely valid **if** you woke and renewed wudu | Broken | Make wudu before praying. |
| Slept through khutbah and entire prayer | You missed Jumu'ah | — | Pray Zuhr as soon as possible. |
| Slept but are sure no wind, touching private parts, etc. happened | Valid if you had wudu | **If sleep was light:** still valid. **If sleep was deep:** broken by the presumption of the lost awareness, even if no specific nullifier happened. |

## 6. What Actually Breaks Wudu? (For Your Reassurance)

You said you are sure you did not do any of these. That is good. The well-known nullifiers of wudu are:

1. **Anything exiting from the front or back passage** (urine, stool, wind, madhiy, wadi, etc.).
2. **Deep sleep / loss of consciousness** (as explained above).
3. **Touching the private part with the bare hand** (flesh to flesh, without a barrier), whether with desire or not.
4. **Eating camel meat** — according to the correct view among Salafis.

If none of these happened and your sleep was only light dozing, your wudu is still valid.

---

## 7. What to Do If You Feel Sleepy During the Khutbah

The Prophet ﷺ instructed:

> **"When any of you dozes in the mosque (on Friday), he should change his place."**  
> (Sunan Abi Dawud 1119; al-Albani graded it sahih)

Shaykh al-Munajjid (Islam Q&A) said:

> **"If a person dozes while listening to the Friday sermon, it is recommended for him to change the place with the person next to him. In doing this he should be careful not to speak; rather, he should use some gestures (hand movements, etc.)."**  
> — [Islam Q&A #46](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/46)

So the next time you feel sleepy, change your sitting place without talking, or stand up briefly at the back, or make wudu if possible, so that you can stay awake and benefit from the khutbah.

---

## 8. Final Answer to Your Specific Question

You said:

> "I slept in the khutbah, while sitting... I know I didn't fart or touch any private parts... but I'm sure I did sleep like real."

- **If the sleep was "real" / deep** — meaning you lost awareness of your surroundings and would not have felt if wind passed — then **your wudu is broken** and you must renew it before praying. If you already prayed without making wudu, that prayer is invalid and you must repeat it. Your Jumu'ah, if prayed with renewed wudu, is valid, but you may have lost the extra reward of listening to the khutbah.

- **If the sleep was light dozing** — you could hear sounds, your head just nodded, and you would have felt any nullifier — then **your wudu remains valid** and your Jumu'ah is valid. You do not need to repeat anything.

- If you are not sure which it was, and you prayed, the safer option is to **renew wudu and repeat the prayer** if you still have time. If time has passed and you cannot repeat Jumu'ah, then pray **Zuhr**.
