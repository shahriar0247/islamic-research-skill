# A Gradual Guide for a New Muslim

> A gradual roadmap for someone who has recently embraced Islam, based on the methodology of the early generations (the Salaf) and the statements of the scholars who followed their path. The Prophet ﷺ taught people gradually — faith first, then actions, then details. This guide follows that order. Nothing here is meant to be learned all at once; each stage builds on the one before it.

---

## STAGE 1: What Just Happened (Day 1)

### The Shahada — the testimony of faith
- **"La ilaha illa Allah, Muhammadur rasul Allah"**
- Meaning: *"There is no god worthy of worship except Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger."*
- By saying this with belief in the heart, a person enters Islam. All past sins are wiped clean — the person is like a newborn. (Sahih Muslim)

### What this means practically
- Upon entering Islam, Allah forgives everything that came before.
- A new Muslim does not need to do everything perfectly from day one. Islam is a journey, not a switch.
- Allah looks at the heart and the effort, not perfection. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"The most beloved deeds to Allah are those done consistently, even if small."* (Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 6464; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 783)
- **The first rule:** do not let the devil cause overwhelm. Shaytan will whisper "you can't do all this, you're not a real Muslim." That is a trap. Start small, stay consistent.

### What the scholars say a new Muslim should learn first

**Shaykh Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz** (may Allah have mercy on him) said in his book *"The Important Lessons for the General Ummah"* (al-Durus al-Muhimmah):
> "It is essential to start with him by teaching him the meaning of the Shahaadatayn (twin declaration of faith), because it is the key to Islam and summarises it. Explain to him Tawheed (the Oneness of Allah), which is the basis of Muslim success in this world and in the Hereafter, so that his heart will become attached to Allah."
>
> *(Source: IslamQA, Fatwa 104533; abdurrahman.org)*

**Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan** (may Allah preserve him) said:
> "Knowledge of (Tawheed and Shirk) is more incumbent upon you than the rulings of: siyaam (fasting), salaah (prayer), zakah (obligatory alms), etc. It is the foremost matter and foundation because, salaah, zakah, hajj, and other acts of ibaadaat are not correct if it is not built upon the foundation of correct aqeedah, and that is to have tawheed sincerely for Allaah."
>
> *(Source: Explanation of "Qawaaidul Arba'ah")*

**Shaykh Salih al-Luhaydan** (may Allah have mercy on him) said:
> "I advise this individual to be consistent in his prayers, in his five daily prayers & he should perform his prayers at their legislated times. I also advise this individual to learn that which he will use to worship Allaah, in terms of recitation of Qur'aan, in terms of supplications he should say. The most important part of the Qur'aan for him to learn at this time is Suratul Faatihaa. He should learn the proper pronunciation of the opening chapter of the Qur'aan."
>
> *(Source: Kitaab-ut-Tawheed Class, Riyadh, 01.12.12)*

**Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymin** (may Allah have mercy on him) said in his *Sharh Thalaathatul-Usool*:
> "Know, may Allaah have mercy upon you, that it is obligatory upon every Muslim, male and female, to learn and act upon the following three matters: THE FIRST: That Allaah created us and provided sustenance for us, and He did not leave us without a purpose rather He sent Messengers to us."
>
> *(Source: Sharh Thalaathatul-Usool)*

## STAGE 2: The Sources of Islam — Quran, Sunnah, and Avoiding Innovation

Before learning what to believe and what to do, a Muslim must know **where the religion comes from**. This is the foundation that protects a person from misguidance for the rest of their life.

### The two primary sources
1. **The Quran** — the literal, preserved word of Allah, revealed to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ over 23 years. Allah has promised to protect it from corruption: *"Indeed, it is We who sent down the Reminder, and indeed, We will preserve it."* (Quran, Surah Al-Hijr 15:9) It is the final revelation and the highest authority in Islam.
2. **The Sunnah** — the sayings, actions, approvals, and characteristics of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The Sunnah is revelation too, and it explains and complements the Quran. Allah says: *"Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it."* (Quran, Surah Al-Hashr 59:7) And: *"Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah."* (Quran, Surah An-Nisa 4:80)

A Muslim does not pick and choose between the two. The Quran commands prayer; the Sunnah shows *how* to pray. The Quran commands Hajj; the Sunnah shows *how* to perform it. Without the Sunnah, the Quran cannot be implemented.

### Avoiding Bid'ah (Religious Innovation)

**Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah** (may Allah have mercy on him) defined bid'ah in *Iqtida' as-Siraat al-Mustaqeem*:
> "As for bid'ah in the religious sense, it is whatever is not proven by a shari'ah proof."

He also laid down two conditions: a newly-introduced matter will only be bid'ah if (1) acting upon it was possible during the time of the Prophet ﷺ, and (2) the reason for its existence was present during the time of the Prophet ﷺ.

The Prophet ﷺ said:
> *"Whoever introduces something into this affair of ours that is not from it, it is rejected."*
> *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 2697; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1718)*

And:
> *"Every innovation is misguidance, and every misguidance is in the Fire."*
> *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 867 — from the khutbah of al-Irbad ibn Sariyah)*

Bid'ah is dangerous because it slowly replaces the authentic religion with invented practices. The way to stay safe is to stick to what the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions practiced.

This does not mean everything new is haram — new technology, new ways of organizing da'wah, new languages for teaching are fine. Bid'ah refers specifically to inventing new acts of *worship* or new beliefs that have no basis in the Quran and Sunnah.

### The Best Generations — the Salaf

The Prophet ﷺ said:
> *"The best of people is my generation, then those who come after them, then those who come after them."*
> *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 6428; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 6472 — agreed upon, from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud and Abu Hurayrah)*

And:
> *"Indeed Allah will not gather my Ummah upon deviation, and Allah's Hand is over the Jama'ah, and whoever deviates, he deviates to the Fire."*
> *(Jami at-Tirmidhi, Hadith 2167 — graded Hasan by Shaykh al-Albani)*

**Note on this hadith:** Shaykh al-Albani graded the Tirmidhi version as Hasan (good). Another version in Sunan Ibn Majah (Hadith 3950) which adds "follow the great majority" was graded **Very Da'if (Very Weak)** by al-Albani. The authentic meaning, as explained by the scholars, is that Allah has protected the **scholars** of the ummah from unanimously agreeing upon something false — not the common folk, and not a simple majority. (IslamQA, Fatwa 147341)

And:
> *"There shall not cease to be a group from my Ummah that remains firmly steadfast upon the command of Allah, the Exalted and Almighty, it cannot be harmed by those who oppose it."*
> *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1920 — from Thawban; also Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 3640, 7311, 7459 — from Mugheerah ibn Shu'bah; graded Hasan by Shaykh al-Albani in As-Saheehah, no. 1962)*

**Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan** said about this hadith:
> "This group will not cease to exist in the Ummah. The Ummah will not be deprived of this Sunni Group. This Taa'ifah (Group) can be one group. If it adheres to the Qur'an and the Sunnah, it is the Taa'ifah Al-Mansoorah (The Victorious Group), even if it is only one."
> *(Source: salafidawahmanchester.com)*

**Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah** stated that the hadiths concerning at-Taa'ifatul-Mansoorah reach the level of being **mutawaatir** (mass-transmitted). *(Source: Iqtidaa' as-Siraat al-Mustaqeem, p.6)*

These hadiths establish several principles:
- **The early generations** (the Companions, their students, and their students' students — the *Salaf as-Salih*) are the benchmark for understanding Islam. Their understanding of the Quran and Sunnah is the most accurate, because they learned it directly from the Prophet ﷺ and lived it in its purest form.
- **Allah will not allow the scholars of the ummah to unanimously agree upon misguidance.** This is a tremendous mercy. It means the consensus of the qualified scholars, following the understanding of the early generations, is a safe guide.
- **A group will always remain on the truth.** The religion will never be completely lost. This is a promise from Allah and His Messenger ﷺ.

### Trusting the Scholars

The Prophet ﷺ said:
> *"The learned are the heirs of the Prophets, and the Prophets leave neither dinar nor dirham, leaving only knowledge, and he who takes it takes an abundant portion."*
> *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 3641 — graded Sahih by Shaykh al-Albani; also narrated by al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Ahmad, Ibn Hibban)*

Not everyone who speaks about Islam is a scholar, and not everyone who wears a thobe or has a platform is qualified to give fatwa. But the response to unqualified speakers is **not** to distrust all scholars — it is to find the qualified ones.

A real scholar is one who:
- Has studied the Arabic language deeply (the language of the Quran and Sunnah).
- Has studied the Quran with its sciences (tafsir, abrogation, reasons for revelation).
- Has studied the Hadith with its sciences (narrators, grading, abrogation).
- Has studied the methodology of the early generations (the Salaf).
- Has studied under qualified teachers with chains of transmission — not just self-taught from the internet.

**The majority of scholars are trustworthy.** The idea that "all scholars are corrupt" or "all scholars sold out" is itself a deviant view that cuts people off from the inheritance of the prophets.

**Abu Khadeejah Abdul Wahid** (Salafi Publications) said:
> "Recognise and know the Scholars of Salafiyyah, and know those who take from them (their students). This knowledge is Dīn so look to whom you take your Dīn from."
> *(Source: salafisounds.com)*

**Books recommended by the scholars for beginners:**

**Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan** said:
> "We advise him to memorize the smaller books in 'Aqeedah, for example, 'Thalaathatul 'Usool' and 'Kashfush Shubuhaat' and 'Kitaabut Tawheed' of Shaykh Muhammad bin Abdul Wahaab Rahimahullaah."
> *(Source: path2islam.com)*

**Shaykh al-Uthaymin** recommended this curriculum for beginners:
1. Aqeedah: Thalaathatul Usool, Qawaa'idul Arba'ah, Kashf Ash-Shubuhaat, Kitaab at-Tawheed
2. Arabic Grammar
3. Hadith: Ulooh al-Hadith
4. Mustalah: Nukhbatul Fikr
5. Fiqh: Adabul Mashee ilas Salaah
> *(Source: salafidawahmanchester.com)*

### Summary of this stage
- The religion comes from the **Quran and the Sunnah**, understood the way the **early generations** understood them.
- **Innovation (bid'ah)** in worship is rejected and must be avoided.
- The **scholars of the ummah** following the understanding of the Salaf will not unanimously agree upon misguidance — Allah has protected them.
- **Qualified scholars** are to be trusted and followed; the existence of unqualified speakers does not invalidate the scholars as a whole.

## STAGE 3: Tawheed — The Foundation of the Religion

Before even the 6 articles of Iman, a Muslim must understand **Tawheed** — the oneness of Allah. This is the first thing the prophets called to, and the first thing a new Muslim must learn.

**Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah** said:
> "Indeed; it is necessarily known from the religion of the Messenger (sallallaahu 'alaihi wa sallam), and upon which the Ummah is in agreement upon that; the basis of Islaam and the first thing which the creation are to be commanded with is: the testimony that none has the right to be worshipped except Allaah, and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allaah."
> *(Cited by Shaykh 'Abdur-Rahmaan bin Hasan)*

And:
> "The beginning of the religion and its end and its outwardness and its inwardness is: Tawheed and making the whole of the religion sincerely for Allaah, it is the actualization of the statement: laa ilaaha illallaah."
> *(Majmu' Fataawa 10/264)*

**Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah** said:
> "So Tawheed is the first thing that is entered into Islaam with and the last thing one departs with from the life of this world, as the Prophet (sallallaahu 'alaihi wa sallam) said: 'Whoever's last words are: laa ilaaha illallaah; will enter Paradise.' Therefore it is the first obligation and the last obligation. So Tawheed is the initiation of the affair as well as its end."
> *(Madaarij as-Saalikeen 3/328)*

**Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab** defined Tawheed in *Kitab at-Tawheed*:
> "Tawheed is to single out Allaah with attributes of majesty and perfection and to single Him out in worship. It comprises both knowledge and action. As for knowledge, then it is Tawheed al-Rububiyyah and Tawheed al-Asmaa wal-Sifaat. And as for action, then it is Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah. All revealed books and sent messengers called to this Tawheed, which is the foundation of Islaam, the religion of all Prophets and Messengers."
> *(Kitab at-Tawheed)*

### The Three Categories of Tawheed
1. **Tawheed al-Rububiyyah** — Oneness of Allah's Lordship. Allah alone is the Creator, Sustainer, and Disposer of all affairs.
2. **Tawheed al-Asma' was-Sifat** — Oneness of Allah's Names and Attributes. Allah has the most beautiful names and perfect attributes, as described in the Quran and Sunnah, without distortion, denial, likening Him to creation, or asking "how."
3. **Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah (Ibadah)** — Oneness of Worship. All worship must be directed to Allah alone — prayer, supplication, sacrifice, vows, hope, fear, trust, love. This is the category the polytheists of old violated, and the category most emphasized by the prophets.

### The Three Fundamental Principles (Thalathat al-Usul)
**Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab** structured beginner's learning around the three questions every person will be asked in the grave:
1. **Who is your Lord?** — Allah, who created and sustains everything.
2. **What is your religion?** — Islam, the religion of all the prophets.
3. **Who is your Prophet?** — Muhammad ﷺ, the final messenger.

*(Source: Thalathat al-Usul, abdurrahman.org)*

## STAGE 4: Iman (Faith) — The Six Articles

Before actions, a Muslim must understand what they believe. Iman has **6 articles**, as taught in the famous Hadith of Jibril (Sahih Muslim, Hadith 8 — from Umar ibn al-Khattab):

### 1. Belief in Allah (Tawheed)
- Allah is One. He has no partner, no son, no parents. He created everything, sustains everything, and deserves all worship.
- He has 99 names (Asma ul-Husna) — each tells us something about Him: Ar-Rahman (The Most Merciful), Al-Ghafur (The Forgiving), Al-Wadud (The Loving), etc.
- **Key point:** Allah is not distant or angry. He is closer than the jugular vein (Quran, Surah Qaf 50:16). He loves repentance. Every time a person turns back to Him, He welcomes them.

### 2. Belief in the Angels
- Created beings made of light, who worship Allah and carry out His commands.
- **Jibril (Gabriel)** — brought the Quran to the Prophet ﷺ.
- Two angels with each person: **Kiraman Katibeen** — one records good deeds, one records bad. This is not to cause fear, but so a person knows they are never truly alone and Allah's record is perfectly fair.
- Angels have no free will — they always obey.

### 3. Belief in the Books
- Allah sent revelations to prophets across history:
  - **Tawrah** (Torah) to Musa (Moses)
  - **Zabur** (Psalms) to Dawud (David)
  - **Injil** (Gospel) to Isa (Jesus)
  - **Quran** to Muhammad ﷺ — the final revelation, preserved unchanged.
- The earlier books were altered over time; the Quran is protected by Allah Himself (Quran, Surah Al-Hijr 15:9).

### 4. Belief in the Messengers
- Allah sent messengers to every nation: Adam, Nuh (Noah), Ibrahim (Abraham), Musa, Isa, and finally Muhammad ﷺ — the last prophet.
- **Muhammad ﷺ** is the final messenger. No prophet after him. Anyone claiming prophecy after him is a liar.
- Muslims love and respect all prophets, but worship Allah alone — not any prophet.

### 5. Belief in the Day of Judgment
- This life is temporary. After death, everyone will be resurrected and judged by Allah.
- Paradise (Jannah) for those who believed and did good. Hell (Jahannam) for those who rejected truth and did evil.
- This is not meant to terrify — it is meant to give life meaning. Every action matters. And Allah's mercy is vast — He forgives all sins for the one who turns to Him.

### 6. Belief in Qadar (Predestination)
- Everything that happens is by Allah's knowledge and will. Nothing escapes Him.
- **This does NOT mean "I have no free will."** Islam teaches both: Allah knows and decrees, AND humans choose and are responsible. Both are true — the "how" is beyond human comprehension.
- Practical benefit: when bad things happen, a person does not despair — Allah has a plan. When good happens, they are grateful, not arrogant.

## STAGE 5: The 5 Pillars (Overview First, Then Detail)

The 5 pillars are the framework of a Muslim's life:

1. **Shahada** — the testimony of faith (already done upon entering Islam)
2. **Salah** — 5 daily prayers (the most important daily practice — see Stage 6)
3. **Zakat** — charity (2.5% of savings once a year, for those who have wealth above a threshold). For now, just know it exists; it applies once a person has savings.
4. **Sawm** — fasting in Ramadan (one month a year, dawn to sunset). Learned in detail when Ramadan approaches.
5. **Hajj** — pilgrimage to Mecca, once in a lifetime, for those who can afford it. Long-term.

**For a new Muslim, the immediate focus is #1 and #2.** The rest come in time.

## STAGE 6: Salah (Prayer) — The Most Important Daily Action

This is the priority. Salah is the difference between a Muslim and a non-Muslim. It should be learned slowly — not in a single day. A separate detailed guide on how to pray is available.

### Why salah matters
- It is the direct connection to Allah, 5 times a day.
- It is the first thing Allah will question on the Day of Judgment.
- It keeps a person mindful of Allah throughout the day and prevents sin.

### The 5 daily prayers and their times
| Prayer | Time window | Rak'ahs (units) |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Fajr** | Dawn to sunrise | 2 |
| **Dhuhr** | After noon until mid-afternoon | 4 |
| **Asr** | Mid-afternoon until sunset | 4 |
| **Maghrib** | Just after sunset until twilight ends | 3 |
| **Isha** | After twilight until dawn | 4 |

- A prayer app (recommended: **Athan** or **Muslim Pro**) will notify when each prayer time enters.
- Local mosque times can also be checked online.

### Practical advice
- **Do not miss Fajr and Isha** — these are the hardest but most important. The Prophet ﷺ said whoever prays these two is in Allah's protection. *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 574; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 657)*
- If a prayer is missed, it should be made up as soon as remembered — but this should not become a habit.
- Praying in the mosque is 27 times more rewarding than praying alone. *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 645 — from Abdullah ibn Umar)* Going to the mosque — especially for Fajr, Maghrib, Isha — also builds community.
- **It is okay if prayers feel "empty" at first.** Keep showing up. The feeling follows the action, not the other way around.

*(For the full step-by-step method of prayer with all adhkar, references, and the arkan/wajibat/sunan, see the separate guide "How to Pray Salah.")*

## STAGE 7: Major Sins to Avoid

A new Muslim should not be overwhelmed with every rule. Start with the **big ones** — the things that are clearly haram and serious. The smaller details are learned over time.

### The Book of Major Sins (Kitab al-Kaba'ir) by Imam al-Dhahabi

**Imam al-Dhahabi** (may Allah have mercy on him) compiled the major sins in his famous book *Kitab al-Kaba'ir*. He defined them as:
> "Major sins are defined as what is forbidden by Allah and His Messenger in the Qur'an and the Sunnah in addition to what is narrated on the authority of early Muslims."

*(Source: Kitab al-Kaba'ir by Imam Shamsu ed-Deen Dhahabi)*

### The major sins (from al-Dhahabi's list — the most important ones for a new Muslim):
1. **Shirk** — associating partners with Allah. The one unforgivable sin if a person dies on it. A Muslim is safe from this as long as worship is kept for Allah alone.
2. **Murder** — taking innocent life.
3. **Sorcery / magic** — forbidden entirely.
4. **Not performing the prayer** — abandoning salah is extremely serious.
5. **Not paying Zakat.**
6. **Breaking one's fast during Ramadan without an excuse.**
7. **Not performing Hajj when able.**
8. **Showing disrespect to parents.**
9. **Severing ties with relatives.**
10. **Adultery / fornication (zina).**
11. **Sodomy.**
12. **Accepting usurious gain (riba / interest).**
13. **Wrongfully consuming an orphan's property.**
14. **Lying about the Prophet ﷺ.**
15. **Fleeing from the battlefield.**
16. **Arrogance, pride, conceit, vanity.**
17. **Bearing false witness.**
18. **Drinking alcohol.**
19. **Gambling.**
20. **Accusing a woman of adultery (qadhf).**
21. **Theft.**
22. **Suicide.**
23. **Telling lies.**
24. **Bribery.**
25. **Women imitating men and vice versa.**
26. **Showing off in good work (riya).**
27. **Learning sacred knowledge for the sake of this world or concealing it.**
28. **Believing in fortune-tellers and astrologers.**
29. **Making statues and pictures.**
30. **Men wearing silk and gold.**

*(Source: Kitab al-Kaba'ir by Imam al-Dhahabi — the full list contains 70 major sins)*

### For a young person specifically — the real battlegrounds
These are where young Muslims actually slip, and they should be addressed honestly:
- **Pornography and masturbation** — extremely common, extremely destructive to the soul. This is a struggle, not a unique weakness. Remedies include fasting, lowering the gaze, avoiding being alone with one's phone, and seeking help when needed.
- **Girlfriend/boyfriend relationships** — haram. The path is marriage. If marriage is not yet possible, fasting and guarding oneself is the way.
- **Alcohol / parties / clubbing** — the lifestyle a person may have known before Islam is now off-limits. New friends and new habits are needed. This is one of the hardest parts — losing the old social circle.
- **Music** — scholars differ. The safer view is to avoid music with instruments; vocal-only (nasheed) and meaningful content is fine. This should not be the first battle — transition gradually.
- **Friendships** — some old friends will likely be lost. This is normal and painful. New Muslim friends are needed — connect to the mosque, to a circle of young Muslims.

### What to know about sin generally
- **Everyone sins.** The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Every son of Adam sins, and the best of sinners are those who repent."* *(Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 4251 — graded Hasan by al-Albani)*
- **When a sin is committed, repent immediately.** Say "Astaghfirullah" and resolve not to repeat it. Allah's mercy is bigger than any sin.
- **Do not let one sin cause giving up on everything.** Shaytan's biggest trick is making a small sin feel like "you're a hypocrite, might as well quit." No — keep praying, keep trying.
- **Major sins require specific repentance:** stop the sin, regret it, resolve never to return, and if it involved someone's rights, restore them.

## STAGE 8: Daily Life as a Muslim — The Practical Stuff

### Food
- **Halal meat only.** If halal is not available, kosher or seafood is permissible (all seafood is halal for the majority). Vegetarian is always safe.
- **Pork is strictly haram** — in all forms (bacon, ham, gelatin from pork, etc.).
- **Alcohol** — forbidden to drink, cook with, or buy. Check ingredient labels for "alcohol" or "wine."
- **Read labels** — many processed foods have hidden pork derivatives (gelatin, emulsifiers). This is learned over time.

### Money
- **Zakat** — 2.5% of savings annually, once wealth is above the nisab threshold (~value of 85g of gold). The details are learned when applicable.
- **Riba (interest)** — haram. Avoid interest-bearing bank accounts (use current/checking, not interest savings), interest-bearing loans, and credit cards if a balance is carried. This is a big topic — learned gradually.
- **Sadaqah** — voluntary charity. Even small amounts given regularly are beloved to Allah. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Charity does not decrease wealth."* *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 2588)*

### Relationships
- **No boyfriend/girlfriend.** Marriage is the only halal path to intimacy.
- **Lowering the gaze** — do not look at the opposite sex with desire (Quran, Surah An-Nur 24:30-31). Hard in the smartphone era, but essential.
- **Not alone with a non-mahram** — the Prophet ﷺ said a man and woman alone together have shaytan as the third. *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 5233; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1341)*
- **Respect for parents** — even if they are not Muslim and even if they are difficult. Kindness to parents is huge in Islam. Do not argue with them about religion; show them Islam through character.
- **Mahram relationships** — a Muslim should learn who they cannot marry (mother, sister, aunt, etc.) and who their mahram are (those they can never marry, and can be alone with or travel with).

### Clothing / Modesty
- **Men:** cover from navel to knee minimum. Do not wear silk or gold (haram for men). Dress modestly — not tight, not flashy.
- **Lower the gaze.**

### Hygiene (Fitrah)
The Prophet ﷺ said:
> *"The deeds connected to the Fitrah are five (or five things are connected to the Fitrah): circumcision, shaving the pubic hairs, clipping the nails, plucking the armpit hairs and trimming the mustache."*
> *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 257; Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 292 — Sahih)*

- **Circumcision** — recommended, done when ready. Not a barrier to being Muslim.
- **Trim nails regularly, keep clean.** Cleanliness is half of faith. *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 223 — from Abu Malik al-Ash'ari)*

## STAGE 9: Building the Relationship with Allah

This is the heart of the religion. Without it, the rules become a burden.

### Quran
- **Read a translation of the Quran** in one's own language. Arabic recitation is a skill built over years — start with meaning.
- Recommended starting point: **Surah Al-Fatihah, then the last juz (Juz Amma — the short surahs at the end)**. These are what are recited in prayer.
- Even 5 minutes a day reading Quran with translation is hugely beneficial.

### Dua (Supplication)
- A person can talk to Allah in their own language, anytime, anywhere. No formula is required.
- A few common ones to learn:
  - Before eating: **"Bismillah"** (بسم الله)
  - After eating: **"Alhamdulillah"** (الحمد لله)
  - When entering the bathroom: **"Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal khubuthi wal khaba'ith"** (اللهم إني أعوذ بك من الخبث والخبائث)
  - When sneezing: **"Alhamdulillah"** (and the one who hears says "Yarhamukallah")
  - When angry: seek refuge in Allah from shaytan: **"A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ir-rajim"** (أعوذ بالله من الشيطان الرجيم)

### Repentance (Tawbah)
- The Prophet ﷺ used to seek forgiveness 70–100 times a day. *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 6307 — from Abu Hurayrah)*
- Formula: **"Astaghfirullah wa atubu ilayh"** (أستغفر الله وأتوب إليه) — I seek Allah's forgiveness and turn to Him.
- This should be a daily habit.

### Dhikr (Remembrance)
- Simple phrases that can be said anytime:
  - **"SubhanAllah"** (سبحان الله) — Glory to Allah
  - **"Alhamdulillah"** (الحمد لله) — All praise is for Allah
  - **"Allahu Akbar"** (الله أكبر) — Allah is Greatest
  - **"La ilaha illa Allah"** (لا إله إلا الله) — There is no god but Allah
- The Prophet ﷺ said two words light on the tongue, heavy on the scale: *"SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi, SubhanAllahil azim."* *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 6406; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 2694 — from Abu Hurayrah)*

## STAGE 10: Community — A Muslim Cannot Do This Alone

This is critical. A new Muslim without community often falls away. What is needed:

1. **A mosque** — find one nearby; attend Maghrib or Isha; meet the imam and regulars.
2. **Muslim friends of similar age** — connect to young Muslims. People who share the same values are essential now.
3. **A mentor** — someone knowledgeable who can be texted with questions. There will always be questions; make sure there is someone reliable to ask.
4. **Old friends** — they do not have to be cut off entirely, but caution is needed. If they pull a person back to haram (drinking, relationships, parties), distance is necessary. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"A person is on the religion of his close friend, so let him look at whom he befriends."* *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 4833; Jami at-Tirmidhi, Hadith 2378 — graded Hasan by al-Albani)*

### Family
- If the family is non-Muslim: be extra kind. Do not preach at them. Show them Islam through behavior. Many families come to Islam through seeing the change in their convert relative.
- If they are upset: be patient. They often come around. Arguing about religion with them usually backfires.
- A Muslim should NOT cut ties with their family. That would be a sin in itself.

## STAGE 11: Common Questions

**"What if I miss a prayer?"** — Make it up as soon as remembered. Do not let one missed prayer become two.

**"What if I committed a major sin?"** — Repent sincerely. Allah forgives all sins. *"Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of Allah's mercy. Indeed Allah forgives all sins."* (Quran, Surah Az-Zumar 39:53)

**"Do I have to learn Arabic?"** — Not immediately. Start with translation. Arabic is a lifelong journey. Learn enough to pray, then build from there.

**"What if my family disowns me?"** — Make dua for them. Be kind. Provide for them if needed. Stay patient. Allah tests those He loves.

**"I keep committing the same sin."** — Keep repenting. The fact that there is guilt is a sign of iman. A heart that does not feel guilt is the danger, not the one that keeps struggling.

**"Is X haram?"** — Do not guess. Ask a knowledgeable person or check reliable sources like IslamQA.info. Better to ask than to assume.

**"I feel disconnected from Allah."** — This is normal. Faith goes up and down. The Companions felt this too. Keep doing the actions even when the feeling is absent — the feeling will return. Actions come before feelings in Islam.

**"Can I still hang out with my old friends?"** — If they respect the Islam and do not pull toward haram, yes. If being with them means drinking, clubbing, relationships — no. Find new company.

## STAGE 12: Gradual Plan — First 6 Months

### Week 1
- Learn what the Shahada means
- Start learning wudu
- Watch an experienced Muslim pray
- Memorize Al-Fatihah
- Learn the three categories of Tawheed
- Learn the two sources (Quran and Sunnah) and the danger of bid'ah

### Weeks 2–4
- Start praying (see the separate "How to Pray" guide)
- Memorize Surah Al-Ikhlas
- Learn the major sins (from al-Dhahabi's list)
- Go to the mosque at least once
- Begin reading Thalathat al-Usul (The Three Fundamental Principles)

### Months 2–3
- Pray all 5 daily prayers
- Learn the full salah in Arabic
- Start reading Quran with translation (10 min/day)
- Build Muslim friendships
- Cut ties with haram environments

### Months 4–6
- Pray in the mosque regularly
- Learn about Ramadan (if approaching)
- Start learning about zakat
- Read Kashf al-Shubuhaat and begin Kitab at-Tawheed
- Begin building a daily dhikr habit

### Long-term
- Learn Arabic (gradually)
- Study seerah (life of the Prophet ﷺ)
- Consider marriage when ready
- Give da'wah to others (this strengthens one's own faith)
