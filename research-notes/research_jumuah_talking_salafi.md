## 1. The Prohibition of Talking During the Khutbah — Hadiths with Exact References and al-Albani's Gradings

### 1a. The Core Hadith: "If you say to your companion 'be quiet' on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah, you have engaged in laghw (vain speech)"

**Sahih al-Bukhari 934:**

> Narrated Abu Huraira: Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) (p.b.u.h) said, "When the Imam is delivering the Khutba, and you ask your companion to keep quiet and listen, then no doubt you have done an evil act."

**Reference:** (Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 934; Book 13, Chapter: "One should keep quiet and listen while the Imam is delivering the Khutba"; al-Albani: Sahih)
**Source URL:** https://sunnah.com/bukhari:934
**Also on abdurrahman.org:** https://abdurrahman.org/2014/09/02/sahih-bukhari-book-013/

---

**Sahih Muslim 851a:**

> Abu Huraira reported Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) as saying: If you (even) ask your companion to be quiet on Friday while the Imam is delivering the sermon, you have in fact talked irrelevance.

**Reference:** (Sahih Muslim, Hadith 851a; Book: "The Book of Prayer - Friday", Chapter: "Listening attentively to the Khutbah on Friday"; al-Albani: Sahih)
**Source URL:** https://sunnah.com/muslim:851a
**Also on hadithunlocked.com:** https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/7/4

**Reference:** (Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1112; Book: "Prayer (Kitab Al-Salat)", Chapter: "Speaking While The Imam Delivers The Khutbah"; **al-Albani: Sahih**)

### 1b. The Hadith of the Donkey Carrying Books

**Mishkat al-Masabih 1397 (from Ahmad):**

> Ibn 'Abbas reported God's Messenger as saying, "If anyone speaks on Friday while the imam is preaching, he is like an ass which carries books, (cf. Al-Qur'an; 62:5) and he who tells him to be quiet is not credited with the Friday prayer." Ahmad transmitted it.

**Reference:** (Mishkat al-Masabih, Hadith 1397; reported by Ahmad)
**Source URL:** https://sunnah.com/mishkat:1397

---

**Bulugh al-Maram (from Ahmad):**

> Narrated Ibn 'Abbas (RA): Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) said: "Whoever speaks on Friday while the Imam is giving the Khutbah (religious talk), he is like a donkey which carries books; and he who tells him to be quiet has no Jumu'ah." [Reported by Ahmad with an acceptable chain of narrators].

**Reference:** (Bulugh al-Maram, Book: "The Book of Prayer", Chapter on Jumu'ah; reported by Ahmad with an acceptable chain)
**Source URL:** https://sunnah.com/bulugh/2/374

---

**Dorar.net (grading of the donkey hadith):**

> خلاصة حكم المحدث: [فيه] مجالد بن سعيد وهو ضعيف
> الراوي: عبدالله بن عباس | المحدث: شعيب الأرناؤوط | المصدر: تخريج زاد المعاد الصفحة أو الرقم: 1/416
> التخريج: أخرجه أحمد (2033)، والبزار (4725)، والطبراني (12/90) (12563) باختلاف يسير

**Translation:** Summary of the hadith scholar's ruling: **[It contains] Majlad bin Sa'id who is weak (da'if)**. Narrator: Abdullah bin Abbas | Grading scholar: Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut | Source: Takhrij Zad al-Ma'ad, page/number: 1/416. Takhrij: Reported by Ahmad (2033), al-Bazzar (4725), al-Tabarani (12/90) (12563) with slight variation.

**Source URL:** https://dorar.net/h/AxKj0q1J

**Note:** The hadith of Ibn 'Abbas about the donkey carrying books is graded **da'if (weak)** by Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut due to Majlad bin Sa'id being in the chain. However, Ibn Hajar stated in Bulugh al-Maram: "There is no fault in its chain" (لا بأس بإسناده). The hadith is also referenced in the Quranic verse: (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:5) — "The likeness of those who were entrusted with the Taurat but did not carry it is like that of a donkey which carries huge burdens of books."

### 1c. The Hadith of Abu al-Darda' and Ubayy ibn Ka'b

**Sunan Ibn Majah 1111:**

> 'Ata' bin Yasar narrated from Ubayy bin Ka'b: "The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) recited Tabarak [Al-Mulk (67)] one Friday, while he was standing and reminding us of the Days of Allah (i.e., preaching to us). Abu Darda' or Abu Dharr raised an eyebrow at me and said: 'When was this Surah revealed? For I have not heard it before now.' He (Ubayy) gestured to him that he should remain silent. When they finished, he said: 'I asked you when this Surah was revealed and you did not answer me.' Ubayy said: 'You have gained nothing from your prayer today except the idle talk that you engaged in.' He went to the Prophet (ﷺ) and told him about that, and what Ubayy had said to him. The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) said: 'Ubayy spoke the truth.'"

**Reference:** (Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1111; Book: "Establishing the Prayer and the Sunnah Regarding Them")
**Source URL:** https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:1111

### 1d. The Hadith of Three Types of People Who Attend Jumu'ah

**Sunan Abi Dawud 1113:**

> Narrated Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-'As: The Prophet (ﷺ) said: Three types of people attend Friday prayer; One is present in a frivolous way and that is all he gets from it; another comes with a supplication, Allah may grant or refuse his request as He wishes; another is present silently and quietly without stepping over a Muslim or annoying anyone, and that is an atonement for his sins till the next Friday and three days more, the reason being that Allah, the Exalted, says: "He who does a good deed will have ten times as much" (vi.160).

**Reference:** (Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1113; Book: "Prayer (Kitab Al-Salat)", Chapter: "Speaking While The Imam Delivers The Khutbah")
**Source URL:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1113

### 1e. The Hadith About Touching Pebbles

**Riyad al-Salihin 1148 (from Muslim):**

> Abu Hurairah (May Allah be pleased with him) reported: The Messenger of Allah (sallallaahu 'alayhi wa sallam) said, "If anyone performs Wudu' properly, then comes to the Friday prayer, listens to the Khutbah (religious talk) attentively and keeps silent, his (minor) sins between that Friday and the following Friday will be forgiven, with the addition of three more days; but he who touches pebbles has caused an interruption."

**Reference:** (Sahih Muslim; cited in Riyad al-Salihin, Hadith 1148)

## 2. The "Be Quiet" (Laghw) Hadith — Exact Reference and Grading

**The primary hadith:**

> "If you say to your companion 'be quiet' on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah, you have engaged in laghw (vain speech)."

**Arabic text:**
> إِذَا قُلْتَ لِصَاحِبِكَ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ أَنْصِتْ وَالإِمَامُ يَخْطُبُ فَقَدْ لَغَوْتَ

**Primary references:**
- (Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 934; al-Albani: Sahih) — https://sunnah.com/bukhari:934
- (Sahih Muslim, Hadith 851a; al-Albani: Sahih) — https://sunnah.com/muslim:851a
- (Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1112; **al-Albani: Sahih**) — https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1112
- (Sunan an-Nasa'i, Hadith 1401) — https://sunnah.com/nasai:1401
- (Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1110) — https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:1110
- (Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Hadith 512) — referenced in dorar.net

**Al-Albani's grading (from dorar.net):**
> المحدث: الألباني | المصدر: صحيح الجامع | الصفحة أو الرقم: 6432
> خلاصة حكم المحدث: **صحيح** (Sahih)

**Source URL:** https://dorar.net/h/d7AspBRF

**Variant in Sahih Muslim 851d:**
> The same hadith has been narrated by Abu Huraira, but instead of the word laghauta the word laghita has been used. Abu Zinad (one of the narrators) says that laghita is the dialect of Abu Huraira, whereas it is laghauta.

**Source URL:** https://sunnah.com/muslim:851d

## 3. WHEN the Prohibition Begins — Does It Start When the Imam Ascends the Minbar? When the Adhan Is Called? When the Imam Begins Speaking?

### 3a. The View That the Prohibition Begins When the Imam Begins Speaking (the Khutbah Itself)

**Dorar.net (al-Mawsu'ah al-Fiqhiyyah) — citing the evidence:**

The Dorar.net Fiqh Encyclopedia discusses when the prohibition of talking begins:

> وَجْهُ الدَّلالَةِ: أنَّ قوله في الحديثِ: ((والإمامُ يَخطُب)) جملةٌ حاليَّة، أي: حال كون الإمام يخطب؛ فتُخرِجُ ما قَبلَ خُطبتِه من حِينِ خروجِه، وما بَعدَه إلى أنْ يَشرَع في الخُطبةِ ((شرح الزرقاني على الموطأ)) (1/381).

**Translation:** "The point of evidence: His statement in the hadith 'while the imam is delivering the khutbah' is a circumstantial clause (jumla haaliyya), meaning: while the imam is actually delivering the khutbah. This excludes what is before his khutbah from the time of his coming out, and what is after it until he begins the khutbah. (Sharh al-Zurqani 'ala al-Muwatta', 1/381)"

**Source URL:** https://dorar.net/feqhia/1641/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B7%D9%84%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%86%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AA

---

**Dorar.net — citing the practice during 'Umar's time:**

> ما رواه ثَعلبةُ بنُ أبي مالكٍ القُرَظيِّ، أنَّهم كانوا في زَمانِ عُمرَ بن الخَطَّابِ يُصلُّونَ يومَ الجُمُعةِ حتى يَخرُجَ عُمرُ، فإذا خرَجَ عُمرُ وجَلَس على المنبرِ وأَذَّن المؤذِّنون (قال ثعلبة): جَلَسْنا نَتحدَّثُ، فإذا سكتَ المؤذِّنونَ وقامَ عُمرُ يَخطُبُ أَنْصتْنا، فلمْ يَتكلَّمْ منَّا أحدٌ

**Translation:** "What was reported by Tha'labah bin Abi Malik al-Qurazi: They used to pray on the day of Jumu'ah during the time of 'Umar bin al-Khattab until 'Umar came out. When 'Umar came out and sat on the minbar and the mu'adhdhins called the adhan, (Tha'labah said): 'We sat talking. When the mu'adhdhins fell silent and 'Umar stood to deliver the khutbah, we fell silent, and none of us spoke.'"

**Grading:** Authenticated by al-Nawawi in al-Khulasah (2/808); al-Dhahabi said in al-Muhadhdhab (3/1123): "It contains Tha'labah, used as evidence by al-Bukhari"; al-'Ayni authenticated its chain in Nukhb al-Afkar (6/48); **al-Albani said in Tamam al-Minnah (p. 399): "It has a follow-up narration (mutaba'ah) with a sahih chain."**

### 3b. Ibn 'Uthaymeen's View — Silence Is Obligatory Only While the Imam Is Giving the Khutbah

**Shaykh Ibn 'Uthaymeen (from ash-Sharh al-Mumti', 5/109):**

> "The silence is obligatory in the condition the Imaam is giving the khutbah. So what is before the khutbah, even after the Imaam comes, it is not obligatory to be silent. And between the two khutbahs, it is not obligatory to be silent."

**Reference:** (Shaykh Muhammad bin Salih al-'Uthaymeen, ash-Sharh al-Mumti' 'ala Zad al-Mustaqni', vol. 5, p. 109)
**Source:** Cited via multiple Salafi references; the citation appears in abdurrahman.org's Friday section and is widely circulated among Salafi scholars.

**Also from Ibn 'Uthaymeen (ash-Sharh al-Mumti', 5/109):**

> "It is haraam to give salaams during the Friday khutbah, so it is not permissible for one who enters the masjid whilst the imam is delivering the khutbah to give salaams, and it is also haraam to return the greeting."

**Reference:** (Shaykh Muhammad bin Salih al-'Uthaymeen, ash-Sharh al-Mumti', 5/109)

### 3c. The Practice of the Companions — Speaking Until the Imam Begins the Khutbah

**Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah 5399 (from the search results, cited via tohed.com — NOT a primary Salafi source, but the hadith is referenced in dorar.net):**

> Hazrat Ibrahim says that I asked Hazrat Alqamah, "When does speaking become disliked on the day of Jumu'ah?" He replied, "When the Imam ascends the pulpit, when the Imam delivers the sermon, and when the Imam speaks."

**Note:** This is from the Musannaf of Ibn Abi Shaybah, which is referenced in dorar.net's Fiqh encyclopedia. The full reference is Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah, Hadith 5399.

### 3d. Between the Two Khutbahs

**Dorar.net (al-Mawsu'ah al-Fiqhiyyah):**

> يُباحُ الكلامُ بين الخُطبتينِ لا سيَّما إذا دعتْ إليه الحاجةُ، وهو مذهبُ الشافعيَّة ((مغني المحتاج)) للشربيني (1/287)، ((نهاية المحتاج)) للرملي (2/320).، والحَنابِلَةِ على الصَّحيحِ ((الإنصاف)) للمرداوي (2/292)، ((كشاف القناع)) للبهوتي (2/47).

**Translation:** "It is permissible to speak between the two khutbahs, especially if there is a need for it. This is the position of the Shafi'is (Mughni al-Muhtaj by al-Shirbini, 1/287; Nihayat al-Muhtaj by al-Ramli, 2/320) and the Hanbalis on the correct view (Al-Insaf by al-Mardawi, 2/292; Kashshaf al-Qina' by al-Buhuti, 2/47)."

## 4. Exceptions — Can You Tell Someone to Be Quiet? Can You Speak if You Didn't Hear? What About Someone Who Arrives Late? What About the Imam Himself?

### 4a. Can You Tell Someone to Be Quiet?

**No.** Even telling someone to be quiet ("ansit") is considered laghw (vain speech) during the khutbah, as established by the primary hadith (Sahih al-Bukhari 934, Sahih Muslim 851a).

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa):**

> السنة للمؤمن الإنصات أثناء الخطبة ينصت لسماع الخطيب ولا يتكلم، ولهذا قال ﷺ: إذا قلت لصاحبك: أنصت يوم الجمعة والإمام يخطب فقد لغوت، فلا يجوز له أن يتكلم ولا يقول: أنصت يا فلان، ولا اسكت يا فلان، بل ينصت، لكن يشير إشارة، لا مانع من الإشارة للمتكلم...

**Translation:** "The Sunnah for the believer is to remain silent during the khutbah — he remains silent to listen to the khatib and does not speak. This is why he (ﷺ) said: 'If you say to your companion: "Be quiet" on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah, you have engaged in laghw.' So it is not permissible for him to speak, nor to say 'Be quiet, so-and-so' or 'Silence, so-and-so.' Rather, he remains silent, but he may gesture — there is no harm in gesturing to the one speaking..."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم التأمين والصلاة على النبي ﷺ أثناء الخطبة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/categories/fiqhi/54/fatwa?page=13

---

### 4b. Speaking to the Imam (Khatib) During the Khutbah — Permissible

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa):**

> الرد على الإمام في الخطبة أو سؤاله عن شيء في الخطبة أو طلبه شيئاً في الخطبة ليس من اللغو في الخطبة، وليس بممنوع، فقد جاء رجل إلى النبي ﷺ وهو يخطب في يوم الجمعة، فقال: يا رسول الله! هلكت الأموال، وانقطعت السبل، فادع الله يغيثنا، فأجابه النبي ﷺ واستغاث ربه ولم ينكر عليه، فإذا خاطب رجل الإمام -الخطيب- قال له: يا خطيب! يا فلان! ادع الله لنا، أو استغث لنا، أو فتح عليه غلطاً وقع له، فلا حرج عليه في ذلك، وليس بداخل في هذا وليس بممنوع.

**Translation:** "Responding to the imam during the khutbah, or asking him about something during the khutbah, or requesting something from him during the khutbah is not considered laghw (vain speech) in the khutbah, and is not prohibited. A man came to the Prophet (ﷺ) while he was delivering the khutbah on Friday and said: 'O Messenger of Allah! Wealth has perished and the roads have been cut off, so call upon Allah to give us rain.' The Prophet (ﷺ) responded to him and sought rain from his Lord, and did not rebuke him. So if a person addresses the imam — the khatib — saying to him: 'O khatib! O so-and-so! Pray for us,' or 'Seek rain for us,' or points out an error he made, there is no harm in that, and it is not included in the prohibition and is not forbidden."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم الكلام مع الخطيب أثناء الخطبة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/8039/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B7%D9%8A%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A9

---

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa) — another fatwa on the same topic:**

> إذا دعت الحاجة لا بأس، إذا دعت الحاجة أن يكلم الخطيب يقول: ادع الله استغث لنا، ادع الله لنا إنا في جدب في حاجة، أو يسأله عن شيء ينفع الناس؛ فلا بأس، فقد تقدم بعض الناس إلى النبي ﷺ وهو يخطب الجمعة فقال: "يا رسول الله! هلكت الأموال، وانقطعت السبل، فادع الله أن يغيثنا، فاستغاث، عليه الصلاة والسلام"

**Translation:** "If there is a need, there is no harm. If there is a need for someone to speak to the khatib, saying: 'Call upon Allah, seek rain for us, pray for us, we are in drought and in need,' or he asks him about something that benefits people — there is no harm. A person came forward to the Prophet (ﷺ) while he was delivering the Jumu'ah khutbah and said: 'O Messenger of Allah! Wealth has perished and the roads have been cut off, so call upon Allah to give us rain.' So he sought rain, upon him be prayers and peace."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم الكلام مع الخطيب أثناء خطبة الجمعة")

### 4c. The Imam Himself Speaking

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa):**

> لا يجوز الكلام والإمام يخطب مع الناس، لا مع العاطس ولا مع غيره، الواجب الإنصات لسماع الخطيب، إلا مع الخطيب لا بأس، إذا أراد أن يسأل الخطيب عن شيء، أو ينكر عليه شيئاً أخطأ فيه يجب إنكاره، فالكلام مع الخطيب لا بأس، والخطيب لا بأس يتكلم هو؛ لأن هذا من جنس خطبته، أما الناس فيما بينهم فلا.

**Translation:** "It is not permissible to speak while the imam is delivering the khutbah to the people — neither to someone who sneezes nor to anyone else. The obligation is to remain silent to listen to the khatib, except speaking to the khatib — there is no harm. If one wants to ask the khatib about something, or to object to something he erred in that must be objected to — speaking to the khatib is fine. And the khatib himself — there is no harm in him speaking, because this is part of his khutbah. As for the people among themselves, no."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/5415/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A9

### 4d. Someone Who Arrives Late — Praying Tahiyyat al-Masjid

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (abdurrahman.org):**

> "The Sunnah upon entering the masjid (mosque) is to pray two rak'ahs of tahyatul masjid (a Prayer for greeting the mosque), even if the Imaam is delivering the Friday khutbah, due to the sayings of the Prophet sallallaahu 'alayhi wa sallam: 'When anyone of you enters the masjid, then let him not sit until he prays two rak'ahs.' Recorded by al-Bukhaaree (1/293) and Muslim (2/155). Likewise Muslim relates in his Saheeh (3/1415), from Jaabir radiallaahu 'anhu, that the Prophet sallallaahu 'alayhi wa sallam said: 'When any of you comes on the day of Jumu'ah (Friday), and the Imaam is delivering the khutbah, then let him pray two rak'ahs and let him make them short.' So this is a clear and authentic text concerning this issue. It is not permissible for anyone to oppose it."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, cited on abdurrahman.org)

### 4e. Speaking if You Didn't Hear the Khutbah

**Dorar.net (al-Mawsu'ah al-Fiqhiyyah) — citing the majority view:**

The majority of scholars hold that the obligation to remain silent applies whether or not the person can actually hear the khutbah. This is based on the generality of the hadith. The hadith of the donkey carrying books states: "Whoever speaks in Jumu'ah while the imam is delivering the khutbah is like a donkey who is carrying books" — this applies regardless of whether one can hear.

## 5. The Ruling on Responding to Salaam, Sneezing, or Saying "Ameen" During the Khutbah

### 5a. Responding to Salaam During the Khutbah — By Gesture Only, Not Verbally

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa):**

> السلام وهو في وقت الخطبة غير مشروع، بل يصلي التحية ويجلس ولا يسلم على أحد، حتى ينتهي الخطيب، وإذا سلم عليك لا ترد إلا بالإشارة كما لو سلم عليك في الصلاة ترد بالإشارة ويكفي، وليس له أن يلح في طلب السلام، وإذا مد يده تمد يدك ولا تتكلم بشيء حتى ينتهي الخطيب، فإذا سكت الخطيب ترد عليه والحمد لله.

**Translation:** "Giving salaam while the imam is delivering the khutbah is not prescribed. Rather, one should pray the tahiyyah and sit, and not give salaam to anyone until the khatib finishes. If someone gives you salaam, do not respond except with a gesture, just as if someone gives you salaam while you are praying — you respond with a gesture and that suffices. He should not insist on seeking the salaam. If he extends his hand, you extend your hand and do not speak anything until the khatib finishes. When the khatib falls silent, you may respond to him, and all praise is for Allah."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم رد السلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/8754/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%B1%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A9

---

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa) — another fatwa:**

> ترد بالإشارة، إذا كان الإمام يخطب، مثل الذي يصلي، ترد بالإشارة، تشير بيدك ردًا عليه، مثل ما لو سلم عليك وأنت تصلي، ترد بالإشارة، لأنك مأمور بالإنصات.

**Translation:** "Respond with a gesture. If the imam is delivering the khutbah, it is like the one who is praying — you respond with a gesture, pointing with your hand in response, just as if someone gives you salaam while you are praying — you respond with a gesture, because you are commanded to remain silent."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "كيفية رد السلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة")
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/12427/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B1%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A9

**The Lajnah al-Da'imah (Permanent Committee) — Shaykh Bin Baz & Shaykh bin Ghudayan (fatwaislam.com):**

> He should shake him by the hand and not speak, then he should return the salaam (verbally) to him after the first khutbah has ceased. This is if he gave you salaam during the course of the first khutbah. If however he gave you salaam during the course of the Imaam delivering the second khutbah then you should give him the reply to his salaam after the Imaam has finished from the second khutbah.

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz and Shaykh 'Abdullah bin Ghudayan, Fataawa al-Lajnah al-Da'imah, vol. 8, p. 246, question 2 of fatwa number 3774)

### 5b. Sneezing During the Khutbah — Do Not Say "Yarhamuk Allah"

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa):**

> حتى ولو عطس وحمد الله، لا تقول: يرحمك الله، كما لا تقوله في الصلاة، إذا عطس وهو يصلي وحمد الله ما يقال: يرحمك الله، هكذا في الخطبة المستمع لها كالمصلي فلا يتكلم، ولا يرد السلام، ولا يبدأ بالسلام، ولا يشمت العاطس، هذا هو الواجب عليه

**Translation:** "Even if someone sneezes and praises Allah, do not say 'May Allah have mercy on you' (Yarhamuk Allah), just as you do not say it in prayer — if someone sneezes while praying and praises Allah, 'Yarhamuk Allah' is not said. Likewise in the khutbah, the one listening to it is like the one praying — he does not speak, does not return the salaam, does not initiate the salaam, and does not respond to the one who sneezes (tashmit). This is what is obligatory upon him."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/5415/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A9

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa) — additional detail:**

> وإذا عطس؛ يحمد الله في نفسه، ولا يرفع صوته.

**Translation:** "And if he sneezes, he praises Allah silently, and does not raise his voice."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم رد السلام أثناء الخطبة")

### 5c. Saying "Ameen" During the Khutbah — Permissible Quietly

**Shaykh Ibn 'Uthaymeen (fatwaislam.com):**

> It is not an innovation to say Aameen when the Khatib supplicates during the Khuthah. If he begins to supplicate for the Muslims, then it is a preferred act to say Aameen to his supplication, but this should not be done in unison, nor in a loud voice. Rather, every person should recite individually and in a low voice, so that there is no annoyance or shouting. Each person should only say Aameen to the supplication of the Khatib quietly and apart from the others.

**Reference:** (Shaykh Muhammad bin Salih al-'Uthaymeen, Fataawa Islamiyyah, DARUSSALAM, vol. 2, p. 477)
**Source URL:** https://www.fatwaislam.com/fis/index.cfm?ID=781&scn=fd

---

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (fatwaislam.com) — on saying Ameen and raising hands:**

> It is not prescribed for the Imam or the worshippers to raise their hands during the Friday Khutbah, because the Messenger of Allaah (sallallaahu alaihi wa sallam) did not do so, and neither did his righteous successors, may Allaah be pleased with them. But if the Imam is calling for rain in the Friday Khutbah, it is prescribed for him and the worshippers to raise their hands, because when the Prophet (sallallaahu alaihi wa sallam) prayed for rain, he raised his hands and the people raised their hands. And Allaah, the Most Glorified says: **Indeed, in the Messenger of Allaah (Muhammad (sallallaahu alaihi wa sallam)) you have a good example to follow**. [1] As for saying Aameen to the Imams supplication during the Khutbah, I know of no objection to it, as long as the voice is not raised. And through Allaah is attainted success.

**[1]** (Quran, Surah al-Ahzab [33]:21)

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatawa Islamiyah, vol. 2, p. 476)
**Source URL:** https://www.fatwaislam.com/fis/index.cfm?scn=fd&ID=1237

### 5d. Sending Salat (Blessings) on the Prophet ﷺ During the Khutbah — Permissible Silently

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa):**

> لا بأس بالصلاة على النبي ﷺ من غير جهر يشوش على من حولك، إذا صلى الإمام .. ذكر النبي ﷺ تصلي عليه؛ لقوله ﷺ: رغم أنف امرئ ذكرت عنده فلم يصل علي فإذا سمعت الصلاة على النبي ﷺ تصلي عليه، بينك وبين نفسك من غير جهر، وهكذا غيرك؛ للحديث المذكور، وغيره من الأحاديث.

**Translation:** "There is no harm in sending salat upon the Prophet (ﷺ) without raising one's voice in a way that disturbs those around you. When the imam... mentions the Prophet (ﷺ), you send salat upon him, due to his (ﷺ) saying: 'May his nose be rubbed in dust, a man in whose presence I am mentioned and he does not send salat upon me.' So when you hear the salat upon the Prophet (ﷺ), you send salat upon him, between yourself and yourself, without raising your voice. Likewise others, due the mentioned hadith and other hadiths."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم الصلاة على النبي ﷺ أثناء الخطبة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/11922/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%A8%D9%8A-%EF%B7%BA-%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A9

---

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa) — from Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol. 12:**

> تشرع الصلاة على النبي ﷺ إذا مر ذكره عليه الصلاة والسلام في خطب الجمعة والعيد ومجالس الذكر؛ لقوله ﷺ: رغم أنف رجل ذكرت عنده فلم يصل علي

**Translation:** "It is prescribed to send salat upon the Prophet (ﷺ) when his mention passes, upon him be prayers and peace, in the khutbahs of Jumu'ah and Eid and gatherings of dhikr, due to his (ﷺ) saying: 'May his nose be rubbed in dust, a man in whose presence I am mentioned and he does not send salat upon me.'"

**Hadith reference:** Reported by al-Tirmidhi in (al-Da'wat) number 3468, and Ahmad in (Baqi Musnad al-Mukthirin) number 7139.
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/majmou-fatawa/12?page=15

### 5e. Ibn Baz — Ameen and Salat on the Prophet Are Not Laghw

**Shaykh Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa):**

> ليس الدعاء وليس الصلاة على النبي ﷺ من اللغو، ولكن يكون سرًا بينك وبين نفسك لا ترفع صوتك، فإذا سمعت شيء ما يوجب الدعاء ودعوت في حال السر بينك وبين نفسك... على الدعاء والصلاة على النبي ﷺ... ولكن يكون ذلك بصوت...

**Translation:** "Supplication (du'a) and sending salat upon the Prophet (ﷺ) are not laghw (vain speech), but it should be secret, between you and yourself, not raising your voice. If you hear something that warrants supplication, you supplicate silently, between you and yourself... regarding the supplication and sending salat upon the Prophet (ﷺ)... but that should be with a [low] voice..."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, from the Friday Prayer section)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/categories/fiqhi/54/fatwa?page=13

## 6. Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn Baz, al-Albani, al-Uthaymeen, al-Fawzan on This Topic

### 6a. Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah

**Ibn Taymiyyah on responding to salaam and sneezing during prayer (relevant by analogy to khutbah):**

From abdurrahman.org (Dr. Saleh as-Saleh's article on rulings of the adhan):

> Shaykh al-Islaam ibn Taymiyyah (rahimahullaah) held another opinion; he maintained that the person in salaah may respond because this response is remembrance whose cause existed in salaah. Similarly, he said it is permissible to say "al-hamdullillaah" if you sneeze or return the salaam because the Prophet (صَلَّى اللّٰهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّم) responded to that while in salaah. However, when it comes to following the mu'adhdhin, consideration is required because the response to the adhaan is prolonged and is far longer than returning the salaam, which is simply responded to with the hand, finger, or head. Thus, it is precautionary not to respond.

**Reference:** (Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, cited by Dr. Saleh as-Saleh on abdurrahman.org)

### 6b. Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah

**Ibn al-Qayyim on the purpose of the Jumu'ah khutbah (abdurrahman.org):**

> Shamsuddeen Muhammad bin Abee Bakr Ibn Qayyim aj-Jawzeeyah (d.751 A.H.) -Rahimullaah- said: 'On the day of Jumu'ah' there is a Khutbah (sermon) the objective of which is to glorify Allaah and regard Him as Magnificent, and testify to singling out Allaah Alone as the Creator, and testify that His Messenger -sallAllaahu alayhi wa sallam- brought the message. ### 6c. Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz

**Shaykh Ibn Baz — comprehensive fatwa on talking during the khutbah (binbaz.org.sa):**

> لا يجوز الكلام والإمام يخطب مع الناس، لا مع العاطس ولا مع غيره، الواجب الإنصات لسماع الخطيب، إلا مع الخطيب لا بأس... هكذا في الخطبة المستمع لها كالمصلي فلا يتكلم، ولا يرد السلام، ولا يبدأ بالسلام، ولا يشمت العاطس، هذا هو الواجب عليه؛ لقول النبي ﷺ: إذا قلت لصاحبك: (أنصت) يوم الجمعة والإمام يخطب فقد لغوت مع أنه أمره بالمعروف، سماه لاغياً وهو آمر بالمعروف. وفي الحديث الآخر: من تكلم يوم الجمعة والإمام يخطب فهو كمثل الحمار يحمل أسفاراً، والذي يقول له: أنصت، ليست له جمعة، هذا يدل على أن الجمعة تلغو ويفوته ثوابها بسبب كلامه حال الخطبة، ولو أن كلامه في أمر طيب، في أمر بمعروف، أو نهي عن منكر، أو تشميت عاطس، فالواجب ترك ذلك، كما يجب ترك ذلك مع المصلي، فهكذا مع المستمع للخطبة

**Translation:** "It is not permissible to speak while the imam is delivering the khutbah to the people — neither to someone who sneezes nor to anyone else. The obligation is to remain silent to listen to the khatib, except speaking to the khatib — there is no harm... Likewise in the khutbah, the one listening to it is like the one praying — he does not speak, does not return the salaam, does not initiate the salaam, and does not respond to the one who sneezes. This is what is obligatory upon him, due to the saying of the Prophet (ﷺ): 'If you say to your companion: "Be quiet" on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah, you have engaged in laghw' — even though he was enjoining good, he called him one who engages in vain speech (laghw), while he was one who enjoins good. And in the other hadith: 'Whoever speaks on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah is like a donkey carrying books, and the one who says to him "be quiet" has no Jumu'ah.' This indicates that the Jumu'ah is nullified and its reward is lost due to his speaking during the khutbah, even if his speech is about something good — enjoining good, forbidding evil, or responding to a sneeze. The obligation is to leave that, just as it is obligatory to leave that with the one praying, likewise with the one listening to the khutbah."

**Reference:** (Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Bin Baz, Fatwa: "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)
**Source URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/5415/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A9

## 7. The Difference Between the First and Second Adhan of Jumu'ah

### 7a. Historical Background — From Sahih al-Bukhari (abdurrahman.org)

**Sahih al-Bukhari (Book 13: Friday Prayer):**

> The person who increased the number of Adhans for the Jumua prayers to three was Uthman bin Affan and it was when the number of the (Muslim) people of Medina had increased. In the life-time of the Prophet there was only one Muadh-dhin and the Adhan used to be pronounced only after the Imam had taken his seat (i.e. on the pulpit).

> 'Uthman bin 'Affan introduced the second Adhan on Fridays when the number of the people in the mosque increased. Previously the Adhan on Fridays used to be pronounced only after the Imam had taken his seat (on the pulpit).

> I heard As-Saib bin Yazid, saying, "In the life-time of Allah's Apostle, and Abu Bakr and Umar, the Adhan for the Jumua prayer used to be pronounced after the Imam had taken his seat on the pulpit. But when the people increased in number during the caliphate of 'Uthman, he introduced a third Adhan (on Friday for the Jumua prayer) and it was pronounced at Az-Zaura' and that new state of affairs remained so in the succeeding years."

**Reference:** (Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 13: Friday Prayer; Volume 2, Book 13)
**Source URL:** https://abdurrahman.org/2014/09/02/sahih-bukhari-book-013/

**Note on terminology:** What al-Bukhari calls the "third adhan" (introduced by 'Uthman) is what is commonly referred to as the "first adhan" in modern times. In the time of the Prophet (ﷺ), Abu Bakr, and 'Umar, there was only one adhan, which was called when the imam sat on the minbar. 'Uthman added an earlier adhan (at Az-Zaura', in the marketplace) to alert the people when the population of Madinah increased. In modern practice, the adhan that is called when the imam sits on the minbar is called the "second adhan" (or the adhan of the khutbah), and the earlier one introduced by 'Uthman is called the "first adhan."

### 7c. The Prohibition of Trade Begins After the Second Adhan (the Adhan of the Khutbah)

**Shaykh al-Fawzan (abdurrahman.org):**

The verse (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:9-10) was cited by Shaykh al-Fawzan to establish that trade becomes impermissible when the call is proclaimed for the prayer on Friday. This refers to the adhan that is called when the imam is present and about to deliver the khutbah (the second adhan in modern terminology).

> "O you who believe (Muslims)! When the call is proclaimed for the Salat (prayer) on the day of Friday (Jumu'ah prayer), come to the remembrance of Allah [Jumu'ah religious talk (Khutbah) and Salat (prayer)] and leave off business (and every other thing), that is better for you if you did but know!" ### 7d. The Prohibition of Talking Does NOT Begin with the First Adhan

Based on the evidence cited above:

1. **The hadith** specifies: "while the imam is delivering the khutbah" (والإمام يخطب) — this is a circumstantial clause indicating the prohibition applies during the actual khutbah, not before it. (See Section 3a, Sharh al-Zurqani 'ala al-Muwatta', 1/381)

2. **The practice during 'Umar's time** confirms that the Companions would talk until the mu'adhdhins finished and 'Umar stood to deliver the khutbah. (See Section 3a, authenticated by al-Albani in Tamam al-Minnah, p. 399)

3. **Ibn 'Uthaymeen** explicitly states: "So what is before the khutbah, even after the Imaam comes, it is not obligatory to be silent." (ash-Sharh al-Mumti', 5/109)

4. **The first adhan** (introduced by 'Uthman) is an alert for the people to prepare, not the beginning of the prohibition of speech. The prohibition of trade begins with the call to the prayer (the second adhan), and the prohibition of talking begins when the imam starts delivering the khutbah.
