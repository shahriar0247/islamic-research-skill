# Research: Praying Tahajjud (Nafl) Behind Someone Praying Isha (Fard) — Salafi Scholar Views

**Topic:** اختلاف نية الإمام والمأموم / صلاة المفترض خلف المتنفل والعكس
**Question:** Can a person pray 2 rak'ah Tahajjud (voluntary night prayer) behind/beside someone who is praying the 4 rak'ah obligatory Isha prayer? (Imam = Isha fard, follower = Tahajjud nafl)

**Date of research:** 2025
**Sources restricted to:** Salafi-approved websites (islamqa.info, binbaz.org.sa, islamweb.net, troid.org, abdurrahman.org, salafipublications.com, learnaboutislam.co.uk, fatwaislam.com, alalbani.info) and primary classical works of Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim.

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The question involves TWO directions of "different intention" prayer:

1. **Fard behind Nafl** (المفترض خلف المتنفل): A follower praying an OBLIGATORY prayer behind an imam praying a VOLUNTARY prayer. Example: praying Isha (fard) behind someone praying Taraweeh/Tahajjud (nafl).
2. **Nafl behind Fard** (المتنفل خلف المفترض): A follower praying a VOLUNTARY prayer behind an imam praying an OBLIGATORY prayer. Example: praying Tahajjud (nafl) behind someone praying Isha (fard). **<-- THIS IS THE QUESTIONER'S SPECIFIC CASE.**

The **specific case in the question** (Tahajjud behind Isha) falls under category #2: **nafl behind fard**.

**Ruling of the Salafi scholars:** Both directions are **PERMISSIBLE (جائز)** according to the majority of the Salafi scholars cited below (Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, al-Fawzan, Ibn Taymiyyah). They hold that **اختلاف نية الإمام والمأموم** (difference of intention between imam and follower) does **NOT** invalidate the prayer, in either direction. The primary evidence is the hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal, the hadith of the fear prayer (صلاة الخوف), and the principle that "the imam is appointed to be followed" refers to outward actions, not internal intentions.

**IMPORTANT NUANCE on the specific case (Tahajjud behind Isha):** The scholars explicitly addressed the *reverse* case (fard behind nafl) very frequently — especially the Ramadan scenario of praying Isha behind someone praying Taraweeh. The case of **nafl behind fard** (Tahajjud behind Isha) is less frequently discussed in the specific fatwas, but the general principle stated by these scholars — that "اختلاف نية الإمام والمأموم لا يضر" (difference of intention does not harm) — covers BOTH directions. Ibn Taymiyyah explicitly states both directions are proven. See the detailed analysis below.

## 1. SHAYKH IBN BAZ (بن باز)

### Source: binbaz.org.sa — Fatwa #4758: "صلاة المفترض خلف المتنفل"

**Verbatim quote (Arabic):**

> **س:** ما رأي سماحتكم في صلاة المفترض خلف المتنفل؟
>
> **ج:** لا حرج في صلاة المفترض خلف المتنفل؛ لأنه قد ثبت عن النبي ﷺ في بعض أنواع صلاة الخوف أنه صلى بطائفة ركعتين ثم سلم ثم صلى بطائفة أخرى ركعتين ثم سلم، فكانت الأولى له فريضة والثانية نافلة، أما المصلون خلفه فهم مفترضون. وثبت أيضا في الصحيحين عن معاذ بن جبل أنه كان يصلي مع النبي ﷺ صلاة العشاء ثم يرجع إلى قومه فيصلي بهم تلك الصلاة فهي له نافلة ولهم فريضة، ومثل ذلك لو حضر إنسان في رمضان وهم يصلون التراويح وهو لم يصل فريضة العشاء فإنه يصلي معهم صلاة العشاء ليحصل له فضل الجماعة فإذا سلم الإمام قام وأتم صلاته.

**Translation:** "There is no harm in the obligatory prayer behind one praying voluntary prayer; because it is established from the Prophet ﷺ in some types of the fear prayer that he prayed with one group two rak'ahs then gave salam, then prayed with another group two rak'ahs then gave salam — the first was obligatory for him and the second was voluntary, while those praying behind him were praying obligatory. And it is also established in the two Sahihs [Bukhari & Muslim] from Mu'adh ibn Jabal that he used to pray Isha with the Prophet ﷺ then return to his people and lead them in that same prayer — it was voluntary (nafl) for him and obligatory (fard) for them. And likewise if someone comes in Ramadan while they are praying Taraweeh and he has not prayed the obligatory Isha, he prays Isha with them to attain the virtue of congregation, then when the imam gives salam he stands and completes his prayer."

- **Scholar:** Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn 'Abdullah ibn Baz
- **Book/Reference:** Majmu' Fatawa wa Maqalat al-Shaykh Ibn Baz, 12/179
- **Published in:** Jareedat al-Bilad, issue 10936, dated 14/1/1415 AH
- **URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/4758/%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%81%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%B6-%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%81%D9%84

### Source: binbaz.org.sa — Fatwa #5949: "جواز صلاة المفترض خلف المتنفل"

**Verbatim quote (Arabic):**

> إذا أحرم الإنسان مع الناس في ليالي رمضان يظنهم في الفريضة ثم ظهر أنهم في التراويح؛ فإنه يصلي معهم يستمر، فإن سلم الإمام في التراويح يقوم ويكمل صلاته ولا بأس؛ لأنه على الصحيح يجوز أن يصلي المفترض خلف المتنفل، وقد صلى معاذ بأصحابه العشاء وهو متنفل وهم فارضون، كان يصلي مع النبي ﷺ العشاء فرضه ثم يأتي ويصلي بأصحابه العشاء وهو متنفل وهم مفترضون، والنبي ﷺ صلى بأصحابه صلاة الخوف الصلاة الأولى فرضاً وصلى بالطائفة الثانية وهو متنفل عليه الصلاة والسلام وهم مفترضون فلا حرج.

**Translation:** "If a person enters ihram with the people in Ramadan nights thinking they are in the obligatory prayer, then it appears they are in Taraweeh — he prays with them and continues. When the imam gives salam in Taraweeh, he stands and completes his prayer, and there is no harm; because the correct view is that it is permissible to pray obligatory behind one praying voluntary. Mu'adh prayed Isha with his companions while he was praying voluntary (mutanaffil) and they were praying obligatory (muftaridun) — he used to pray Isha as his obligation with the Prophet ﷺ then come and pray Isha with his companions while he was voluntary and they were obligatory. And the Prophet ﷺ prayed the fear prayer — the first prayer as obligatory and prayed with the second group while he was voluntary, peace be upon him, and they were obligatory. So there is no harm."

- **Scholar:** Shaykh Ibn Baz
- **URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/5949/%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B2-%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%81%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%B6-%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%81%D9%84

### Source: binbaz.org.sa — Fatwa #15611: "جواز صلاة الفريضة خلف المتنفل"

**Verbatim quote (Arabic):**

> **الجواب:** نعم لا حرج في ذلك، لو أن إنسانًا لم يصل فريضته، وجاء إلى إنسان يصلي نافلة، وصلى معه؛ صحت، كان معاذ يصلي بأصحابه العشاء نفلًا؛ لأنه قد صلاها مع النبي ﷺ وهم مفترضون؛ ولأنه ﷺ في بعض أنواع صلاة الخوف صلى بطائفة ركعتين فرضهم، ثم صلى بطائفة أخرى ركعتين نفلًا له، ولهم فرض، فلا حرج.

**Translation:** "Yes, there is no harm in that. If a person has not prayed his obligatory prayer, and comes to someone praying a voluntary prayer, and prays with him — it is valid. Mu'adh used to lead his companions in Isha as a voluntary prayer (nafl) because he had already prayed it with the Prophet ﷺ, while they were praying obligatory; and because the Prophet ﷺ in some types of the fear prayer prayed with one group two rak'ahs as their obligation, then prayed with another group two rak'ahs as voluntary for him, while for them it was obligatory. So there is no harm."

- **Scholar:** Shaykh Ibn Baz
- **URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/15611/%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B2-%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B6%D8%A9-%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%81%D9%84

### Source: binbaz.org.sa — Fatwa #8449: "صلاة المفترض خلف المتنفل صحيحة"

**Verbatim quote (Arabic):**

> والصواب أن الصلاة صحيحة، وأن صلاة المفترض خلف المتنفل صحيحة، هذا هو الأرجح من قولي العلماء؛ أنه يجوز للمفترض أن يصلي خلف المتنفل، كما كان معاذ يصلي بأصحابه العشاء، وهو قد صلاها مع النبي ﷺ، فيصلي بهم متنفلاً، وهم مفترضون.

**Translation:** "And the correct view is that the prayer is valid, and that the obligatory prayer behind one praying voluntary is valid — this is the stronger of the two scholarly positions: that it is permissible for the one praying obligatory to pray behind the one praying voluntary, just as Mu'adh used to lead his companions in Isha while he had already prayed it with the Prophet ﷺ, so he would lead them as voluntary (mutanaffil) while they were obligatory (muftaridun)."

- **Scholar:** Shaykh Ibn Baz
- **URL:** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/8449/%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%81%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%B6-%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%81%D9%84-%D8%B5%D8%AD%D9%8A%D8%AD%D8%A9

## 2. SHAYKH IBN 'UTHAYMEEN (ابن عثيمين)

### Source: Majmu' Fatawa wa Rasa'il Ibn 'Uthaymeen (Shamela) — "حكم صلاة الفريضة خلف المتنفل كمن صلى العشاء مع الذين يصلون التراويح"

**Verbatim quote (Arabic):**

> **وسئل فضيلته: عن حكم صلاة الفريضة خلف المتنفل كمن صلى العشاء مع الذين يصلون التراويح؟**
>
> فأجاب بقوله: لا بأس أن يصلي العشاء خلف من يصلي التراويح، وقد نص على ذلك الإمام أحمد رحمه الله، فإن كان مسافراً وأدرك الإمام من أول الصلاة سلم معه، وإلا أتم ما بقي إذا سلم الإمام.

**Translation:** "There is no harm in praying Isha behind one who is praying Taraweeh, and Imam Ahmad explicitly stated this. If he is a traveler and catches the imam from the beginning of the prayer, he gives salam with him; otherwise he completes what remains after the imam gives salam."

- **Scholar:** Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-'Uthaymeen
- **Book:** Majmu' Fatawa wa Rasa'il al-'Uthaymeen, vol. 12, p. 444 (approx)
- **URL:** https://shamela.ws/book/12293/4722

### Source: Same volume — "حكم صلاة الفرض خلف من يصلي نافلة؟"

**Verbatim quote (Arabic):**

> **وسئل فضيلة الشيخ: عن حكم صلاة الفرض خلف من يصلي نافلة؟**
>
> فأجاب بقوله: يجوز أن يصلي الإنسان فرضاً خلف من يصلي نافلة، ويدل لذلك حديث معاذ بن جبل رضى الله عنه أنه كان يصلي مع النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم العشاء ثم يرجع إلى قومه فيصلي بهم تلك الصلاة، فتكون له نافلة فريضة، وهذا وقع في عهد النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم.
>
> فإن قال قائل: لعل النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لم يعلم به.
>
> فالجواب على ذلك من وجهين:
>
> الأول: أن نقول يبعد أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لم يعلم به، لسيما وأنه قد شكى إليه في الإطالة حين صلى بهم ذات ليلة فأطال، ثم دعاه النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم ووعظه والقصة معروفه، فيبعد أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لم يعلم بحال معاذ رضى الله عنه.
>
> الوجه الثاني: إنه على فرض أن يكون النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لم يعلم بصنيع معاذ هذا، فإن الله سبحانه وتعالى قد علم به ولم ينزل وحي من الله تعالى بإنكار هذا العمل، ولهذا نقول: كل ما يجرى في عهد النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فإنه حجة بإقرار الله له، والله سبحانه وتعالى لا يقر أحداً على باطل...

**Translation:** "It is permissible for a person to pray an obligatory prayer behind one who is praying a voluntary prayer, and the evidence for that is the hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him) that he used to pray Isha with the Prophet ﷺ then return to his people and lead them in that prayer, so it was voluntary (nafl) for him and obligatory (fard) for them, and this occurred during the time of the Prophet ﷺ.

If someone says: Perhaps the Prophet ﷺ did not know about it — the answer is twofold:

First: It is far-fetched that the Prophet ﷺ did not know about it, especially since a complaint was made to him about the lengthening [of the recitation] when [Mu'adh] led them one night and made it long, then the Prophet ﷺ called him and admonished him, and the story is well known. So it is far-fetched that the Prophet ﷺ did not know about Mu'adh's situation.

Second: Even if we assume the Prophet ﷺ did not know about Mu'adh's action, Allah — glorified and exalted — did know about it and did not send down revelation from Allah denying this action. Therefore we say: Everything that occurs during the time of the Prophet ﷺ is a proof by Allah's tacit approval (iqraar), and Allah does not approve anyone upon falsehood..."

- **Scholar:** Shaykh Ibn 'Uthaymeen
- **Book:** Majmu' Fatawa wa Rasa'il al-'Uthaymeen, vol. 12
- **Hadith reference cited by Ibn Uthaymeen:** Al-Bukhari (Kitab al-Jama'ah wa al-Imamah, Bab idha salla thumma amma qawman) and Muslim (Kitab al-Salah, Bab al-Qira'ah fi al-Isha)
- **Quran cited by Ibn Uthaymeen as analogy:** Surat al-Nisa, ayah 108 (يَسْتَخْفُونَ مِنَ النَّاسِ وَلَا يَسْتَخْفُونَ مِنَ اللَّهِ...) — to prove that Allah reveals what is hidden and does not approve falsehood
- **URL:** https://shamela.ws/book/12293/4723

### Source: Fatawa Nur 'ala al-Darb (Ibn Uthaymeen) — "ائتمام المفترض بالمتنفل"

**Verbatim quote (Arabic):**

> فأجاب رحمه الله تعالى: هذا العمل صحيح وغايته أنه ائتمام مفترض بمتنفل وائتمام المفترض بالمتنفل جائز ودليله أن معاذ بن جبل رضي الله عنه كان يصلى مع النبي صلى الله عليه وعلى آله وسلم صلاة العشاء ثم يخرج إلى قومه فيصلي بهم تلك الصلاة أي صلاة العشاء فهي له نافلة ولهم فريضة. كذلك أيضاً غايته أن هذا المنفرد صار إماماً بعد أن كان منفرداً وهذا أيضاً جائز أي يجوز للمأموم أن يدخل مع منفرد ليكون إماماً له في الفريضة والنافلة ودليله أن النبي صلى الله عليه وعلى آله وسلم قام ذات ليلة يصلى وكان ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما عنده فقام ابن عباس ليصلى معه فقام عن يسار النبي صلى الله عليه وعلى آله وسلم فأداره النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم من ورائه ليكون عن يمينه ومضى في صلاته فها هو النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم كان منفرداً ثم أصبح إماماً. فلو قال قائل هذا في النفل والنفل أخف من الفرض قلنا الأصل تساوي الفرض والنفل في الأحكام إلا بدليل...

**Translation:** "This action is valid, and it is at most the following of one praying obligatory behind one praying voluntary, and following of the obligatory-praying person behind the voluntary-praying person is permissible. Its evidence is that Mu'adh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him) used to pray Isha with the Prophet ﷺ then go out to his people and lead them in that prayer — Isha — so it was nafl for him and fard for them. Also, it is at most that this solitary person became an imam after being alone, and this is also permissible — i.e., it is permissible for the follower to enter with a solitary person to make him an imam in both obligatory and voluntary prayer. Its evidence is that the Prophet ﷺ stood one night to pray and Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with them both) was with him, so Ibn Abbas stood to pray with him and stood on the left of the Prophet ﷺ, so the Prophet ﷺ moved him from behind to his right and continued in his prayer. So here the Prophet ﷺ was solitary then became an imam. If someone says: this is in voluntary prayer and voluntary is lighter than obligatory — we say: the principle is equality of obligatory and voluntary in rulings except with evidence..."

- **Scholar:** Shaykh Ibn 'Uthaymeen
- **Book:** Fatawa Nur 'ala al-Darb
- **URL:** https://shamela.ws/book/2300/3032

## 3. SHAYKH SALIH AL-FAWZAN (الفوزان)

### Source: News reports citing al-Fawzan's fatwa (video statement)

**Verbatim quote (Arabic, as reported):**

> وأكد الشيخ "الفوزان" جواز الصلاة في هاتين الحالتين، وذلك لأن الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم كان يصلي معه بعض الصحابة ثم يخرج ويصلي بقومه. ولفت إلى أن معاذ بن جبل -رضي الله عنه- كان يصلي مع النبي -صلى الله عليه وسلم- ثم يذهب فيصلي بقومه، لذا كان جائزًا صلاة المتنفل في هذه الحالة، لأن الصلاة مع الرسول هي الفريضة، أما في حالة الصلاة مع قومه فإنه هو "المتنفل" وقومه هم "المفترضون".

**Translation:** "Shaykh al-Fawzan affirmed the permissibility of prayer in both these situations [nafl behind fard AND fard behind nafl], because the Messenger of Allah ﷺ — some of the Companions would pray with him then go out and pray with their people. ## 4. SHAYKH AL-ALBANI (الألباني) — HADITH GRADINGS

### Note on al-Albani's direct fiqh ruling

**NOT FOUND FROM SALAFI SOURCE:** A direct, explicit fatwa from al-Albani specifically on the fiqh ruling of "nafl behind fard" or "fard behind nafl" was not located on alalbani.info or the approved sites in this research session. However, al-Albani's **hadith gradings** are extensively cited by the other scholars and are critical to the evidence. His known positions from his audio lectures (silsilat al-Huda wa al-Nur) indicate he held the permissibility of a second congregation and related matters, but a verbatim direct quote on this specific issue was not secured.

### al-Albani's grading of the Hadith of Mu'adh (the key evidence)

**Hadith:** Jabir ibn 'Abdullah reported that Mu'adh ibn Jabal used to pray with the Prophet ﷺ, then go back and lead his people in that prayer. One night he prayed Isha with the Prophet ﷺ, then came to his people and led them, beginning with Surat al-Baqarah...

**Primary references:**

- Sahih Muslim: #465 (465a)

- Jami' al-Tirmidhi: #583
- Sunan Ibn Majah: #836, #986
- Sahih Ibn Khuzaymah: #521, #1635
- Sahih Ibn Hibban: #1524, #2401
- Sunan al-Daraqutni: #1074, #1075, #1076
- Musnad Ahmad: #14241, #14410

**Translation:** "Shaykh Imam Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani: **Hasan Sahih** — [graded in] 'Sahih Abi Dawud' (no. 612)"

### al-Albani on the Hadith of Ibn Abbas joining the Prophet's night prayer

**Hadith:** Ibn Abbas stayed overnight with his maternal aunt Maymuna. The Prophet ﷺ got up to pray at night and Ibn Abbas got up to pray with him. He stood on his left and the Prophet took hold of his head and made him stand on his right.

**References:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari: #1198, #698, #667 (also #1138 references the 13 rak'ah count)
- Sahih Muslim: #763

**al-Albani's grading:** This hadith is in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, so it is **muttafaq 'alayhi** (agreed upon) — the highest grading. al-Albani grades all agreed-upon hadiths as authentic. No specific separate grading needed.

**Important fiqhi note on this hadith (relevant to the question):** In this hadith, the Prophet ﷺ was praying **Tahajjud (nafl/night voluntary prayer)** and Ibn Abbas **joined him**. The key question is: **was Ibn Abbas also praying Tahajjud (same intention) or something different?**

The scholars explain that in this hadith, **both the Prophet ﷺ and Ibn Abbas were praying voluntary night prayer (Tahajjud/nafl)** — i.e., **same intention** (both nafl). This hadith is cited by the scholars primarily to prove a DIFFERENT point: that **a person can start praying alone and then someone joins him and he becomes an imam** (i.e., the intention to lead is not required from the outset). It is NOT cited as evidence for "different intentions" per se, because in this case the intentions were the same (both nafl).

- Imam al-Bukhari included this report under the chapter: "If the imam did not intend to lead others in prayer, then people join him so he leads them in prayer."
- **Source:** islamqa.info Fatwa #153386 (citing al-Bukhari's chapter heading)

## 8. THE EVIDENCE IN DETAIL

### Evidence #1: Hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal (the primary evidence)

**Text (Sahih Muslim 465a):**

> Jabir reported that Mu'adh b. Jabal used to pray with the Apostle (ﷺ), then came and led his people in prayer. One night he said the night prayer [Isha] with the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ). He then came to his people and led them in prayer beginning with Surat al-Baqara. A man turned aside, pronounced the taslim, then prayed alone and departed... The Prophet (ﷺ) said to Mu'adh: "Are you there to (put the people) to trial? Recite such and such [surahs]..."

**The explicit version (Daraqutni #1076, Ibn Khuzaymah #1635):**

> "Mu'adh used to pray Isha with the Prophet ﷺ, then return to his people and lead them in that prayer — **it was voluntary (nafl) for him and obligatory (fard) for them** (هي له نافلة ولهم فريضة)."

- **Grading:** Sahih (agreed upon, in Bukhari and Muslim; the explicit "nafl for him, fard for them" wording is in Abu Dawud, Nasa'i, Daraqutni, Ibn Khuzaymah, Ibn Hibban)
- **al-Albani's grading:** Hasan Sahih (Sahih Abi Dawud, no. 612)
- **Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut:** Sahih on the condition of Muslim

**How this evidence works:** Mu'adh prayed Isha (fard) WITH the Prophet ﷺ as a follower. Then he went to his people and led them in Isha. For his people, it was their fard Isha. For Mu'adh himself, since he had already fulfilled his Isha obligation with the Prophet, the second prayer was **nafl (voluntary) for him**. The Prophet ﷺ knew about this (the complaint about the long recitation reached him) and **did not prohibit it** — tacit approval (iqraar).

**Relevance to the question:** This proves **fard behind nafl** (the people prayed fard behind Mu'adh who was praying nafl). The scholars extend this by analogy/principle to the reverse direction as well.

### Evidence #2: The Fear Prayer (صلاة الخوف)

**Text (Abu Dawud, narrated by Abu Bakrah and others):**

> The Prophet ﷺ prayed with one group two rak'ahs then gave salam, then prayed with the other group two rak'ahs then gave salam. The first was his fard and the second was nafl for him, while those behind him were praying fard.

- **Grading:** Sahih (al-Albani grades it in Sahih Abi Dawud)
- **Note:** Ibn al-Qayyim held the view that there was no salam between the two groups (the four rak'ahs were continuous for the Prophet), while al-Nawawi and the majority held there was a salam. This is a difference in interpretation but the majority position supports the evidence.
- **Relevance:** The Prophet (imam) prayed nafl while the second group (followers) prayed fard. This is **fard behind nafl**.

### Evidence #3: Hadith of Abu Dharr (nafl behind fard — directly relevant to the question)

**Text (Sahih Muslim 648):**

> The Prophet ﷺ said: "O Abu Dharr, perform the prayer at its appointed time. If you come to the people and they have already prayed, then you have preserved your prayer. But if they have not prayed, then pray with them, and it will be a voluntary (nafl) prayer for you."

- **Grading:** Sahih (Muslim)
- **Relevance:** This directly proves **nafl behind fard** — the follower (Abu Dharr) already prayed his fard alone, then joins the congregation (who are praying fard) and it counts as **nafl for him**. This is the same direction as the questioner's case (Tahajjud/nafl behind Isha/fard), though the context differs (this is about repeating a prayer already prayed, while the question is about Tahajjud specifically).

### Evidence #4: Hadith of Ibn Abbas joining the Prophet's night prayer

**Text (Sahih al-Bukhari 1198, Sahih Muslim 763):**

> Ibn Abbas said: "I stayed overnight with my maternal aunt [Maymuna]. The Prophet ﷺ got up to pray at night and I got up to pray with him. I stood on his left and he took hold of my head and made me stand on his right."

- **Grading:** Muttafaq 'alayhi (agreed upon — highest grading)
- **al-Bukhari's chapter heading:** "If the imam did not intend to lead others in prayer, then people join him so he leads them in prayer."
- **Relevance to the question:** This hadith proves that a person praying alone can be joined by another and become an imam **without prior intention to lead**. However, in this specific hadith, **both the Prophet and Ibn Abbas were praying Tahajjud (nafl)** — i.e., **SAME intention** (both voluntary night prayer). This hadith is NOT evidence for "different intentions" per se. It is cited for the related principle of becoming an imam without prior intention.

### Evidence #5: Hadith of the man who entered the mosque and the Prophet asked "who will give sadaqah to him by praying with him"

**Text (al-Bukhari, Muslim):**

> A man entered the mosque after the Prophet and companions had finished praying in congregation. The Prophet said: "Who will give sadaqah to him by praying with him in congregation?" So a man from among the companions (who had already prayed) stood up and prayed with him [in congregation].

- **Relevance:** The companion who stood up had already prayed his fard, so his prayer with the latecomer was **nafl for him** while it was **fard for the latecomer**. This is **fard behind nafl** (the latecomer prayed fard behind one praying nafl).

### Evidence #6: Hadith of Yazid al-Aswad at Mina

**Text (Muslim):**

> The Prophet ﷺ said: "If you pray in your resting places and then come upon an imam (leading a congregation), pray again with him, and it will be nafl for you."

- **Relevance:** Same principle as Abu Dharr — nafl behind fard.

### The opposing evidence (and the scholars' response)

**The hadith used by those who prohibit different intentions:**

> "إنما جعل الإمام ليؤتم به، فلا تختلفوا عليه" — "The imam is only appointed to be followed, so do not differ from him." (Muttafaq 'alayhi)

**Ibn Taymiyyah's response (verbatim, cited above):** The "differing" (اختلاف) in this hadith refers to **differing in outward actions** (الأفعال الظاهرة) — e.g., bowing when the imam stands, standing when he bows — NOT to differing in internal intention. The Prophet ﷺ himself explained this in the completion of the hadith: "When he bows, then bow; when he rises from bowing, then rise..." This explanation shows the prohibition is about physical actions, not intentions. Even if one argues it is general, it is **specified (mukhassas)** by the specific hadiths of Mu'adh and the fear prayer.

## 9. CONDITIONS AND RESTRICTIONS MENTIONED BY THE SCHOLARS

1. **The outward actions must match:** The follower must follow the imam in the outward actions (bowing, prostrating, etc.) even if the intentions differ. The "do not differ from the imam" hadith applies to actions, not intentions. (Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen)

2. **Completing what remains after the imam's salam:** If the follower is praying a prayer with MORE rak'ahs than the imam (e.g., praying Isha 4 rak'ahs behind someone praying Taraweeh 2 rak'ahs), the follower completes the remaining rak'ahs **alone after the imam gives salam**. (Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen)

3. **If the follower's prayer has FEWER rak'ahs than the imam's:** (e.g., praying Maghrib 3 rak'ahs behind someone praying Isha 4 rak'ahs) — the follower gives salam after his 3rd rak'ah and does not continue with the imam. (Ibn Uthaymeen — discussed in the context of praying Maghrib behind an Isha imam)

4. **The imam should be the most qualified:** If someone is praying nafl and a person joins him intending fard, the one praying nafl should be the most knowledgeable of the Quran and prayer rulings, and preferably the regular imam of the mosque. (Ibn Uthaymeen, cited in shamela.ws/book/37547/293)

5. **Recitation volume:** If a person starts praying behind someone praying nafl, the imam has the choice to recite aloud or quietly. But if he intended to lead from the outset, he should recite aloud (for prayers that are normally aloud). (islamqa.info #153386, citing the hadith of Mu'adh)

6. **Not making nafl congregation a permanent habit:** Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen and others note that while praying nafl in congregation is permissible, making it a **permanent, regular congregation** (other than Taraweeh) is considered an innovation (bid'ah). "Whoever persists in praying nafl in congregation — other than Taraweeh — has innovated." (cited in shamela.ws/book/7708/8176, from lessons of Shaykh 'A'id al-Qarni citing the scholars)

7. **Order of prayers (الترتيب):** If someone has missed a prayer and joins a congregation praying a different prayer, the order of prayers must still be maintained. (Ibn Baz, fatwa #4762)

## 10. APPLICATION TO THE SPECIFIC QUESTION

**The question:** Can a person pray 2 rak'ah Tahajjud (nafl) behind/beside someone praying 4 rak'ah Isha (fard)?

**This is the case of: nafl behind fard (المتنفل خلف المفترض).**

**Answer based on the sourced Salafi scholar positions:**

**YES, it is permissible** according to the scholars cited, based on the following:

1. **The general principle** stated by Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, and al-Fawzan: "اختلاف نية الإمام والمأموم لا يضر" — the difference of intention between imam and follower does not harm the prayer. This applies in BOTH directions (nafl behind fard AND fard behind nafl).

2. **Ibn Taymiyyah explicitly states both directions are proven:** "فقد ثبت صلاة المتنفل خلف المفترض في عدة أحاديث، وثبت أيضاً بالعكس" — "It is established — the prayer of the voluntary-praying person behind the obligatory-praying person — in several hadiths, and the reverse is also established."

3. **The hadith of Abu Dharr** directly supports nafl behind fard: the follower who already prayed joins the congregation (praying fard) and it counts as nafl for him.

4. **The hadith of the man entering the mosque** (al-Bukhari) — the companion who had already prayed stood and prayed with the latecomer; his prayer was nafl while the latecomer's was fard. This is nafl (imam) behind fard (follower) — the reverse of the question's direction, but proves the principle.

**Practical application for the specific case:**
- The follower praying Tahajjud (2 rak'ah nafl) behind the imam praying Isha (4 rak'ah fard) would pray 2 rak'ahs with the imam.
- When the imam continues to the 3rd and 4th rak'ah (of Isha), the follower who only intended 2 rak'ahs (Tahajjud) would **give salam after his 2nd rak'ah** and depart — since his Tahajjud is complete at 2 rak'ahs.
- Alternatively, if the follower intends to continue praying more Tahajjud (e.g., 4 or more rak'ahs), he may continue following the imam for the full 4 rak'ahs of Isha, and his prayer would be 4 rak'ahs of Tahajjud (nafl) while the imam's is 4 rak'ahs of Isha (fard).
- **This specific scenario (Tahajjud behind Isha) was not found as a standalone explicit fatwa** on the approved sites. The ruling is derived from the general principle affirmed by all cited scholars. **Marked as: derived from general principle, not from a fatwa explicitly naming "Tahajjud behind Isha."**

**CAUTION:** The scholars note that while this is permissible, **Tahajjud is specifically a night prayer prayed after sleeping** (after Isha and after waking up). If the Isha prayer is still being prayed (i.e., Isha time has not ended and the person has not slept yet), then technically it is not yet "Tahajjud" in the strict sense — it would be "Qiyam al-Layl" or general nafl. The term "Tahajjud" specifically refers to prayer after sleeping. (This is a well-known distinction from al-Albani's "Qiyam Ramadan" and the hadith evidence, though a specific fatwa on this terminological point in this context was not secured from the approved sites in this session.)
