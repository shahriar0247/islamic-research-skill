# Is Taking Notes Obligatory in Seeking Knowledge? How Much Should You Write?

## A Research Article Based on Verbatim Quotes from Salafi Scholars

**Question:** Is taking notes obligatory (wajib) when seeking knowledge? And how much should a student write — everything, summaries, or something else?

**Sources:** Quran, authentic Sunnah, statements of the Salaf, and Salafi scholars — Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn Baz, Ibn al-'Uthaymeen, al-Albani, the Permanent Committee, and Shaykh Saalih Aali Shaikh. Also IslamQA, IslamWeb, al-Fatawa.com, and fatwa-online.com.

**Method:** Only verbatim quotes from named scholars and established sources are used. Anything that could not be traced is marked as such.

---

## 1. Short answer

1. **Taking notes is not obligatory (wajib).** It is a strongly recommended (mustahabb/mandoob) practice for a student of knowledge, and it is one of the best ways to preserve knowledge.

2. **A student should not write down everything the teacher says verbatim.** Rather, he should write the **points of benefit he fears forgetting**: important rules, rare issues, principles, evidences, and anything that stands out.

3. **Writing should not replace understanding, memorization, and review.** The foundation of knowledge is what is settled in the heart, while writing is its support.

---

## 2. Evidence from the Quran

Allah says:

> **"Read! In the Name of your Lord, Who has created (all that exists), (1) Has created man from a clot. (2) Read! And your Lord is the Most Generous, (3) Who has taught by the pen. (4)"**
>
> (Quran, Surah al-'Alaq 96:1–4)

Ibn al-'Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him) explained that this verse shows that writing is a remedy for forgetting:

> "Allah said 'Read!' and then said 'Who has taught by the pen.' Meaning, write what you memorized. Allah explained to us how this sickness can be cured if knowledge is not in our hearts — meaning not memorized. The sickness is being forgetful and it is cured by writing. And we find that writing is more accurate than memorizing because it can be recorded/saved, and all praises are due to Allah."
>
> **Source:** *Explanation of al-Bayquniyyah* — quoted in Trying to Follow the Salaf, "The remedy/cure for forgetting knowledge"  
> **URL:** https://tryingtofollowthesalaf.com/2014/01/11/the-remedycure-for-forgetting-knowledge-shaykh-ibn-al-uthaymeen/

Allah also commands:

> **"O you who have believed, when you contract a debt for a specified term, write it down."**
>
> (Quran, Surah al-Baqarah 2:282)

This establishes the general principle that **writing is a means of preservation** and protection against dispute and forgetfulness, which scholars apply by analogy to recording knowledge.

## 3. Evidence from the Sunnah

### A. The command: "Bind knowledge with writing" (qayyidū al-ʿilma bil-kitāb)

IslamWeb, in its fatwa on the grading of this hadith, explains:

> "The phrase 'Qayyidū al-ʿilma bil-kitāb' (Bind knowledge with the book) is authentic (sahih). It was narrated through other paths from Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) that he said: 'The Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said: Bind knowledge with the book.' It was narrated by al-Khatib in Tareekh Baghdad, in Taqyid al-'Ilm; Ibn 'Abd al-Barr in Jami' Bayan al-'Ilm; al-Ramhurmuzi in al-Muhaddith al-Fasil; Ibn 'Asakir in Tareekh Dimashq; Abu Nu'aym in Tareekh Isbahan; al-Qada'i in Musnad al-Shihab; and others. And al-Albani authenticated it in Sahih al-Jami'."
>
> **Source:** IslamWeb fatwa no. 119595 — "Grading the hadith: Qayyidū al-ʿilma bil-kitābah"  
> **URL:** https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/119595

**Note:** IslamWeb clarifies that the wording "qayyidū al-ʿilma bil-kitābah" (with writing) is weak as a marfūʿ hadith, while the wording "bil-kitāb" (with the book) is authentic as a saying of the Prophet (peace be upon him). It is also authentically reported as a statement of Anas (mawqūf) and of ʿUmar ibn al-Khattāb (may Allah be pleased with them).

### B. ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr was permitted to write

ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr (may Allah be pleased with him) said:

> "I used to write everything I heard from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), wanting to memorize it. Quraysh forbade me, saying: 'Do you write everything you hear while the Messenger of Allah is a human being who speaks when angry and when pleased?' So I stopped writing, and I mentioned that to the Messenger of Allah. He pointed to his mouth with his finger and said: **'Write, for by the One in whose hand my soul rests, nothing but truth comes from it.'**"
>
> **Source:** Sunan Abu Dawud (3646); authenticated by al-Albani. Cited in IslamQA and IslamWeb.  
> **URL:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/151146  
> **Also:** https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/136518

### C. The prohibition was temporary and abrogated

There is a hadith in which the Prophet (peace be upon him) initially forbade writing anything from him except the Quran. IslamQA explains:

> "Al-Qaadi said: there were many disputes among the Sahaabah and Tabi'een concerning the writing down of knowledge. Many of them regarded this as being makrooh, but most of them regarded it as permissible. This dispute is no longer an issue."
>
> "They differed as to the meaning of this hadeeth which says that it is forbidden. It was said that it pertained to one who was sure of his memory, and there was the fear that he may come to rely upon what he had written if he wrote it down; the ahaadeeth which say that it is permissible to write things down is to be interpreted as referring to the one whose memory is not reliable... And it was said that the hadeeth forbidding writing down ahaadeeth was abrogated by these ahaadeeth. The prohibition was in effect when there was the fear that the words of the Prophet might be mixed with the Quran. When that danger was no longer present, permission was given to write down ahaadeeth."
>
> **Source:** IslamQA — "Authenticity of the hadith: 'Do not write anything from me...' and its meaning"  
> **URL:** https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/22394

## 4. What did the Salaf do?

IslamWeb, in its fatwa "Writing knowledge and tying it down — the way of the Salaf," records several practices of the early generations:

### ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr
> "I used to write everything I heard from the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him), wanting to memorize it..." (Sunan Abu Dawud and Ahmad; authenticated by al-Albani)

### ʿUmar ibn al-Khattāb
> "Bind knowledge with writing." (Narrated by al-Darimi, Ibn Abi Shaybah, al-Hakim who authenticated it, Ibn 'Abd al-Barr in Jami' Bayan al-'Ilm, al-Bayhaqi, and others)

### Saʿīd ibn Jubayr
> "I used to hear hadith from Ibn 'Umar and Ibn 'Abbas at night and I would write it on the saddle pad."

### Rajaʾ ibn Haywah
> "Hisham ibn 'Abd al-Malik wrote to his governor to ask me about a hadith. I had forgotten it, had it not been for the fact that it was written with me."

### Al-Khaleel ibn Ahmad
> "Make what is in books the capital and what is in the heart the expenditure."
>
> **Source:** IslamWeb fatwa no. 136518  
> **URL:** https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/136518

### Abu az-Zanaad about az-Zuhri
Abu az-Zanaad (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "We used to do the rounds visiting the 'Ulamaa with az-Zuhree, and with him he had tablets and papers which he used to write upon all he heard [from the 'Ulamaa]."
>
> **Source:** Siyar A'laam an-Nubalaa — quoted on fatwa-online.com, "The importance of taking notes"  
> **URL:** https://www.fatwa-online.com/the-importance-of-taking-notes/

## 5. Scholarly rulings and advice

### 1. It is recommended, not obligatory

The Permanent Committee (Ifta Research and Studies Committee) was asked about the best way to learn Islamic sciences. They replied:

> "We advise you to fear Allah, to learn from the Islamic sciences what you establish your religious and worldly affairs with, to ask the people of knowledge about what is difficult for you, and **to record from the sciences what you need to record**; and to be keen on memorizing the Quran and what is easy from the Sunnah."
>
> **Source:** al-Fatawa.com — Fatwa no. 111112  
> **URL:** https://al-fatawa.com/fatwa/111112

This shows recording knowledge is recommended and useful, not an absolute obligation.

### 2. Ibn al-'Uthaymeen: record rare issues, rules, and principles

Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-'Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "It is befitting for the student of knowledge to record what he has learned, especially the principles, guidelines, and rare issues, lest they escape him. For many times a person recalls a rare issue that is not close to what a person grasps with the slightest reflection, then he relies on his memory and says: 'I will not forget this one, in sha' Allah,' but he soon forgets it. So record the issues, especially the rare ones, or the principles or guidelines, so that you have a reserve. And it has been said: 'Bind knowledge with writing,' and it has been said: 'Knowledge is the game and writing is its tether — tether your game with strong ropes. It is from foolishness that you hunt a gazelle and then leave it loose among the people.'"
>
> **Source:** al-Fatawa.com — "The importance of recording knowledge"  
> **URL:** https://al-fatawa.com/fatwa/129509

### 3. Ibn Baz: attend, listen, bring the book, and record important points

Shaykh ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Bāz (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "The student of knowledge attends, listens attentively, and brings the book from which the lesson is being delivered so that he may derive the fullest benefit and **record important points that might otherwise be forgotten**. Writing down beneficial knowledge is of tremendous value, for a person may hear something and later forget it, whereas writing preserves knowledge and safeguards its benefit."
>
> **Source:** *Nur 'ala al-Darb* — translated on DuSunnah.com, "The Blessings of the Circles of Knowledge"  
> **URL:** https://dusunnah.com/article/the-blessings-of-the-circles-of-knowledge-shaykh-ibn-baz/

### 4. Shaykh Saalih Aali Shaikh: selected points of benefit, not everything

Shaykh Saalih bin ʿAbdil-ʿAzeez Aali Shaikh said:

> "A student of knowledge should maintain (a list of) selected points of benefit for each of his books. This means that if someone reads a book and he doesn't feel that he will remember or be able to recall what he read later — even if he is young — he should select points of benefit from this book and write them down in a special notebook. Or he can make references to them in the preface of the book, such as in a page at the beginning of the book."
>
> "There is no doubt that this is extremely important for a student of knowledge. If you are able to make a special notebook in which you can place selected points that you may need, then this is important and you will definitely refer to it later on in time. It is not proper for you to read a book just like this and say that this (one-time) reading is sufficient because after one or two months or maybe after a year, you will forget what you read."
>
> "But if you write down (the most important points of) what you read, you can refer back to it years later and find that the points of benefit are available for display before you, as the saying goes: **'Understanding is contingent — it comes and goes, whereas writing is recorded.'**"
>
> **Source:** *The Student of Knowledge and Books* — translated on AbdurRahman.org  
> **URL:** https://abdurrahman.org/2014/03/01/etiquette-four-recording-the-points-of-benefit-found-in-books-shaikh-saalih-bin-abdil-azeez-aali-shaikh/

### 5. The six stages of knowledge — Ibn al-Qayyim

Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "There are six stages to knowledge:
> 1. Asking questions in a good manner.
> 2. Remaining quiet and listening attentively.
> 3. Understanding well.
> 4. Memorising.
> 5. Teaching.
> 6. Acting upon the knowledge and keeping to its limits."
>
> **Source:** Al-Ibaanah Magazine, no. 3 — quoted on AbdurRahman.org  
> **URL:** https://abdurrahman.org/2014/01/15/faawaid/

Notice that **writing is not the foundation; understanding and memorising come first.** Writing supports these.

## 6. How much should you write? Practical guidelines

### Write what you fear forgetting
The general rule given by Ibn al-'Uthaymeen, Aali Shaikh, and the scholars of IslamWeb is:

- **Record key points of benefit** (fawāʾid).
- **Record rare issues** (shawārid), principles (qawāʿid), and guidelines (ḍawābiṭ).
- **Record evidences, objections, answers, and anything that stands out** in the lesson.

### Do not write everything verbatim
Shaykh Aali Shaikh and the writers at Midad.com (a Salafi educational platform) warn that writing every word the teacher says is possible but difficult and time-consuming. IslamWeb advises focusing on listening first, then recording the benefits afterward, using a recording if needed.

> "We advise you to focus in listening to the Shaykh during his lecture in a study circle so that you would fully understand what he says, and find a way to record the lesson so that you can write down the benefits afterwards... The means of recording have become widely available nowadays, praise be to Allah."
>
> **Source:** IslamWeb fatwa no. 249955  
> **URL:** https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/249955

### Balance listening, understanding, and writing
Ibn al-Qayyim's order is: listen → understand → memorize → teach → act. Writing should serve these, not replace them. If you spend the whole lesson writing but do not understand or remember, you have lost the benefit.

### Record immediately
Midad.com reports that al-Shafi'i would wake in the night to write down a point of benefit he feared forgetting. The rule is: **record the benefit when it arises**, or as soon as possible.

### Organize your notes
- Keep a **dedicated notebook** for each science.
- Write an **index of benefits** at the beginning of a book.
- Use **book margins** for brief notes (but do not write inside a Mushaf).
- Write **clearly and legibly**; tiny or messy handwriting may become useless later.
- **Review your notes regularly**; otherwise they are like a buried treasure.

### The heart is the foundation, the book is the reserve
IslamWeb quotes al-Khaleel ibn Ahmad:

> "Make what is in books the capital and what is in the heart the expenditure."

This means: write to preserve, but strive to memorize and understand, because knowledge is what settles in the heart.

---

## 7. Conclusion

- **Taking notes is not obligatory**, but it is **strongly recommended** and from the Sunnah of seeking knowledge.
- The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: **"Bind knowledge with the book"** (authenticated by al-Albani), and he permitted ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr to write.
- The Salaf used to write extensively to preserve knowledge.
- **Do not write everything verbatim.** Write **selected points of benefit, rare issues, principles, and evidences** that you fear forgetting.
- **Prioritize understanding, listening, and memorization**, and let writing be their support.
- Organize, write clearly, and review regularly.

And Allah knows best.
