# Faris Al Hammadi: Can We Take Knowledge From Him?

> **A research note prepared according to the methodology of the Salaf.** Every effort has been made to attribute statements to their sources. Where a claim could not be verified against a Salafi scholar or a primary source, it is marked explicitly. This article does **not** issue a tabdee (declaration of innovation) or takfeer against anyone — it only gathers information so the reader can make an informed decision, in light of the guidance of the people of knowledge, about whether to take knowledge from a particular daee (caller).

## 5. Is He an "Extremist" or "On the Haqq"?

This question has two parts, because "extremist" can mean two different things:

### a) Is he a Khariji / takfeeri extremist?
**No.** By all available evidence, Faris Al Hammadi is **the opposite of a Khariji**. He refutes the Khawarij, warns against false takfeer, teaches that takfeer requires conditions and preventions, and affirms obedience to rulers. He is not accused by anyone of making takfeer of Muslims or of promoting violence. On this axis, he is **not an extremist** — he is, if anything, on the *other* extreme (excessive quietism).

### b) Is he "on the haqq" in his manhaj?
This is **mixed** and is the subject of genuine disagreement among the Salafis themselves:
- **In aqeedah and the foundations of the religion (Tawheed, Sunnah, anti-bid'ah, anti-Khawarij):** his positions are sound and align with the Salafi scholars.
- **In his political/manhaj stances (silence on Gaza, anti-boycott, framing all criticism of rulers as deviance, vagueness on internal disputes):** his positions are criticized by other Salafis as:
  - Going beyond the balanced position of the senior scholars (excessive madkhali-style loyalty to rulers).
  - Lacking the clarity and courage to speak the truth that the religion demands.
  - Being influenced by his **employment under a government-funded organization** (Dar al Ber Society / Islamic Information Center), which critics argue compromises his independence.

Shaykh Muhammad Hisham At-Tahiri — a qualified Salafi scholar — **gave him a tazkiyyah** and called him among the best duat. This is a positive recommendation from a known scholar and should be weighed seriously.

At the same time, the **Salafi Dawah Manchester** refutation series (written by students of knowledge associated with the Salafi publications circles) raises serious manhaj concerns that have **not been answered** by Faris with the clarity the situation demands.

**Honest conclusion:** He is **not a deviant in aqeedah**, and he is **not an extremist (Khariji)**. He is a **student of knowledge with a sound foundation in Tawheed** but with **controversial, government-aligned manhaj positions** and a tendency toward **vagueness** that has drawn legitimate criticism from other Salafis. Whether one calls this "on the haqq" depends on which issue is being discussed — the answer is not a simple yes or no.

## 6. Can We Take Knowledge From Him? — The Verdict in Light of the Scholars

This is the central question, and the Salafi scholars have laid down clear principles for answering it.

### Principle 1: Knowledge is taken from mature scholars, not from students who put themselves forward
Shaykh **Ibn Uthaymeen** (rahimahullah) was asked about students who attend lessons of *other students* instead of the scholars. He said:

> *"It is befitting for a person to seek knowledge from a mature [fully-qualified] scholar. This is because some students of knowledge put themselves forward to teach... if he was to divert as little as a fingertip away from the topic which he has fully grasped, then you will find he does not [really] have any knowledge. So it is obligatory upon the amateur student of knowledge to seek knowledge at the hands of scholars who are known to be authoritative because of their knowledge, trustworthiness and [practicing their] religion."* [[Fatwa-Online](https://www.fatwa-online.com/seeking-knowledge-from-students-of-knowledge/)]

### Principle 2: A scholar is known by the recommendation (tazkiyyah) of the scholars
Shaykh **Rabee ibn Hadi al-Madkhali** (hafizahullah) said:

> *"None of the Salaf would go into teaching until the scholars permitted him and authorized him, like Malik — he did not set up to teach and give verdicts except after seventy scholars nominated him."* [[Darussaafi](https://darussaafi.com/?p=213)]

### Principle 3: Knowledge is NOT taken from one who has no foundation from the scholars
Shaykh **Salih al-Fawzan** (hafizahullah) said:

> *"As long as he does not have foundation from the scholars (i.e. did not acquire knowledge from them in person and has no recommendations for that), rather he sought (knowledge) from papers and books, then knowledge is not taken from him because he does not understand the Madhab of the salaf. And it is not understood except by studying under the scholars... The likes of this is called a Muta-aalim and knowledge is not taken from him."* [[Salafi Dawah Manchester — Who do we take our Knowledge from?](https://salafidawahmanchester.com/2013/08/07/acquiring-knowledge-from-who/)]

### Applying these principles to Faris Al Hammadi:

| Criterion | Faris's case |
|-----------|--------------|
| Is he a mature scholar (aalam)? | **No** — by his own statement he is a "student of knowledge," not a scholar. |
| Does he have a tazkiyyah from a scholar? | **Yes** — from Dr. Muhammad Hisham At-Tahiri (a qualified Salafi scholar). |
| Did he acquire knowledge directly from scholars (not just books)? | **Partially** — he attended lectures of scholars in Dubai; his main documented link is to Shaykh At-Tahiri. No formal ijaazah is published. |
| Is he known for clarity and precision in manhaj? | **No** — he has been specifically criticized for vagueness and evasion by other Salafis. |
| Does he give fatwas? | **No** — he himself says "I don't give fatwas, I simply explain Islam." This is a point in his favour. |
| Are his foundational teachings (Tawheed, etc.) sound? | **Yes** — his Kitab at-Tawhid, anti-Khawarij, and anti-bid'ah content aligns with the scholars. |
| Are his manhaj/political positions reliable? | **Disputed** — they lean toward excessive quietism/Madkhalism and have drawn serious criticism. |

### Practical guidance (the balanced position):

1. **For foundational Aqeedah, Tawheed, refutation of Khawarij and bid'ah, and basic Seerah/Hadith explanations:** Faris's content is **generally sound and aligns with the Salafi scholars**. A beginner may benefit from his simplifications, *as long as* the topics are verified against the primary works of the scholars (Kitab at-Tawhid, fatawa of Ibn Baz, al-Albani, Ibn Uthaymeen, al-Fawzan).

2. **For fatwas (specific rulings):** Faris himself says he does not give fatwas. **Do not take specific fiqh rulings from him** — return to the scholars and their verified fatwa sites (binbaz.org.sa, alalbani.info, islamqa.info, fatwa-online.com).

3. **For manhaj, politics, rulers, Palestine, boycotts, and internal Salafi disputes:** **Be very cautious.** His positions here are controversial, lean toward excessive government-aligned quietism, and have been criticized by other Salafis with knowledge. **Do not take his political/manhaj positions as the final word** — refer to the statements of the senior scholars (Ibn Baz, al-Albani, Ibn Uthaymeen, al-Fawzan) on these issues, which are more balanced.

4. **For the issue of taking knowledge generally:** The ideal, as the scholars always advise, is to take knowledge **directly from the scholars** and from their verified books and recorded lessons — not from a social-media daee, however popular. Faris may be a useful **introduction** for an English-speaking beginner, but he should not be one's **primary or only** source of knowledge.

> **Bottom line:** Faris Al Hammadi is a **student of knowledge with a sound foundation in Tawheed and a tazkiyyah from a known Salafi scholar**, but he is **not a scholar**, his **manhaj/political positions are controversial and lean toward excessive quietism**, and he has been **criticized by other Salafis for vagueness**. Take your foundational knowledge from the scholars and their books; if you benefit from his simplifications, verify them against the primary sources, and **do not rely on him for manhaj, politics, or fatwas.**

## 7. Valuable Questions to Ask / Investigate About Him

These are questions a sincere seeker should ask — both about Faris specifically, and as a general methodology for evaluating any daee:

### About his scholarship and qualifications:
1. **What is his formal ijaazah?** Which scholars has he studied under *in person*, for how long, and in which specific books/sciences? A tazkiyyah (recommendation) is not the same as an ijaazah (authorization).
2. **Does Shaykh At-Tahiri's tazkiyyah cover his manhaj positions (politics, rulers, Palestine), or only his general da'wah and aqeedah?** The tazkiyyah as translated speaks of him being among the "best duat to Islam" — it does not appear to endorse every specific political stance he has taken. Does the Shaykh know about his stance on boycotts, Gaza silence, and the leaked audio?
3. **Has any senior scholar (al-Fawzan, al-Abbad, al-Luhaydan, etc.) specifically endorsed him**, or only Dr. At-Tahiri?
4. **Why did he say he has not studied under Shaykh Abdullah al-Bukhari?** Is this because he was not accepted, not interested, or simply lacked the opportunity? This matters because al-Bukhari is one of the major scholars of hadith and manhaj in Madinah.

### About his manhaj and positions:
5. **Why does he discourage boycotting Israeli products?** What is his evidence from the Quran, Sunnah, and the statements of the Salafi scholars? Does his position match that of Ibn Baz, al-Albani, Ibn Uthaymeen, and al-Fawzan on supporting the oppressed and opposing the oppressor?
6. **Why does he remain silent on the killing of Muslims in Gaza?** Is silence in the face of Muslim suffering a position supported by the Salafi scholars, or is it a compromise born of his employment under a government that has normalized with Israel?
7. **Is his near-total defense of the UAE government's actions consistent with the Salafi position?** The scholars affirm obedience to the ruler in ma'roof, but they also affirm forbidding evil and speaking the truth. Where does he draw the line, and does he draw it at all?
8. **Why is he vague about his disagreements with Salafipublications?** He said "I disagree with some of the things they say but they are my brothers" without specifying. Is this avoidance of clarity, and is it consistent with the scholars' emphasis on bayaan (clarification)?
9. **Why does he dismiss those seeking clarity on the Muhammad bin Hadi affair as "foolish" and "influenced by Shaytaan"?** Is this a knowledge-based refutation or an insult?
10. **What is his position on the leaked audio in which he allegedly says speaking about Israel could "stop his dawah"?** Is the clip authentic? If so, does this not indicate that his silence is motivated by worldly considerations (protecting his platform) rather than religious principle?

### About his da'wah and associations:
11. **Does his employment at the Islamic Information Center (under the government-funded Dar al Ber Society) compromise his independence?** Can a daee employed by a state that has normalized with Israel freely speak the truth about that normalization?
12. **What is his position on the "Abrahamic Family House" and the Hindu temple built in the UAE?** Has he criticized these, or has he defended/silent on them? (Critics accuse him of defending them.)
13. **What is his stance on Andrew Tate?** Critics accuse him of "tolerance" of Tate. Has he clearly warned against Tate's corrupt messages (promiscuity, arrogance, gambling, etc.) as the Salafi scholars would?
14. **Does he cooperate with people of bid'ah in his da'wah?** He himself warns against this — does he practice what he preaches?

### General methodology questions (apply to any daee):
15. **Do I take this person as my primary source of knowledge, or do I return to the scholars?** The scholars consistently advise taking knowledge from the scholars, not from social-media personalities.
16. **If I find a position of his that contradicts a clear statement of Ibn Baz, al-Albani, Ibn Uthaymeen, or al-Fawzan, which do I follow?** (Answer: the scholar with the dalil.)
17. **Am I following this daee because of his evidence, or because of his personality, popularity, and relatability?** Popularity is not a proof of correctness.
