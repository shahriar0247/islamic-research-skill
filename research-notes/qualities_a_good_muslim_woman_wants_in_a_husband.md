# Qualities a Good Muslim Woman Wants in a Husband

**Category:** Marriage  
**Topic:** Spouse selection based on the Qur’an, the authentic Sunnah, and the explanations of the approved Salafi scholars.

> **Foreword on cultural framing:** Salafi scholarship does not divide women into “Arab/non-Arab,” “traditional/non-traditional,” or “modern/conservative.” The Shariah addresses every Muslim woman with the same religious standard: the man she marries must be pleasing to Allah first. Compatibility (kafaa’ah) in the view of the strongest evidence is based on religion and piety, not on tribe, wealth, nationality, or culture.

---

## Chapter 1: The Foundation — Religion and Good Character

Every discussion about choosing a husband must begin with the Prophet’s command about religious commitment and character.

> The Messenger of Allah (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“If there comes to you one whose religious commitment and character pleases you, then marry [your female relative under your care] to him. If you do not do that, there will be fitnah (tribulation) on earth and great corruption.”*
>
> — Narrated by at-Tirmidhi (1084, 1085) and others; classed as **sahih/hasan** by al-Albani in *Sahih Sunan at-Tirmidhi*. Source: IslamQA, [“Should he marry someone who is above him in terms of social status?”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/84306); AbdurRahman.org, [“Marriage, Casts and Compatibility — Ibn Baaz”](https://abdurrahman.org/2014/01/29/marriagecastscompatibility/)

The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) also clarified what people naturally notice, then put the final criterion where it belongs.

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“Women are married for four things: their wealth, their lineage, their beauty, and their religion. So seek the one with religion — may your hands be rubbed with dust (i.e., may you prosper).”*
>
> — Agreed upon (al-Bukhari 5090; Muslim 1466); classed **sahih** by al-Albani. Source: IslamWeb, [“A Woman Is Married for Four Reasons”](https://islamweb.net/en/article/163819)

Shaykh Ibn Baaz (may Allah have mercy on him) summarized this for the woman who is choosing:

> “Seek the good man known for righteousness, maintaining the prayers, with good creed. Ask about him from trustworthy people who know him if he proposes to her. And even if she is the one who initiates, there is no harm… The point is: a woman may choose for herself a good man known for uprightness in his religion, but the final decision is through her wali. Her wali is the one who marries her off. If she and her wali agree on a good man, that is what is required. Her wali has no right to force her to marry someone who is not good, or someone she does not accept.”
>
> — Shaykh ‘Abd al-‘Aziz ibn Baz, BinBaz.org.sa, [“صفات الزوج الصالح”](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/11199/%D8%B5%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%88%D8%AC-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD)

## Chapter 2: Compatibility Is Only in Religion

A woman should not be told that a man is unsuitable simply because he is of a different nationality, tribe, or social class. Allah made piety the only true distinction.

> **Allah says:** *“O mankind! We have created you from a male and a female, and made you into nations and tribes, that you may know one another. Verily, the most honourable of you with Allah is he who has the most taqwa.”* (Quran 49:13)
>
> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“There is no superiority of an Arab over a non-Arab, or of a non-Arab over an Arab, or of a white man over a black man, or of a black man over a white man, except in taqwa.”* (Narrated by Ahmad; classed hasan by al-Albani.)
>
> Source: IslamQA, [“Should he marry someone who is above him in terms of social status?”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/84306)

Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote:

> “What is implied by the ruling of the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) is that attention should be paid to compatibility in religious commitment first and foremost. So a Muslim woman should not be given in marriage to a kaafir, or a chaste woman to an immoral man. The Qur’aan and Sunnah do not pay attention to any compatibility beyond that. It is haraam for a Muslim woman to marry an evil adulterer. No attention is paid to lineage, profession, wealth, or whether the man is free or a slave. It is permissible for a lowly slave to marry a free woman of noble birth, if he is chaste and Muslim.”
>
> — Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah, *Zad al-Ma’ad* 5/144, quoted in IslamQA [84306](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/84306)

Shaykh Ibn Baaz (may Allah have mercy on him) also warned against using clan or caste as a barrier:

> “From the evil and reprehensible matters is that some… say that there is no kafaa’ah (compatibility) between them and someone from outside their own clan… This is a great error, a monstrous ignorance, oppression against the woman, and it is a legislation which neither Allah nor His Messenger legislated… Rather, what it is obligatory in this matter is to consider only Religion as the compatibility factor.”
>
> — Shaykh ‘Abd al-‘Aziz ibn Baz, AbdurRahman.org, [“Marriage, Casts and Compatibility”](https://abdurrahman.org/2014/01/29/marriagecastscompatibility/)

## Chapter 3: Religious Commitment and Its Practical Signs

Religion is not a vague feeling. The woman and her guardian should look for its outward signs. The most important of these is prayer.

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“The covenant that is between us and them is the prayer; whoever abandons it has committed an act of disbelief.”* (Narrated by Imam Ahmad, at-Tirmidhi 2621, an-Nasa’i, and Ibn Majah; classed sahih by al-Albani.)
>
> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“Between a man and disbelief and shirk is the abandoning of the prayer.”* (Narrated by Muslim, 82.)
>
> Source: FatwaIslam, [“The Ruling on Giving a Daughter in Marriage to One Who Does Not Pray”](http://www.fatwaislam.com/fis/index.cfm?ID=64&scn=fd)

Shaykh Ibn Baaz (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> “And similarly, the man should be chosen by the woman, and she should strive for him to be righteous and good, far from causes of evil — not a drunkard, not one who shaves his beard, not a smoker, not one who commits adultery, and the like. She should strive for him to be sound; this is what is required — out of concern for the safety of religion.”
>
> — BinBaz.org.sa, [“الشروط الواجب توفرها في الزوجين”](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/10813/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%A8-%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%87%D8%A7-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%88%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%86)

He also said:

> “As for abandoning the prayer, it is kufr; its doer is considered a kaafir. It is necessary for him to pray, because of the saying of the Prophet: ‘Between a man and kufr and shirk is abandoning the prayer,’ and his saying: ‘The covenant between us and them is the prayer; whoever leaves it has committed kufr.’ So it is not permissible for a Muslim to marry a woman who does not pray, and it is not permissible for a Muslim woman to marry a man who does not pray.”
>
> — BinBaz.org.sa, [“الشروط الواجب توفرها في الزوجين”](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/10813/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%A8-%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%87%D8%A7-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%88%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%86)

IslamQA’s answer on the qualities to look for lists the practical signs of religion:

> “Religious commitment. This is the most important thing to look for in the man you want to marry. The husband should be a Muslim who adheres to all the laws and teachings of Islam in his daily life… One of the most important things to ask about is the man’s prayer; the one who neglects the rights of Allah is more likely to neglect the rights of others.”
>
> Source: IslamQA, [“Qualities to Look for in a Husband in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

## Chapter 4: Good Character, Gentleness, and Patience

Religion must be paired with good treatment. A man may pray, but if he is harsh, arrogant, or short-tempered, he will make married life miserable.

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“The best of you are the best to their wives, and I am the best of you to my family.”*
>
> — Narrated by at-Tirmidhi (3895); classed **hasan sahih** by at-Tirmidhi and al-Albani. Also narrated by Ibn Majah (1977). Source: IslamQA, [“Gifting Your Wife in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/323262)

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“Treat women kindly, for they were created from a rib, and the most crooked part of the rib is the top — if you try to straighten it you will break it, and if you leave it, it will remain crooked.”*
>
> — Agreed upon (al-Bukhari 5185, 5186; Muslim 1468). Source: Sunnah.com, [al-Bukhari 5185](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:5185)

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“No believing man should hate a believing woman. If he dislikes one of her characteristics, he may be pleased with another.”*
>
> — Sahih Muslim (1468b). Source: Sunnah.com, [Muslim 1468b](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:1468b)

Shaykh Ibn Baaz (may Allah have mercy on him) described how the husband should treat his wife:

> “And he also must treat her with good treatment (ma’ruf), so he should not harm her, nor beat her without right, nor be harsh with her without right, nor frown in her face without right, nor fall short in her spending without right. Rather, he must provide her customary spending for her like — clothing and other things — and be of good character, cheerful with his wife. He must treat her with gentleness in his intimacy with her, in his sleeping with her, in his speech to her with good words, joking with her, and being at ease with her, among other things… He has a right, and she has a right, and though his right is greater, he has a right upon him to treat her with good treatment and to be good to her.”
>
> — BinBaz.org.sa, [“حقوق كل من الزوجين على الآخر”](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/15561/%D8%AD%D9%82%D9%88%D9%82-%D9%83%D9%84-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%88%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%B1)

The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) encouraged spouses to strengthen love through small acts of kindness:

> “Exchange gifts; you will love one another.”
>
> — Narrated by al-Bukhari in *al-Adab al-Mufrad* (594); classed **sahih** by al-Albani in *Irwa’ al-Ghalil* (6/44). Source: IslamQA, [“Gifting Your Wife in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/323262)

Allah commands good treatment in clear terms:

> **Allah says:** *“And live with them in kindness. For if you dislike them — perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah makes therein much good.”* (Quran 4:19)
>
> Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) said: *“Live with them honorably, by saying kind words to them, treating them kindly and making your appearance appealing for them, as much as you can, just as you like the same from them.”* (Tafsir Ibn Kathir on 4:19.)
>
> Source: Alim.org, [Tafsir Ibn Kathir, Surah An-Nisa 4:19](https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/4/19/)

## Chapter 5: Ability to Provide and Maintain

Islam places the financial burden of the household on the man. A woman wants a man who can provide for her according to what is reasonable, not necessarily a millionaire.

> **Allah says:** *“Men are the protectors and maintainers of women, because Allah has made one of them to excel the other, and because they spend (to support them) from their means.”* (Quran 4:34)
>
> **Allah says:** *“And they (women) have rights (over their husbands) similar (to those of their husbands) over them to what is reasonable, but men have a degree (of responsibility) over them.”* (Quran 2:228)

Shaykh Ibn Baaz (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> “As Allah — blessed and exalted — stated: ‘Men are qawwamun (protectors/maintainers) over women because of what Allah has favored some over others and because of what they spend of their wealth’ [An-Nisa 4:34] — due to the favor of men over women, and because of what he spends of his wealth, and what he spends on her… so he is the maintainer over her.”
>
> — BinBaz.org.sa, [“توضيح حول قوامة الرجل على المرأة”](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/14079/%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%8A%D8%AD-%D8%AD%D9%88%D9%84-%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%AC%D9%84-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A3%D8%A9)

IslamQA explains that the woman should look for sufficient means:

> “It is good if he has sufficient wealth to keep him and his family from having to ask people for anything, because the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said to Fatimah bint Qays, when she came to consult him about three men who had proposed marriage to her, ‘As for Mu’awiyah, he is a poor man who has no wealth…’ (Narrated by Muslim, 1480). It is not essential that he should be a businessman or rich, it is sufficient for him to have an income that will keep him and his family from having to ask people for anything. If there is a choice between a man who is religiously committed and a man who is wealthy, then the religious man should be given preference over the wealthy man.”
>
> Source: IslamQA, [“Qualities to Look for in a Husband in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

A man who does not provide, or who is known for stinginess, is also a warning:

> Fatimah bint Qays asked the Prophet about Abu Jahm among the suitors, and he said: *“As for Abu Jahm, his stick never leaves his shoulder,”* referring to the fact that he used to beat women a lot. He also said about Mu’awiyah: *“He is a poor man who has no wealth.”*
>
> — Sahih Muslim (1480). Source: IslamQA, [“Qualities to Look for in a Husband in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

The wife’s basic rights are clear:

> “The rights of the wife of one of us are that you should feed her when you eat, clothe her when you clothe yourself, do not strike her face, do not insult her, and do not separate from her except in the house.”
>
> — Narrated by Abu Dawud (2142); classed **sahih** by al-Albani in *Irwa’ al-Ghalil* (7/98). Source: IslamQA, [“Gifting Your Wife in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/323262)

Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) said about maintenance:

> “That is, it should be in line with what is customary among women like her in her country, without any extravagance or stinting, and according to what the husband can afford, whether he is well off, of moderate means, or in financial difficulty.”
>
> — *Tafsir Ibn Kathir* 1/634, quoted in IslamQA [126316](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/126316)

## Chapter 6: Knowledge, Leadership, and Religious Responsibility

The husband is the shepherd of his household. A woman wants a man who can lead her and their children in knowledge and worship, not one who is ignorant or careless about the religion.

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“Each of you is a shepherd and each of you is responsible for his flock. A man is the shepherd of the members of his household and is responsible for them.”*
>
> — Agreed upon (al-Bukhari 7138; Muslim 1829). Source: Sunnah.com, [al-Bukhari 7138](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:7138)

IslamQA states:

> “It is preferable that he should have knowledge of the Quran and Sunnah; if you find someone like this it is good, otherwise you should realise that this is something rare.”
>
> — IslamQA, [“Qualities to Look for in a Husband in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

And about his responsibility:

> “It is quite clear that a man is responsible for his family with regard to teaching them and making sure that they follow Islam… Whoever neglects his wife and children in this regard is undoubtedly taking a great risk.”
>
> — IslamQA, [“The husband’s responsibility to educate his wife”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/13802)

## Chapter 7: Family, Health, and Appearance

After religion, a known good family, sound body, and a permissible level of physical attraction may be considered. These must never be made more important than deen.

> “As well as being religiously committed, it is preferable that he should come from a good family and a known lineage. If two men come to propose marriage to one woman, and they are equal in terms of religious commitment, then preference should be given to the one who comes from a good family that is known for its adherence to the commands of Allah… because the righteousness of the husband’s close relatives could be passed on to his children.”
>
> — IslamQA, [“Qualities to Look for in a Husband in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

IslamQA also mentions:

> “It is good if he is of sound body and health, free of faults, sickness, etc., and not disabled or sterile.”
>
> — IslamQA, [“Qualities to Look for in a Husband in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) permitted a suitor and a woman to see one another so that attraction could be a basis for lasting love:

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“Go and look at her, for that is more likely to create love between you.”*
>
> — Narrated by at-Tirmidhi (1087), an-Nasa’i (3235), and Ibn Majah (1865); classed **sahih** by al-Albani in *Sahih Sunan at-Tirmidhi*. Source: IslamQA, [“Method of Marriage Proposal in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/88130)

Shaykh Ibn ‘Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him) explained that physical and spiritual beauty both play a role, but spiritual beauty is the lasting one:

> “Physical beauty means the perfection of features, for the more the woman appears beautiful and articulate, the more the eye is comforted by looking at her… On the other hand, spiritual beauty means the perfection of religion and moral character. The more the woman is religious and perfect in moral character, the lovelier she becomes, and the safer the expected outcome is… A religious woman always complies with the commands of Allah, guards her husband’s rights, honor, children and wealth, and helps him obey Allah whenever she reminds him.”
>
> — Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-‘Uthaymeen, *Kitab az-Zawaj*, quoted in IslamWeb, [“A Woman Is Married for Four Reasons”](https://islamweb.net/en/article/163819)

## Chapter 8: Honor, Protectiveness, and Lowering the Gaze

A righteous husband guards his wife’s honor and his own chastity. He is protective (has ghayrah) and does not permit free mixing or indecency.

> **Allah says:** *“Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and be mindful of their chastity. That is purer for them.”* (Quran 24:30)
>
> **Allah says:** *“Men are the protectors and maintainers of women, because Allah has made one of them to excel the other, and because they spend (to support them) from their means. Therefore the righteous women are devoutly obedient, and guard in the husband’s absence what Allah would have them guard.”* (Quran 4:34)

Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan (may Allah preserve him) said:

> “The prophet (sallâ Allâhu ‘alayhi wa sallam) ordered us to take good care of the women because they play an important role in the society. It’s not only about taking good care of them and giving them their due rights… this is obligatory, what is more important is to protect them from indecencies – the woman is weak as you know – and [to protect them from] trying appearances [i.e., fitnah] and letting them do whatever they want to do. This is the most important thing the prophet ordered [in regards to women], namely that we protect them.”
>
> — Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan, AbdurRahman.org, [“A Message to the Woman — Lecture by Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan”](https://abdurrahman.org/2012/02/12/a-message-to-the-woman-lecture-by-shaykh-salih-al-fawzan-must-see/)

A woman should want a man who understands that his qawamah is responsibility, not tyranny. He is responsible for guarding her from fitnah, not for oppressing her.

## Chapter 9: Intimacy, Justice, and Marriage Conditions

The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) warned against oppressing a wife. This includes withholding intimacy and being unjust between co-wives.

> **Allah says:** *“You will never be able to do perfect justice between wives even if it is your ardent desire. So do not incline altogether [toward one] and leave another as if suspended.”* (Quran 4:129)

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) stated the wife’s right to intimacy:

> “The husband must render his wife her conjugal rights in a goodly manner (bil-ma’ruf). This is one of her most crucial rights. It is more important than even feeding her. Regarding how often it is obligatory, some have said once every four months, and others have said that it depends on her need and his ability, and this is the more correct position.”
>
> — Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, *Majmu’ al-Fatawa*, cited in IslamQA [143120](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/143120)

A woman may also stipulate conditions that protect her rights, such as that the husband will not take another wife or will not remove her from her home. Such conditions are valid according to the understanding of Imam Ahmad and many of the Salaf.

> “Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyah (may Allah have mercy on him) was asked about a man who married a woman, and she stipulated that he should not take another wife or make her move from her home… He replied: Yes, these conditions and similar ones are valid according to the madhhab of Imam Ahmad and others among the Sahaabah and Taabi’een.”
>
> — Ibn Taymiyyah, *al-Fatawa al-Kubra* 3/90, quoted in IslamQA [143120](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/143120)

> The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) said: *“The conditions that are most deserving to be fulfilled are those by means of which intimacy becomes permissible for you.”*
>
> — Agreed upon (al-Bukhari 2721; Muslim 1418). Source: IslamQA [143120](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/143120)

## Chapter 10: What to Avoid in a Suitor

The woman should not only look for positive qualities; she should also beware of red flags. Shaykh Ibn Baaz (may Allah have mercy on him) listed the following:

> “She should strive for him to be righteous and good, far from causes of evil — not a drunkard, not one who shaves his beard, not a smoker, not one who commits adultery, and the like. She should strive for him to be sound; this is what is required — out of concern for the safety of religion.”
>
> — BinBaz.org.sa, [“الشروط الواجب توفرها في الزوجين”](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/10813/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%A8-%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%87%D8%A7-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%88%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%86)

The Prophet (blessings and peace of Allah be upon him) warned Fatimah bint Qays about Abu Jahm, who used to beat women:

> *“As for Abu Jahm, his stick never leaves his shoulder.”*
>
> — Sahih Muslim (1480). Source: IslamQA [5202](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

A man who pressures the woman into marriage, or a guardian who forces her, is not a legitimate match:

> “Neither the father, or anyone other than the father, may force a woman who is under his guardianship to marry a man that she does not wish to marry. In fact her permission must be sought first… If a lady is married without her permission, then the marriage will not be correct.”
>
> — Shaykh ‘Abd al-‘Aziz ibn Baz, AbdurRahman.org, [“Questions Concerning Everyday Issues: Issue 4”](https://abdurrahman.org/2015/02/18/daily-issues-4/)

## Chapter 11: Her Rights in the Process — Consent, Wali, Mahr, and Conditions

### Her consent

> “A previously married woman (without a husband) must not be married until she is consulted, and a virgin is not married until her permission is sought.” They said: O Messenger of Allah, how is her permission sought? So he said: *“By her being silent.”*
>
> — Sahih Muslim (1421). Source: Sunnah.com, [Muslim 1421](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:1421)

### Her guardian

> “It is not permissible for the woman to marry herself off or others whether she is a virgin or previously married… if the woman was in such description, from deficiency of intellect and religion, then she is in need of a guiding guardian who knows the appropriate/capable and knows the interests of marriage.”
>
> — Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-‘Uthaymeen, AbdurRahman.org, [“Can a Woman get Married without a Wali (Guardian)?”](https://abdurrahman.org/2015/04/17/can-a-woman-get-married-without-a-wali-guardian-imam-ibn-uthaymeen/)

### Her mahr

> **Allah says:** *“And give to the women (whom you marry) their mahr (obligatory bridal gift) with a good heart. But if they, of their own good pleasure, remit any part of it, take it, and enjoy it without fear of any harm.”* (Quran 4:4)

Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> “The man must definitely pay the dowry to the woman, and he should not resent it.”
>
> — *Tafsir Ibn Kathir*, quoted in IslamQA [2378](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/2378)

Shaykh Ibn Baaz (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> “What is prescribed in the Sharee’ah is that the mahr should be a small amount and that people should not compete with each other in this regard… And it is not permissible for the awliyaa (guardians of the brides-to-be) to demand and stipulate gifts for themselves, since they have no right at all in this matter. Rather the right belongs to the woman.”
>
> — AbdurRahman.org, [“Mahr (Dowry) should be a small amount”](https://abdurrahman.org/2009/12/29/mahr-dowry-should-be-a-small-amount-and-that-people-should-not-compete/)

### Her right to look and be informed

> “It is permissible for the woman to look at the man who comes to propose marriage, and for him to look at her. This should be in the presence of her mahram, and it is not permitted to look more than is necessary, or for him to see her alone, or for her to go out with him on his own, or to meet repeatedly for no reason.”
>
> — IslamQA, [“Qualities to Look for in a Husband in Islam”](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5202)

### Do not prevent a divorced woman from remarrying

> **Allah says:** *“Do not prevent them from remarrying their (former) husbands, if they mutually agree to reasonable terms.”* (Quran 2:232)

## Conclusion

The qualities a good Muslim woman wants in a husband are not a checklist of culture, race, or wealth. They are the qualities that Allah and His Messenger made the foundation of marriage: religious commitment, good character, the ability and willingness to provide, knowledge and leadership, kindness and protection, and respect for her rights. When these are present, the marriage is built on the mercy and tranquility that Allah described:

> **Allah says:** *“And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves spouses, that you may find tranquility in them; and He placed between you affection and mercy. Indeed in that are signs for a people who reflect.”* (Quran 30:21)

May Allah guide every Muslim woman to a righteous husband and every man to be the righteous husband she seeks.
