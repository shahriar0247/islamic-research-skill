## I. Issues That Should Be Corrected

### 1. Publishing Your Phone Number and Telegram Publicly

You wrote your phone number (`+1 942 380 1174`) and Telegram handle (`@salafi73`) at the top of the bio. Based on the research, this is problematic for several reasons:

**IslamQA** (Fatwa no. 85099) laid down guidelines for matrimonial websites, including:

> "3 – It should not allow any correspondence between the two sexes, because of the evils that result from that, including the participation of mischief-makers both male and female whose intention is to do evil or have fun. **Rather the administrators of the site should first check on the identity of the suitor, then put him in touch with the guardian (wali) of the woman.**"

> "Secondly: You should seek the help of your family and friends, and those who are in charge of Islamic centres, in looking for a righteous wife, in your homeland or in the place where you are staying. **This is easy, praise be to Allaah, and it is safer and better than doing that via the internet.**"

**IslamWeb** stated:

> "The safest way is to seek a husband/wife through matchmakers."

**The issue:** Posting your direct contact publicly bypasses the role of the *wali* (guardian) and intermediaries, opens the door to direct unmonitored communication between non-mahram men and women (which the scholars have strongly warned against — see the hadith "No man is alone with a woman except that Satan is the third," reported by at-Tirmidhi 2165, graded sahih by al-Albani), and exposes your personal information to anyone — including mischief-makers.

**Recommendation:** Remove your phone number and Telegram from the public bio. If you use a matrimonial service, it should route contact **through the wali of the prospective wife**, not directly to you. If you are using a mosque or a trusted shaykh/brother as an intermediary, give your contact details only to them.

### 2. Self-Praise and Boasting

Several parts of your bio contain self-praise, which the scholars have warned against. Consider these statements:

- *"I know and have fixed quite a lot of my mistakes... I fixed most of them I think."*
- *"I learnt about being a good husband a lot, and I am always learning, on how to raise a child, advise a teen, etc."*
- *"Alhumduillah I make enough."*
- *"I have several clients and business opportunities which I am chasing and they are working out."*

**The Prophet ﷺ said:**

> "Do not praise yourselves, for Allaah knows best who among you is righteous."

(Sahih Muslim, 2142)

**The Prophet ﷺ also said:**

> "Beware of praising one another, for it is like slaughter (i.e., fatal)."

(Reported by Ahmad, 16460; **graded saheeh by al-Albani** in *Saheeh al-Jaami'*, 2674)

**Ibn al-Qayyim** (may Allah have mercy on him) warned about the oppression of "I – My/Mine – I Possess":

> "Every Muslim should be warned of the oppression of: 'I – My/Mine – I Possess.' Indeed these three words are what Iblees, Fir'awn and Qaroon were tried with."

(*Za'ad al-Ma'ad*, 2/475)

**Al-Fudayl ibn 'Iyaad** (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "If you are able not to be known, then do so. What harm will it do you if you are not known? What harm will it do you if no one praises you?"

**The issue:** While it is appropriate to mention your religious practice, your occupation, and your situation truthfully, the tone in several places leans toward self-congratulation — listing accomplishments, emphasizing that you "make enough," and that your business ventures "are working out." This contradicts the humility the scholars praised.

**Recommendation:** State facts plainly and let others praise you. For example:
- Instead of *"Alhumduillah I make enough. I have several clients and business opportunities which I am chasing and they are working out"* — say: *"I work freelance in software/web development and marketing, alhamdulillah, and am able to provide for a family."*
- Instead of *"I have fixed quite a lot of my mistakes... I fixed most of them I think"* — say: *"I have reflected on my previous marriage and sought to learn from it, with the help of a shaykh."*

Let your deen and character speak through the wali's verification, not through your own praise of yourself.

### 3. Disclosing Intimate Details of the Previous Marriage

You wrote:

> *"I am divorced but bearly 1 year living together. I know and have fixed quite a lot of my mistakes which first timers make, and have taken marriage councelling where Shaykh would tell me all the problems about me and I fixed most of them I think."*

**IslamQA** (Fatwa no. 2021) distinguished between:
- **Genuine defects** that affect marriage (e.g., impotence, disease) — these **must** be disclosed.
- **Past sins and private matters** — these **must be concealed**, not publicized.

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "All of my Ummah will be forgiven except those who commit sin openly."

(Reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim; **graded saheeh by al-Albani** in *al-Silsilah al-Saheehah*, no. 663)

**Abu Khadeejah** (abukhadeejah.com) explained the distinction: a person must disclose genuine defects that would affect the validity or quality of the marriage, but must **conceal** past sins and the intimate/private details of a previous marriage.

**The issue:** Mentioning that you went to marriage counselling and that "the shaykh would tell me all the problems about me" — this is disclosing private matters that should be concealed. It is sufficient to say you are divorced, that the marriage was brief (under a year), and that you have reflected and sought to improve. The specific problems, counselling details, and self-assessment of "fixing most of them" should not be in a public bio.

**Recommendation:**
> *"I am divorced (talaaq). The marriage was brief — under a year, alhamdulillah no children. I have reflected on the experience and sought knowledge and advice to be a better husband, inshaAllah."*

This is truthful, discloses the relevant facts (divorced, brief, no children), and avoids exposing private matters.

### 4. The Tone of "Respects and Obeys Me" in the Spouse Preference

You wrote:

> *"respects and obeys me, and I will be kind to her."*

While it is true that Allah has placed the husband as a *qawwam* (protector and maintainer) and that the wife's obedience is established in the religion, **the way this is phrased in a marriage bio is problematic** for several reasons:

**(a) It presents the wife's duty without the husband's corresponding duties.**

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "My advice to all husbands is that they fear Allah with regard to their women, and that they treat them with gentleness and good treatment, as Allah — the Mighty and Majestic — said: *'And live with them in kindness'* [An-Nisa:19] — with good speech, good style, forbearance, noble character, and fulfilling their rights in full. This is the obligation upon all husbands."

> "The fact that your right is greater — you having a degree of increase — does not mean this degree entails that you mistreat her or oppress her. No, it is obligatory upon you to have good character, to live with them in kindness, and to fulfill the obligation — in terms of the necessary maintenance (nafaqah) in a good manner, and in terms of good speech and good address."

(https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/13285/%D9%86%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%AD%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AD%D8%B3%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%88%D8%AC-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%B2%D9%88%D8%AC%D8%AA%D9%87)

**(b) The Prophet ﷺ emphasized being good to wives as the standard of a man's excellence.**

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "The best of you is the one who is best to his wives, and I am the best of you to my wives."

(Sunan at-Tirmidhi, 3895; **graded sahih by al-Albani** in *Sahih Sunan at-Tirmidhi*)

And:

> "The most complete of the Believers in their Eemaan are those who have the best manners, and the best of you are those who are best to their women."

(**Graded sahih by al-Albani** in *Silsilah Sahihah*, no. 284)

**(c) The scholars balance qawwamah with kindness, consultation, and the wife's rights.**

**Ibn Taymiyyah** (may Allah have mercy on him) explained that the husband's authority is tied to his responsibility of maintenance and good treatment, not absolute control.

(*Majmoo' al-Fataawa*, 32/260-261)

**The issue:** Leading your spouse preference with *"respects and obeys me"* — before mentioning anything about how you will treat her — comes across as demanding and one-sided. A righteous sister reading this may be put off, not because obedience is wrong, but because the framing emphasizes your right while minimizing your heavy obligations. The scholars always present the wife's obedience **together with** the husband's obligation of good treatment, maintenance, gentleness, and consultation.

**Recommendation:** Reframe to reflect the balance the scholars established:

> *"I seek a sister who adheres to the Quran and Sunnah upon the understanding of the Salaf, who respects her husband as Allah has commanded. In return, I fear Allah with regard to her — I will treat her with kindness, good character, and gentleness, maintain her as Allah has obligated, consult her in our affairs, and support her in her religious and worldly goals, inshaAllah."*

This states the same Islamic reality but in the balanced, dignified manner the scholars used.

### 5. Specifying the Wife's Activities, Friends, and Hobbies as Conditions

You wrote:

> *"I will let her do everything if I find good in it inShaAllah, university, classes, teaching others (maybe online), volunterring in masjid, i will help her find good practicing muslim friends and online groups, someone who has hobbies and things she likes to do in her own times."*

**Note:** No specific Salafi fatwa was found on "specifying a wife's hobbies and friends as conditions in a marriage bio." This is marked honestly.

However, general principles from the scholars apply:

**IslamQA** (Fatwa no. 22397) on a wife studying/working/teaching: these are **permissible with the husband's permission**, with conditions including no mixing, proper hijab, and not neglecting household duties.

**IslamQA** on valid and invalid marriage conditions: a condition that contradicts the Shariah is invalid; a condition that is permissible and agreed upon by both parties is valid.

**The issue:** The phrasing *"I will let her do everything if I find good in it"* — while technically reflecting the husband's role of approval — reads as controlling when stated in a bio *before* marriage has even occurred. It presumes authority over a woman who is not yet your wife and frames her autonomy as something you *grant* rather than something she naturally has within the bounds of the Shariah.

Additionally, *"I will help her find good practicing muslim friends and online groups"* — while well-intentioned, this comes across as managing her social life. A righteous sister with her own wali, community, and halaqas may find this presumptuous.

**Recommendation:** Express your support without framing it as control:

> *"I support my wife in seeking knowledge, studying, teaching, and contributing to the community within the bounds of the Shariah, inshaAllah. I would encourage her to maintain good company and pursue her interests, and I hope we can grow together in deen."*

## III. Structural and Tone Recommendations

### 1. Lead With Deen, Not Worldly Details

Your bio currently leads with name, phone, Telegram, gender, height, age, build, beard, nationality, ethnicity, occupation, education, and languages — **before** any mention of your religious practice.

**IslamQA** (Fatwa no. 5202) listed the qualities a wali should look for in a husband, in order:
1. **Religious commitment** (most important)
2. Good character
3. Good family/lineage
4. Sufficient wealth to maintain
5. Kindness and gentleness
6. Sound health
7. Knowledge of Quran and Sunnah

**Recommendation:** Restructure your bio so that your religious practice, prayer, seeking of knowledge, and character come **first**. Then mention your worldly situation (occupation, education, nationality) as supporting information. This reflects the priority the scholars gave.

---

### 2. Reduce the Word Count and Be More Concise

Your "Brief description about you" exceeds the spirit of the 150-word limit (it is approximately 170+ words) and your preference section is also long. Scholars praised conciseness and avoiding excessive speech.

**Recommendation:** Trim both sections to be concise, dignified, and focused on deen and character. Let the wali and intermediaries fill in the details through direct inquiry.
