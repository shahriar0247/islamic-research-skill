## 1. Is Jumu'ah Obligatory Upon You?

### The Quranic Command

Allah (the Most High) said:

> **"O you who believe (Muslims)! When the call is proclaimed for the Salat (prayer) on the day of Friday (Jumu'ah prayer), come to the remembrance of Allah [Jumu'ah religious talk (Khutbah) and Salat (prayer)] and leave off business (and every other thing), that is better for you if you did but know!"**
> (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:9)

> **"Then when the (Jumu'ah) Salat (prayer) is finished, you may disperse through the land, and seek the Bounty of Allah (by working, etc.), and remember Allah much, that you may be successful."**
> (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:10)

**Imam Ibn Kathir** (may Allah have mercy on him) explained the meaning of "hasten" (fas'aw):

> "O you who believe! When the call is proclaimed for the Salah on Al-Jumu'ah (Friday), then hasten (Fas'aw) to the remembrance of Allah" meaning, go to it and head for it. The meaning of Sa'y (hasten) here does not refer to walking quickly. It only refers to the importance of it. 'Umar bin Al-Khattab and Ibn Mas'ud - may Allah be pleased with them - recited it; 'Then proceed to the remembrance of Allah.' As for walking in haste to the prayer, that was indeed prohibited, since it was recorded in the Two Sahihs from Abu Hurayrah that the Prophet said, 'When you hear the Iqamah, proceed to offer the prayer with calmness and solemnity and do not rush. And pray whatever you catch, and complete whatever you have missed.'"
> — *(Tafsir Ibn Kathir, Surah al-Jumu'ah 62:9)*

**Shaykh 'Abdur-Rahman as-Sa'di** (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "Allah, the Most High, commanded His believing servants to be present for the Jumu'ah prayer, and to hasten to it and strive for it when the call is made. Here 'sa'y' (hastening) means to be eager, to give importance to the Jumu'ah prayer and to make it the most important matter — it does not mean running, which is prohibited when going to prayer."
> — *(Tayseer al-Kareem ar-Rahman fi Tafsir Kalam al-Mannan, Surah al-Jumu'ah 62:9)*

As-Sa'di also derived key rulings from these verses:

> "From these verses there are several benefits: Among them: that Jumu'ah is an obligation (fard) upon all believers, it is obligatory upon them to hasten to it, and to be eager and concerned with it. Among them: that the two khutbahs on the day of Jumu'ah are two obligations that must be attended, because He explained 'dhikr' here to mean the two khutbahs, and Allah commanded to proceed to it and hasten to it. Among them: the prohibition of buying and selling after the call for Jumu'ah, and its prohibition — and that is only because it causes one to miss the obligation and distracts from it."
> — *(Tafsir as-Sa'di, Surah al-Jumu'ah 62:9)*

### The Four Conditions: Who Is Jumu'ah Obligatory Upon?

Jumu'ah is obligatory upon every **free, adult, sane, resident Muslim male**. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "The Friday prayer in congregation is an obligatory duty upon every Muslim, with the exception of four: a slave, a woman, a child and a sick person."
> — *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1067; al-Albani: Sahih)*

**Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan** (may Allah preserve him) said:

> "Congregational prayer in the mosque is an obligation upon the sane, adult males who are able to attend. This is the most correct saying of the scholars."
> — *(Ithaaful-Qaaree bit-Ta`leeqaat `alaa Sharhis-Sunnah, annotation of Sharh as-Sunnah by Imam al-Barbahaaree)*

And commenting on the statement of Imam al-Barbahaaree:

> "Whoever abandons the Jumu'ah Prayer and the congregational prayers in the mosque, without a valid excuse, then he is an innovator. The valid excuse is such as an illness, because of which he is unable to go out to the mosque, or fear of an oppressive ruler and whatever is besides that then there is no excuse for you."
> — *(Sharh as-Sunnah, Point 128, annotated by Shaykh al-Fawzan)*

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "Whoever did not attend the Friday prayer with the Muslims due to some lawful reason, such as illness or the like, or some other reasons, should pray the Zuhr prayer. Likewise, a woman prays the Zuhr prayer and so does the traveller and those living in the desert, as is proved by the Sunnah, and it is the opinion of the majority of the scholars, and no consideration should be paid to those who deviate from them."
> — *(Fatawa Islamiyah, Vol. 2, Page 448)*

### Those Who Are Excused: Workers on Fridays

**Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin** (may Allah have mercy on him) was asked about those who work on Fridays:

> "If their attendance of the Friday prayer causes a major loss, then those who are directly responsible for the work needed are excused for missing the Friday prayer. They pray zuhr instead. However, this should be limited to the minimum number of employees who can accomplish what is required. They should take turns: this Friday for so-and-so and next Friday for so-and-so. As for if no loss will occur, but rather, one will miss out on profits, then it is wajib for them to attend the Friday prayer."
> — *(Majmu' Rasa'il wa Fatawa al-'Uthaymin, 16/37)*

### The Severe Warning Against Missing Three Jumu'ahs

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Whoever neglects the Friday prayer three times (in a row) without an excuse, then Allah sets a seal upon his heart."
> — *(Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Hadith 500; al-Albani: Hasan Sahih)*

Also narrated:

> "Whoever missed three jumu'ahs out of negligence, Allah will place a seal over his heart."
> — *(Sunan an-Nasa'i, Hadith 1369; al-Albani: Hasan Sahih)*

> "He who leaves the Friday prayer (continuously) for three Friday on account of slackness, Allah will print a stamp on his heart."
> — *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1052; al-Albani: Hasan Sahih)*

> "The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) said: 'Whoever abandons Friday (prayer) three times, for no necessary reason, Allah will place a seal over his heart.'"
> — *(Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1126; al-Albani: Hasan)*

And from Ibn 'Umar and Abu Hurairah:

> "People must cease to neglect the Friday prayer or Allah will seal their hearts and then they will be among the negligent."
> — *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 865; al-Albani: Sahih)*

## 2. The Mandatory Acts (Fard) of Jumu'ah

### Attending When the Call Is Made

As established above, Allah commanded: "When the call is proclaimed for the prayer on the day of Friday, come to the remembrance of Allah and leave off business" (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:9). This makes attendance obligatory when the adhan is called.

**Shaykh al-Fawzan** explained that attending Jumu'ah does not reduce one's provision — it increases it:

> "So the prayer, which some people claim takes time away from them seeking after the rizq (sustenance) and from buying and selling, is in fact the opposite of what they claim. It opens for them the door of provision, comfort and blessing. This is because the sustenance (rizq) is in the Hand of Allaah. So if you were to turn your attention towards remembering Him and worshipping Him, He would facilitate and open the door of sustenance for you. 'And Allaah is the best of Providers.' [Surah Al-Jumu'ah: 11]"
> — *(Forbidden Business Transactions, al-Ibaanah eBook)*

### Leaving Off All Trade After the Second Adhan

**Shaykh al-Fawzan** established that trade becomes impermissible after the second adhan (the adhan called when the imam is present and about to deliver the khutbah):

> "O you who believe (Muslims)! When the call is proclaimed for the Salat (prayer) on the day of Friday (Jumu'ah prayer), come to the remembrance of Allah [Jumu'ah religious talk (Khutbah) and Salat (prayer)] and leave off business (and every other thing), that is better for you if you did but know!"
> — *(Citing Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:9-10)*

### The Two Khutbahs Are a Condition for Validity

**Shaykh as-Sa'di** explicitly stated:

> "Among them [the benefits of these verses]: that the two khutbahs on the day of Jumu'ah are two obligations (fard) that must be attended, because He explained 'dhikr' here to mean the two khutbahs, and Allah commanded to proceed to it and hasten to it."
> — *(Tafsir as-Sa'di, Surah al-Jumu'ah 62:9)*

**Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin** said:

> "That it be preceded by two sermons; since the Prophet ﷺ always did that. The two sermons are obligatory and they are a precondition for the validity of (Salat al-Jumu'ah)."
> — *(As cited in Summary of Fiqh)*

**Shaykh al-Albani** (may Allah have mercy on him) described the legislated khutbah:

> "The Messenger of Allaah –sallAllaahu alayhi wa sallam– used to deliver two Khutbahs and sit between them, he would also recite the Qur'aan, remind the people and warn them."
> — *(al-Ajwiba an-Nafiah, pp. 94-99)*

### Listening Attentively to the Khutbah

Listening to the khutbah is obligatory. The evidence is the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ:

> "When the Imam is delivering the Khutba, and you ask your companion to keep quiet and listen, then no doubt you have done an evil act."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 934; al-Albani: Sahih)*

**Shaykh al-Albani** laid down a general principle about listening to the Qur'an and gatherings of knowledge:

> "If the circumstance is a gathering of knowledge, remembrance and recitation of the Qur'an, then in this situation it is obligatory to pay complete attention to it. And whoever doesn't do so is sinning because he is disobeying Allah's statement in the Qur'an: 'And when the Qur'an is recited, then listen to it and remain silent, in order that you may receive mercy.' [Surah Al-A'raaf: 204]"
> — *(Playing the Qur'aan on tape while not Listening to it)*

> **Note:** This principle of obligatory silence and listening when the Qur'an or knowledge is being recited/delivered applies even more so to the Jumu'ah khutbah, which is a gathering of knowledge, remembrance, and recitation of Qur'an.

### The Reward of Listening and Remaining Silent

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "If anyone performs Wudu' properly, then comes to the Friday prayer, listens to the Khutbah (religious talk) attentively and keeps silent, his (minor) sins between that Friday and the following Friday will be forgiven, with the addition of three more days; but he who touches pebbles has caused an interruption."
> — *(Sahih Muslim; Riyad as-Salihin, Hadith 1148; al-Albani: Sahih)*

And the hadith of Salman al-Farsi:

> "Whoever takes a bath on Friday, purifies himself as much as he can, then uses his (hair) oil or perfumes himself with the scent of his house, then proceeds (for the Jumua prayer) and does not separate two persons sitting together (in the mosque), then prays as much as (Allah has) written for him and then remains silent while the Imam is delivering the Khutba, his sins in-between the present and the last Friday would be forgiven."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 883; al-Albani: Sahih)*

### The Two Rak'ahs of Jumu'ah Prayer

Jumu'ah consists of **two rak'ahs** prayed in congregation behind the imam, with **recitation aloud**.

**Shaykh al-Albani** said:

> "He should recite aloud in the Dawn (Fajr) Prayer, the Jumu'ah Prayer, the two 'Eid Prayers, the Prayer for seeking rain, the Eclipse Prayer and in the first two rak'ahs of the Maghrib and 'Ishaa Prayers."
> — *(The Abridgement of the Prophet's Prayer Described)*

The Prophet ﷺ used to recite specific surahs in the Jumu'ah prayer:

> "The Messenger of Allaah –sallAllaahu alayhi wa sallam– used to pray the Jumma' prayer reciting Soorah al-Jumma' and Soorah al-Munafiqoon, and likewise as is collected by Muslim from Ibn Abbas and an-Num'aan bin Basheer –RadhiAllaahu anhumma: 'The Messenger –sallAllaahu alayhi wa sallam– used to recite in the two Eid prayers and in the Jumma' prayer with Soorah al-Ala and al-Ghashiyah.'"
> — *(Ajwibaa an-Nafi'ah by Shaykh al-Albani, p. 57)*

The specific narration from Sahih Muslim:

> "Abu Hurairah led us in prayer on Friday, and he recited Surat Al-Jumu'ah in the first Rak'ah, and in the second, 'When the hypocrites come to you,' [Al-Munafiqun (63)]." ... Abu Hurairah said: "I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ reciting them."
> — *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 877a; al-Albani: Sahih)*

### Catching at Least One Rak'ah

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Whoever could get one rak'ah of a prayer, (in its proper time) he has got the prayer."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 580; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 607; al-Albani: Sahih)*

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "Whoever caught a Rak'ah of the Jumu'ah prayer, then he caught the Jumu'ah prayer, as is reported in an authentic Hadith from the Messenger of Allah. [...] This is according to the generality of the prophet's statement: 'Whoever caught a Rak'ah of the prayer, then verily he caught the prayer.' (Al-Bukhari no. 580 and Muslim no. 607)"
> — *(Fatawa Islamiyah, vol. 3, pp. 24-25)*

## 3. What Invalidates Your Jumu'ah

### Speaking During the Khutbah

As will be detailed in Section 4, speaking during the khutbah — even telling someone to be quiet — is considered "laghw" (idle talk) and is prohibited. It may cause your Jumu'ah to be lost of its reward.

### Trading After the Second Adhan

**Shaykh al-Fawzan** established that trade becomes impermissible after the second adhan, citing (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:9-10).

### Missing Three Jumu'ahs Out of Negligence

As cited above, missing three Jumu'ahs out of negligence results in Allah sealing the heart — among the most severe warnings in the religion.

### Giving Salaam and Shaking Hands During the Khutbah

**Shaykh Ibn Baz and the Permanent Committee** said:

> "He should shake him by the hand and not speak, then he should return the salaam (verbally) to him after the first khutbah has ceased. This is if he gave you salaam during the course of the first khutbah. If however he gave you salaam during the course of the Imaam delivering the second khutbah then you should give him the reply to his salaam after the Imaam has finished from the second khutbah."
> — *(Fataawaa Al-Lajnah ad-Daa'imah, vol. 8, page 246, question 2 of fatwa number 3774)*

### Touching Pebbles (Fidgeting) During the Khutbah

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "...but he who touches pebbles has caused an interruption."
> — *(Sahih Muslim; Riyad as-Salihin, Hadith 1148; al-Albani: Sahih)*

## 4. Talking During the Khutbah — The Core Prohibition

### The Primary Hadith

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "When the Imam is delivering the Khutba, and you ask your companion to keep quiet and listen, then no doubt you have done an evil act."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 934; al-Albani: Sahih)*

> "If you (even) ask your companion to be quiet on Friday while the Imam is delivering the sermon, you have in fact talked irrelevance."
> — *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 851a; al-Albani: Sahih)*

> "When you tell (your brother on Friday) to be silent while the imam is giving the sermon you are guilty of idle talk."
> — *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1112; al-Albani: Sahih)*

**Al-Albani's grading** (from dorar.net):

> خلاصة حكم المحدث: **صحيح** (Sahih)
> الراوي: أبو هريرة | المحدث: الألباني | المصدر: صحيح الجامع الصفحة أو الرقم: 6432
> التخريج: أخرجه البخاري (934)، ومسلم (851)، وأبو داود (1112)، والترمذي (512)، والنسائي (1401) واللفظ له، وابن ماجه (1110)، وأحمد (7332)

**Translation:** Summary of the hadith scholar's ruling: **Sahih (Authentic)**. Narrator: Abu Hurairah | Grading scholar: al-Albani | Source: Sahih al-Jami', number 6432. Reported by al-Bukhari (934), Muslim (851), Abu Dawud (1112), al-Tirmidhi (512), al-Nasa'i (1401), Ibn Majah (1110), and Ahmad (7332).

### The Hadith of the Donkey Carrying Books

> "Whoever speaks on Friday while the Imam is giving the Khutbah (religious talk), he is like a donkey which carries books; and he who tells him to be quiet has no Jumu'ah."
> — *(Reported by Ahmad with an acceptable chain of narrators; Bulugh al-Maram)*

> **Note on grading:** This hadith is graded **da'if (weak)** by Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut due to Majlad bin Sa'id being in the chain. However, Ibn Hajar stated in Bulugh al-Maram: "There is no fault in its chain" (لا بأس بإسناده). The hadith is also referenced in the Quranic verse: (Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah [62]:5) — "The likeness of those who were entrusted with the Taurat but did not carry it is like that of a donkey which carries huge burdens of books."

### The Hadith of Abu al-Darda' and Ubayy ibn Ka'b

> 'Ata' bin Yasar narrated from Ubayy bin Ka'b: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ recited Tabarak [Al-Mulk (67)] one Friday, while he was standing and reminding us of the Days of Allah (i.e., preaching to us). Abu Darda' or Abu Dharr raised an eyebrow at me and said: 'When was this Surah revealed? For I have not heard it before now.' He (Ubayy) gestured to him that he should remain silent. When they finished, he said: 'I asked you when this Surah was revealed and you did not answer me.' Ubayy said: 'You have gained nothing from your prayer today except the idle talk that you engaged in.' He went to the Prophet ﷺ and told him about that, and what Ubayy had said to him. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'Ubayy spoke the truth.'"
> — *(Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1111)*

### Ibn Baz's Comprehensive Fatwa on Talking During the Khutbah

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** (may Allah have mercy on him) gave a comprehensive ruling:

> "It is not permissible to speak while the imam is delivering the khutbah to the people — neither to someone who sneezes nor to anyone else. The obligation is to remain silent to listen to the khatib, except speaking to the khatib — there is no harm... Likewise in the khutbah, the one listening to it is like the one praying — he does not speak, does not return the salaam, does not initiate the salaam, and does not respond to the one who sneezes. This is what is obligatory upon him, due to the saying of the Prophet (ﷺ): 'If you say to your companion: "Be quiet" on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah, you have engaged in laghw' — even though he was enjoining good, he called him one who engages in vain speech (laghw), while he was one who enjoins good. And in the other hadith: 'Whoever speaks on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah is like a donkey carrying books, and the one who says to him "be quiet" has no Jumu'ah.' This indicates that the Jumu'ah is nullified and its reward is lost due to his speaking during the khutbah, even if his speech is about something good — enjoining good, forbidding evil, or responding to a sneeze. The obligation is to leave that, just as it is obligatory to leave that with the one praying, likewise with the one listening to the khutbah."
> — *(Fatwa: "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)*

And from his Majmu' al-Fatawa (vol. 30):

> "The obligation is to remain silent and not speak — neither with supplication nor with anything else — during the khutbah, due to the saying of the Prophet (ﷺ): 'If you say to your companion: "Be quiet" on Friday while the imam is delivering the khutbah, you have engaged in laghw' — agreed upon its authenticity. So he called forbidding evil with speech while the imam is delivering the khutbah 'laghw' (vain speech), so how about other than that of speech?"
> — *(Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol. 30)*

### The Three Types of People Who Attend Jumu'ah

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Three types of people attend Friday prayer; One is present in a frivolous way and that is all he gets from it; another comes with a supplication, Allah may grant or refuse his request as He wishes; another is present silently and quietly without stepping over a Muslim or annoying anyone, and that is an atonement for his sins till the next Friday and three days more, the reason being that Allah, the Exalted, says: 'He who does a good deed will have ten times as much' (vi.160)."
> — *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1113)*

## 5. When Does the Prohibition of Talking Begin and End?

### The Prohibition Begins When the Imam Starts Delivering the Khutbah

The hadith specifies: "while the imam is delivering the khutbah" (والإمام يخطب) — this is a circumstantial clause indicating the prohibition applies during the actual khutbah, not before it.

**Sharh al-Zurqani** (1/381) explains:

> "His statement in the hadith 'while the imam is delivering the khutbah' is a circumstantial clause (jumla haaliyya), meaning: while the imam is actually delivering the khutbah. This excludes what is before his khutbah from the time of his coming out, and what is after it until he begins the khutbah."
> — *(Sharh al-Zurqani 'ala al-Muwatta', 1/381)*

### Ibn Uthaymin's Clear Statement

**Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin** (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

> "The silence is obligatory in the condition the Imaam is giving the khutbah. So what is before the khutbah, even after the Imaam comes, it is not obligatory to be silent. And between the two khutbahs, it is not obligatory to be silent."
> — *(ash-Sharh al-Mumti' 'ala Zad al-Mustaqni', vol. 5, p. 109)*

### The Practice of the Companions During 'Umar's Time

It was reported by Tha'labah bin Abi Malik al-Qurazi:

> "They used to pray on the day of Jumu'ah during the time of 'Umar bin al-Khattab until 'Umar came out. When 'Umar came out and sat on the minbar and the mu'adhdhins called the adhan, (Tha'labah said): 'We sat talking. When the mu'adhdhins fell silent and 'Umar stood to deliver the khutbah, we fell silent, and none of us spoke.'"

**Al-Albani** authenticated this in **Tamam al-Minnah** (p. 399):

> "It has a follow-up narration (mutaba'ah) with a sahih chain."

### Speaking Between the Two Khutbahs Is Permissible

From the Fiqh Encyclopedia (dorar.net):

> "It is permissible to speak between the two khutbahs, especially if there is a need for it. This is the position of the Shafi'is (Mughni al-Muhtaj by al-Shirbini, 1/287; Nihayat al-Muhtaj by al-Ramli, 2/320) and the Hanbalis on the correct view (Al-Insaf by al-Mardawi, 2/292; Kashshaf al-Qina' by al-Buhuti, 2/47)."

### The First and Second Adhan of Jumu'ah

**Historical background** (Sahih al-Bukhari):

> "The person who increased the number of Adhans for the Jumua prayers to three was Uthman bin Affan and it was when the number of the (Muslim) people of Medina had increased. In the life-time of the Prophet there was only one Muadh-dhin and the Adhan used to be pronounced only after the Imam had taken his seat (i.e. on the pulpit)."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 13: Friday Prayer)*

> **Note on terminology:** What al-Bukhari calls the "third adhan" (introduced by 'Uthman) is what is commonly referred to as the "first adhan" in modern times. In the time of the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr, and 'Umar, there was only one adhan, called when the imam sat on the minbar. 'Uthman added an earlier adhan (at Az-Zaura', in the marketplace) to alert people when the population of Madinah increased.

**Shaykh al-Fawzan** refuted the claim that the first adhan is a bid'ah:

> "This Imam abandoned the Sunnah and introduced an innovation; because the first adhan is the Sunnah of the Rightly-Guided Caliphs — 'Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him) commanded it during his caliphate."
> — *(Official website of Shaykh al-Fawzan)*

**Summary:**
- The **first adhan** (introduced by 'Uthman) is an alert for people to prepare — it is NOT the beginning of the prohibition of speech.
- The **prohibition of trade** begins with the second adhan (the adhan of the khutbah).
- The **prohibition of talking** begins when the imam starts delivering the khutbah.

## 6. What You CAN and CANNOT Say During the Khutbah

### What You CANNOT Do:

| Action | Ruling | Evidence |
|--------|--------|----------|
| Talk to your companion | Haram (laghw) | Sahih al-Bukhari 934 |
| Tell someone "be quiet" | Haram (laghw) — even this is vain speech | Sahih al-Bukhari 934 |
| Return salaam verbally | Haram — respond by gesture only | Ibn Baz fatwa |
| Initiate salaam | Haram | Ibn Uthaymin, ash-Sharh al-Mumti' 5/109 |
| Say "Yarhamuk Allah" to someone who sneezed | Haram | Ibn Baz fatwa |
| Touch pebbles / fidget | Causes interruption | Sahih Muslim (Riyad al-Salihin 1148) |
| Raise hands in du'a | Not prescribed (except for istisqa/rain) | Ibn Baz, Permanent Committee |

### What You CAN Do:

#### Speaking to the Imam (Khatib) — Permissible If There Is a Need

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "Responding to the imam during the khutbah, or asking him about something during the khutbah, or requesting something from him during the khutbah is not considered laghw (vain speech) in the khutbah, and is not prohibited. A man came to the Prophet (ﷺ) while he was delivering the khutbah on Friday and said: 'O Messenger of Allah! Wealth has perished and the roads have been cut off, so call upon Allah to give us rain.' The Prophet (ﷺ) responded to him and sought rain from his Lord, and did not rebuke him. So if a person addresses the imam — the khatib — saying to him: 'O khatib! O so-and-so! Pray for us,' or 'Seek rain for us,' or points out an error he made, there is no harm in that, and it is not included in the prohibition and is not forbidden."
> — *(Fatwa: "حكم الكلام مع الخطيب أثناء الخطبة", from Nur 'ala al-Darb)*

#### The Imam Himself Speaking

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "And the khatib himself — there is no harm in him speaking, because this is part of his khutbah. As for the people among themselves, no."
> — *(Fatwa: "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة")*

#### Latecomers: Praying Tahiyyat al-Masjid (2 Short Rak'ahs)

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "The Sunnah upon entering the masjid is to pray two rak'ahs of tahyatul masjid (a Prayer for greeting the mosque), even if the Imaam is delivering the Friday khutbah, due to the sayings of the Prophet sallallaahu 'alayhi wa sallam: 'When anyone of you enters the masjid, then let him not sit until he prays two rak'ahs.' Recorded by al-Bukhaaree (1/293) and Muslim (2/155). Likewise Muslim relates in his Saheeh (3/1415), from Jaabir radiallaahu 'anhu, that the Prophet sallallaahu 'alayhi wa sallam said: 'When any of you comes on the day of Jumu'ah (Friday), and the Imaam is delivering the khutbah, then let him pray two rak'ahs and let him make them short.' So this is a clear and authentic text concerning this issue. It is not permissible for anyone to oppose it."
> — *(Al-Fataawaa 1/53; Al-Istiqaamah Issue No. 7)*

#### Responding to Salaam — By Gesture Only

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "Giving salaam while the imam is delivering the khutbah is not prescribed. Rather, one should pray the tahiyyah and sit, and not give salaam to anyone until the khatib finishes. If someone gives you salaam, do not respond except with a gesture, just as if someone gives you salaam while you are praying — you respond with a gesture and that suffices. He should not insist on seeking the salaam. If he extends his hand, you extend your hand and do not speak anything until the khatib finishes. When the khatib falls silent, you may respond to him, and all praise is for Allah."
> — *(Fatwa: "حكم رد السلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة")*

#### Sneezing — Say "Alhamdulillah" Silently

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "Even if someone sneezes and praises Allah, do not say 'May Allah have mercy on you' (Yarhamuk Allah), just as you do not say it in prayer — if someone sneezes while praying and praises Allah, 'Yarhamuk Allah' is not said. Likewise in the khutbah, the one listening to it is like the one praying — he does not speak, does not return the salaam, does not initiate the salaam, and does not respond to the one who sneezes (tashmit). This is what is obligatory upon him."
> — *(Fatwa: "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة")*

And:

> "And if he sneezes, he praises Allah silently, and does not raise his voice."
> — *(Fatwa: "حكم رد السلام أثناء الخطبة")*

#### Saying "Ameen" — Permissible Quietly, Not in Unison

**Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin** said:

> "It is not an innovation to say Aameen when the Khatib supplicates during the Khutbah. If he begins to supplicate for the Muslims, then it is a preferred act to say Aameen to his supplication, but this should not be done in unison, nor in a loud voice. Rather, every person should recite individually and in a low voice, so that there is no annoyance or shouting. Each person should only say Aameen to the supplication of the Khatib quietly and apart from the others."
> — *(Fataawa Islamiyyah, DARUSSALAM, vol. 2, p. 477)*

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** concurred:

> "As for saying Aameen to the Imam's supplication during the Khutbah, I know of no objection to it, as long as the voice is not raised."
> — *(Fatawa Islamiyah, vol. 2, p. 476)*

#### Sending Salat on the Prophet ﷺ — Permissible Silently When Mentioned

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "There is no harm in sending salat upon the Prophet (ﷺ) without raising one's voice in a way that disturbs those around you. When the imam... mentions the Prophet (ﷺ), you send salat upon him, due to his (ﷺ) saying: 'May his nose be rubbed in dust, a man in whose presence I am mentioned and he does not send salat upon me.' So when you hear the salat upon the Prophet (ﷺ), you send salat upon him, between yourself and yourself, without raising your voice."
> — *(Fatwa: "حكم الصلاة على النبي ﷺ أثناء الخطبة")*

#### Raising Hands — NOT Prescribed (Except for Rain)

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "It is not prescribed for the Imam or the worshippers to raise their hands during the Friday Khutbah, because the Messenger of Allaah (sallallaahu alaihi wa sallam) did not do so, and neither did his righteous successors, may Allaah be pleased with them. But if the Imam is calling for rain in the Friday Khutbah, it is prescribed for him and the worshippers to raise their hands, because when the Prophet (sallallaahu alaihi wa sallam) prayed for rain, he raised his hands and the people raised their hands. And Allaah, the Most Glorified says: 'Indeed, in the Messenger of Allaah (Muhammad (sallallaahu alaihi wa sallam)) you have a good example to follow.' [Quran, Surah al-Ahzab 33:21]"
> — *(Fatawa Islamiyah, vol. 2, p. 476)*

#### If You Feel Sleepy

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "If one of you feels as though he is falling asleep, let him trade places with his companion so he may remain attentive."
> — *(Authentic narration, cited on troid.org)*

## 7. The Recommended and Sunnah Acts of Jumu'ah

### Ghusl (Bathing) on Friday

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "The taking of a bath on Friday is compulsory for every male (Muslim) who has attained the age of puberty."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 895; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 849; al-Albani: Sahih)*

And:

> "When one of you intends to come for the Friday prayer, he should take a bath."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 882; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 845; al-Albani: Sahih)*

And:

> "It suffices to perform Wudu' properly for the Friday prayer; but it is better to take a bath."
> — *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 354; al-Albani: Sahih)*

#### Is Ghusl Wajib or Sunnah?

The word "wajib" in the hadith has been interpreted by the Salafi scholars as "emphasized/confirmed" (muta'akkid), NOT obligatory in the sense of sinning.

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "The Ghusl of Jumu'ah is a confirmed Sunnah (sunnah mu'akkadah) for men... It is NOT obligatory in the sense that one sins by leaving it, but it is 'wajib' in the sense of being confirmed/emphasized... By this it is known that his statement ﷺ 'wajib' does not mean obligatory (fard), but rather it means 'confirmed/emphasized' (muta'akkid), just as the Arabs say in their language: 'Your right upon me is wajib,' meaning: confirmed/emphasized. This is reconciling the hadiths on the topic... Whoever bathes from janabah (sexual impurity) on Friday, that suffices him for the Ghusl of Jumu'ah, and it is better to intend both when bathing. The recommended Ghusl on Friday is not achieved unless it is after the dawn (Fajr). And it is best that his bath be when he sets out for the Jumu'ah prayer, because that is more complete in terms of energy and cleanliness."
> — *(Majmu' Fatawa wa Maqalat, 10/171)*

**Shaykh al-Fawzan** said:

> "The Ghusl of Friday is recommended (mustahabb) by consensus of all the scholars, and it is also emphasized (muta'akkid). It is emphasized when one goes to Jumu'ah, so that he cleans himself and prepares for the Jumu'ah prayer with the Muslims, and to remove any unpleasant odors and sweat — so he bathes, cleans himself, applies perfume, and wears his best clothes. This is recommended on this day, because it is a great day and a great gathering of Muslims, so one should prepare for it. As for his statement ﷺ 'Ghusl on Friday is wajib upon every adult' — the meaning of 'wajib' here is 'emphasized' (muta'akkid), NOT an obligatory duty (fard)."
> — *(Official website of Shaykh al-Fawzan)*

**Ghusl is for men only, not women:**

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "There is no Ghusl of Jumu'ah upon women. The Ghusl is upon the one who goes to Jumu'ah, as the Prophet ﷺ said: 'Whoever goes to Jumu'ah, let him take a bath.' As for women, the Sunnah for them is to pray in their houses, and there is no ghusl upon them. It is only upon men."
> — *(Majmu' Fatawa)*

### Using the Siwak, Applying Perfume, and Wearing Best Clothes

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "The taking of a bath on Friday is compulsory for every male Muslim who has attained the age of puberty and (also) the cleaning of his teeth with Siwak, and the using of perfume if it is available."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 880; al-Albani: Sahih)*

And:

> "Ghusl should be performed on Friday by everyone who has reached the age of puberty, and using the siwak, and he should put on whatever he is able of using perfume."
> — *(Sunan an-Nasa'i, Hadith 1383; al-Albani: Sahih)*

And:

> "It is a duty for the Muslims, that they perform Ghusl on Friday, and that each of them wear some of his family's perfume. If he does not find any, then water is a perfume for him."
> — *(Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Hadith 528; al-Albani: Hasan)*

And:

> "Whoever takes a bath on a Friday and does it well, and purifies himself and does it well, and puts on his best clothes, and puts on whatever Allah decrees for him of the perfume of his family, then comes to the mosque and does not engage in idle talk or separate (pushing between) two people; he will be forgiven for (his sins) between that day and the previous Friday."
> — *(Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1097; al-Albani: Sahih)*

**Shaykh al-Fawzan** mentioned these acts together:

> "...so he bathes, cleans himself, applies perfume, and wears his best clothes. This is recommended, or these actions are recommended on this day."
> — *(Official website)*

### Going Early to Jumu'ah — The Hadith of Camel, Cow, Sheep, Hen, Egg

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Any person who takes a bath on Friday like the bath of Janaba and then goes for the prayer (in the first hour i.e. early), it is as if he had sacrificed a camel (in Allah's cause); and whoever goes in the second hour it is as if he had sacrificed a cow; and whoever goes in the third hour, then it is as if he had sacrificed a horned ram; and if one goes in the fourth hour, then it is as if he had sacrificed a hen; and whoever goes in the fifth hour then it is as if he had offered an egg. When the Imam comes out (i.e. starts delivering the Khutba), the angels present themselves to listen to the Khutba."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 881; Sahih Muslim, Hadith 850; al-Albani: Sahih)*

And:

> "When it is Friday, the angels stand at every door of the mosque and record the people in the order of their arrival, and when the Imam sits (on the pulpit for delivering the sermon) they fold up their sheets (manuscripts of the Qur'an) and listen to the mention (of Allah). And he who comes early is like one who offers a she-camel as a sacrifice, the next like one who offers a cow, the next a ram, the next a hen, the next an egg."
> — *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 850b; al-Albani: Sahih)*

#### When Does the First Hour Begin?

**Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin** said:

> "So, he has divided the time from sunrise until the arrival of the Imam into five parts. Each part might be equivalent to the well known hour (i.e. 60 minutes), or the hour might be more or less, because the time changes, so the hours are five between the sunrise and the arrival of the Imam for the prayer. It begins from sunrise, although it has been said that it is from the start of Fajr, but the former is more likely, because before sunrise is the time for the Fajr prayer."
> — *(Fatawa Arkan al-Islam, Volume 2)*

### Reciting Surah al-Kahf on Friday

The Prophet ﷺ said (regarding the day of Friday):

> "Whoever reads Surah Al-Kahf on the day of Jumu'ah, will have a light that will shine from him from one Friday to the next."
> — *(Narrated by al-Hakim, 2/399; al-Bayhaqi, 3/249; al-Albani: **Sahih** in Sahih al-Jami' 6470, Sahih at-Targhib 736, Irwa' al-Ghalil 3/93)*

And (regarding the night of Friday):

> "Whoever reads Surah al-Kahf on the night of Jumu'ah, will have a light that will stretch between him and the Ancient House (the Ka'bah)."
> — *(Narrated by al-Darimi, Hadith 3407; al-Albani: **Sahih** in Sahih al-Jami' 6471)*

> **Note on grading:** Al-Albani graded these hadiths as **Sahih** (not merely Hasan) in multiple works: *Sahih al-Jami'* (6470, 6471), *Sahih at-Targhib* (736), and *Irwa' al-Ghalil* (3/93-94). Ibn Hajar graded them **Hasan** in *Takhrij al-Adhkar*. Both gradings affirm the practice's legitimacy.

> **Note on timing:** The night of Jumu'ah starts from sunset on Thursday, and the day of Jumu'ah ends at sunset on Friday. So the time for reading Surah al-Kahf extends from sunset on Thursday to sunset on Friday.

**Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin** said:

> "The recitation of Surah Al-Kahf on Friday is a recommended act and there is reward in it and there is no difference between a person reciting it from the Book, or by heart."
> — *(Fatawa Nur 'ala al-Darb, 2/5)*

### Sending Abundant Salawat Upon the Prophet ﷺ on Friday

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Among the most excellent of your days is Friday; on it Adam was created, on it he died, on it the last trumpet will be blown, and on it the shout will be made, so invoke more blessings on me that day, for your blessings will be submitted to me."

The people asked: "Messenger of Allah, how can it be that our blessings will be submitted to you while your body is decayed?"

He replied: "Allah, the Exalted, has prohibited the earth from consuming the bodies of Prophets."
> — *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1047; Sunan an-Nasa'i, Hadith 1374; Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1085; al-Albani: **Sahih**)*

### Reciting Surah as-Sajdah (32) and al-Insan (76) in Fajr on Friday

The Prophet ﷺ used to recite specific surahs in the Fajr prayer on Friday:

> "The Prophet ﷺ used to recite the following in the Fajr prayer of Friday, 'Alif, Lam, Mim, Tanzil' (Surat as-Sajdah #32) and 'Hal-ata-ala-l-Insani' (i.e. Surah Ad-Dahr #76)."
> — *(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 891; al-Albani: Sahih)*

**Ibn al-Qayyim** (quoting **Ibn Taymiyyah**) explained why:

> "The Prophet ﷺ would only recite these two surahs during the fajr prayer on Friday because they contain what was and will be on this day. They cover the creation of Adam, the Hereafter, and the congregation (of the dead), which will be on a Friday. By reciting them on this day, they act as a reminder to the Ummah about what happened on this day and what will happen."
> — *(Zad al-Ma'ad, 1/363)*

### Walking to Jumu'ah (Not Riding)

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Whoever takes a bath on Friday, and bathes completely, and goes early, arriving early, and walks and does not ride (to the mosque), and sits close to the Imam and listens to him, and does not engage in idle talk; for every step he takes he will have the reward of one year, the reward of a year's fasting and praying (at night)."
> — *(Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1087; Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 345; al-Albani: **Sahih**)*

> **Note:** If the mosque is far, it is permissible to ride. However, walking brings greater reward.

### The Sunnah Prayers After Jumu'ah

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "The Sunnah after Jumu'ah is to pray four rak'ahs. The Prophet (salallāhu 'alayhi wa sallam) said: 'Whoever prays after Jumu'ah, let him pray four [rak'ahs].' If you pray after Jumu'ah, pray four rak'ahs, and the Sunnah is that he prays [the four] with two tasleems (i.e. two rak'ahs at a time) — and that is better. And if he prays [the four altogether] with only one tasleem [at the end] then that is sufficient. So four rak'ahs is best. It is established from the Messenger (salallāhu 'alayhi wa sallam) that he would pray two rak'ahs Sunnah prayer in his house after Jumu'ah but what he instructed with carries more weight – and he instructed with four rak'ahs and his instruction is more emphatic."
> — *(Cited on abukhadeejah.com)*

### Not Reserving Places in the Masjid

**Abu Khadeejah** said:

> "The male worshipper is commanded with attending the Masjid early and with standing near to the Imaam himself, and not reserving his place by the use of a stick, or a staff or a prayer mat. Those who reserve places in this manner do so with the intention of praying in the first row and attaining the reward for that. However by merely placing a stick, or a prayer mat in the front row and then leaving the masjid, a person opposes the Sunnah."
> — *(abukhadeejah.com)*

## 8. The Hour of Acceptance (Saa'at al-Ijabah)

There is a specific hour on Friday in which du'a is accepted. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "There is a time on Friday at which no Muslim would ask Allah for what is good but He would give it to him." And further said: "This is a very short time."
> — *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 852a; al-Albani: Sahih)*

### When Is This Hour?

There are multiple authentic narrations about when this hour occurs:

**Opinion 1: Between the Imam sitting on the pulpit and the prayer ending:**

> "It occurs between the time when the Imam sits down (on the pulpit) and the time Salat is over."
> — *(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 853; al-Albani: Sahih)*

**Opinion 2: The last hour after 'Asr:**

> "The day of Friday has twelve hours, in which there is no Muslim slave who asks Allah for anything but He will grant it to him, so seek it in the last hour after 'Asr."
> — *(Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith 1048; Sunan an-Nasa'i, Hadith 1389; al-Albani: **Sahih**)*

**Opinion 3: The last hours of the day:**

> 'Abdullah bin Salam said: "I said, when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was sitting: 'We find in the Book of Allah that on Friday there is an hour when no believing slave performs prayer and asks Allah for anything at that time, but Allah will fulfill his need.'" ... He said: "It is the last hours of the day." ... He said: "Yes (it is so), when a believing slave performs prayer and then sits with nothing but the prayer keeping him, he is still in a state of prayer."
> — *(Sunan Ibn Majah, Hadith 1139; al-Albani: Sahih)*

### Ibn Baz's Reconciliation of All Views

**Shaykh Ibn Baz** said:

> "Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, has made in Friday an hour in which supplication is accepted, and it is a brief hour. No Muslim coincides with it while standing in prayer except that Allah gives him what he asks for — it is a great, brief hour. In some narrations in Sahih Muslim, it is when the Imam sits on the pulpit on Friday until the prayer is completed... It has also come from the hadith of Jabir bin 'Abdullah and 'Abdullah bin Salam that it is between 'Asr prayer and sunset. And in some hadiths, that it is the last hour of Friday. **All of these are authentic and there is no contradiction between them.** The most likely and most hoped-for is: between the sitting of the Imam on the pulpit until the prayer is completed, and after 'Asr prayer until sunset. These times are the most likely for the hour of acceptance. The remaining times on Friday all have hope for acceptance of supplication... One should make abundant supplication on Friday hoping to coincide with this blessed hour, but the three times mentioned above should be given extra attention because the Messenger ﷺ explicitly stated they are the hour of acceptance."
> — *(Majmu' Fatawa, 12/401)*

And in another fatwa:

> "And likewise after 'Asr: he sits in his prayer place waiting for the prayer, because whoever sits waiting for the prayer is in prayer. So if he sits in his prayer place waiting for Maghrib prayer, and supplicates to his Lord at the end of the day of Friday — acceptance is hoped for him."
> — *(Majmu' Fatawa)*

## 9. Quick Reference: What to Do on Friday

### Before Going to the Masjid:
1. **Perform Ghusl** (after Fajr, best when setting out) — confirmed Sunnah for men *(Sahih al-Bukhari 895)*
2. **Use the siwak** — Sunnah *(Sahih al-Bukhari 880)*
3. **Apply perfume** — Sunnah *(Sahih al-Bukhari 880)*
4. **Wear your best clothes** — Sunnah *(Sunan Ibn Majah 1097)*
5. **Recite Surah al-Kahf** — from sunset Thursday to sunset Friday *(al-Albani: Sahih)*
6. **Send abundant salawat upon the Prophet ﷺ** — *(Sunan Abi Dawud 1047; al-Albani: Sahih)*

### Going to the Masjid:
7. **Go early** — the five hours begin from sunrise *(Sahih al-Bukhari 881; Ibn Uthaymin)*
8. **Walk, don't ride** (if close) — reward of a year's fasting and praying for every step *(Sunan Ibn Majah 1087; al-Albani: Sahih)*
9. **Do not separate two people sitting together**
10. **Do not reserve places with sticks/mats** — opposes the Sunnah

### Upon Entering the Masjid:
11. **Pray 2 rak'ahs of tahiyyat al-masjid** — even if the imam is delivering the khutbah *(Ibn Baz fatwa)*
12. **Do NOT give salaam to anyone** while the khutbah is being delivered

### During the Khutbah:
13. **Remain completely silent** — do not speak, even to tell someone "be quiet" *(Sahih al-Bukhari 934)*
14. **Listen attentively** — this is obligatory
15. **Do not fidget with pebbles** — causes interruption *(Sahih Muslim)*
16. **Do not return salaam verbally** — gesture only *(Ibn Baz)*
17. **Do not say "Yarhamuk Allah"** to someone who sneezed — say "Alhamdulillah" silently only *(Ibn Baz)*
18. **Say "Ameen" quietly** when the khatib makes du'a — not in unison *(Ibn Uthaymin)*
19. **Send salat on the Prophet ﷺ silently** when he is mentioned *(Ibn Baz)*
20. **Do not raise hands** in du'a — except if the imam is praying for rain *(Ibn Baz)*
21. **If sleepy, trade places with your companion** *(Prophet's ﷺ instruction)*
22. **You MAY speak to the imam/khatib** if there is a genuine need *(Ibn Baz)*
23. **Speaking between the two khutbahs is permissible** *(Fiqh Encyclopedia)*

### The Jumu'ah Prayer:
24. **Pray 2 rak'ahs** behind the imam with **recitation aloud**
25. The imam may recite **Surah al-Jumu'ah (62)** and **al-Munafiqun (63)** *(Sahih Muslim 877a)*
26. **Catch at least 1 rak'ah** and you have caught Jumu'ah *(Sahih al-Bukhari 580)*

### After Jumu'ah:
27. **Pray 4 rak'ahs Sunnah** (with 2 tasleems, i.e. 2+2) — in the masjid or at home *(Ibn Baz)*
28. **Make abundant du'a** — especially after 'Asr until sunset (the hour of acceptance) *(Ibn Baz)*

### Fajr on Friday:
29. The imam may recite **Surah as-Sajdah (32)** and **al-Insan/Ad-Dahr (76)** in Fajr *(Sahih al-Bukhari 891)*
