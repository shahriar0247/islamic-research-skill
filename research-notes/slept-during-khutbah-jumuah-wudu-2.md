## The Short Answer First {#the-short-answer-first}

Before going into the detailed evidence, here is the direct answer to your question:

**Your Jumu'ah prayer is most likely valid, and your wudu is most likely intact — but it depends on what type of sleep you experienced.** The Salafi scholars distinguish between two types of sleep:

1. **Drowsiness / light sleep (al-nu'as):** Where you nod off briefly but can still hear sounds around you. This does **NOT** break your wudu, and your Jumu'ah is valid. This is the type of sleep most people experience during a khutbah while sitting.

2. **Deep / overwhelming sleep (al-nawm al-mustaghriq):** Where you completely lose awareness and would not feel it if you passed wind. This **DOES** break your wudu, and if you prayed without renewing it, your Jumu'ah is invalid and must be repeated as Dhuhr (4 rak'ahs).

Since you mentioned that you know you didn't pass wind, touch your private parts, or do anything to break wudu — and you were sitting during the khutbah — the most likely scenario is that you experienced **drowsiness (nu'as)**, which does not break wudu. However, if you truly lost consciousness completely (couldn't hear the khutbah at all), then you need to read the detailed scenarios below.

Let us now examine the evidence in detail from the Salafi scholars.

## The Core Question: Does Sleep Break Wudu? {#the-core-question}

The question of whether sleep nullifies wudu is one of the most discussed issues in the books of fiqh. The scholars agree that sleep is **not itself a hadath (event that breaks wudu)** but rather a **مظنة الحدث (occasion/cause for hadath)** — meaning, it is a situation where wind or other nullifiers may exit without the person being aware.

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) stated:

> "وَأَمَّا النَّوْمُ الْيَسِيرُ مِنْ الْمُتَمَكِّنِ بِمَقْعَدَتِهِ فَهَذَا لَا يَنْقُضُ الْوُضُوءَ عِنْدَ جُمَاهِيرِ الْعُلَمَاءِ مِنْ الْأَئِمَّةِ الْأَرْبَعَةِ وَغَيْرِهِمْ، فَإِنَّ النَّوْمَ عِنْدَهُمْ لَيْسَ بِحَدَثٍ فِي نَفْسِهِ لَكِنَّهُ مَظِنَّةُ الْحَدَثِ"

**Translation:** "As for light sleep of the one who is firmly seated, this does not break wudu according to the majority of scholars among the four Imams and others. For sleep, according to them, is not a hadath in itself, but it is an occasion (mazhanna) for hadath."

**Source:** Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 21/228-230

Ibn Taymiyyah also summarized the position of the early generations:

> "وَجُمَاهِيرُ السَّلَفِ وَالْخَلَفِ أَنَّ النَّوْمَ نَفْسَهُ لَيْسَ بِنَاقِضٍ، وَلَكِنَّهُ مَظِنَّةُ خُرُوجِ الْحَدَثِ"

**Translation:** "And the majority of the Salaf and the Khalaf [hold] that sleep itself is not a nullifier, but it is an occasion for the exit of hadath."

**Source:** *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 21/391, cited in *Fath al-'Allam* 1/290

The disagreement among the scholars lies in **what type and what degree of sleep breaks wudu**, and whether the physical position of the sleeper matters.

## The Two Types of Sleep: Drowsiness (Nu'as) vs. Deep Sleep (Nawm Mustaghriq) {#two-types-of-sleep}

All the contemporary Salafi scholars agree that there are two categories of sleep with different rulings:

### 1. Drowsiness / Light Sleep (النعاس — al-nu'as)

This is when a person nods off but does not completely lose awareness. They can still hear sounds around them, even if they don't fully understand what is being said. **This type does NOT break wudu.**

The evidence is the authentic hadith of Anas ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him):

> "كَانَ أَصْحَابُ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ يَخْفُقُونَ بِرُءُوسِهِمْ ثُمَّ يُصَلُّونَ وَلَا يَتَوَضَّئُونَ"

**Translation:** "The Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would nod their heads [from drowsiness] and then pray without performing wudu."

**Source:** Reported in *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*. Imam Muslim even titled a chapter: "باب الدليل على أن نوم الجالس لا ينقض الوضوء" (Chapter: The evidence that the sleep of the seated one does not break wudu). (Sahih Muslim, Kitab al-Hayd, no. 376)

### 2. Deep / Overwhelming Sleep (النوم المستغرق — al-nawm al-mustaghriq)

This is when a person completely loses awareness — they would not feel it if they passed wind, and they can no longer hear sounds around them. **This type DOES break wudu.**

The evidence is the hadith of Safwan ibn 'Asal al-Muradi (may Allah be pleased with him):

> "كَانَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ يَأْمُرُنَا إِذَا كُنَّا سُفَّرًا أَلَّا نَنْزِعَ خِفَافَنَا ثَلَاثَةَ أَيَّامٍ وَلَيَالِيَهُنَّ إِلَّا مِنْ جَنَابَةٍ، لَكِنْ مِنْ غَائِطٍ وَبَوْلٍ وَنَوْمٍ"

**Translation:** "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to command us, when we were travelers, not to remove our leather socks for three days and nights, except from sexual impurity, but [we may remove them] from defecation, urine, and sleep."

**Source:** Reported by al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi (and the wording is his), and authenticated by Ibn Khuzaymah. Graded **Hasan** by al-Albani in *Irwa' al-Ghalil* (no. 112). Ibn Baz called it "حديث صحيح" (an authentic hadith).

## The Criterion: How Do I Know Which Type of Sleep I Had? {#the-criterion}

This is the most practical question, and the scholars have given clear criteria:

### Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen's Criterion (the sensation test):

> "النعاس لا ينقض النوم، وكذلك النوم الخفيف، أما النوم العميق فإنه ناقض للوضوء، والفرق بينهما: أنه إذا كان نائماً يحس بنفسه إذا أحدث فنومه خفيف سواء كان جالساً أو متكئاً أو مضطجعاً، وأما إذا كان لا يحس بنفسه لو أحدث فنومه عميق ينقض الوضوء."

**Translation:** "Drowsiness does not break wudu, and likewise light sleep. As for deep sleep, it is a nullifier of wudu. The difference between them is: **if the sleeping person would feel it if he passed wind, then his sleep is light** — whether he is seated, leaning, or lying down. **But if he would not feel it if he passed wind, then his sleep is deep and breaks wudu.**"

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen, *Sharh Bulugh al-Maram*, cited in *Fatawa Majallat al-Da'wah*

### Shaykh al-Albani's Criterion (the hearing test):

> "النعاس هو ألَّا يغيب الناعس عن سمعه للأصوات التي حوله، لكن يغيب عنه الوعي والفهم... فإذا غاب الصوت عنه فقد نام، هذا الذي عليه الوضوء، هذه هي الضابطة والفرق بين النعاس وبين النوم."

**Translation:** "Drowsiness is that the drowsy person does not lose the hearing of sounds around him, but he loses consciousness and understanding... **If the sound disappears from him, then he has slept** — this is the one upon whom wudu is obligatory. This is the criterion and the difference between drowsiness and sleep."

**Source:** Shaykh al-Albani, audio fatwa via al-fatawa.com (originally from al-albany.com)

### Shaykh Ibn Baz's Criterion (the awareness test):

> "الضابط هو ذهاب الشّعور، إذا عرف من نفسه أنه ذهب شعوره ولا بقي له إحساس ينتقض الوضوء، أما ما دام يعتقد أنه نعاس وخفقان الرأس وليس شعوره ذاهبًا، بل يحسّ بمَن حوله من قُرَّاء وغيرهم؛ فصلاته صحيحة."

**Translation:** "The criterion is the loss of awareness. If he knows from himself that his awareness is gone and he has no sensation, his wudu is broken. As long as he believes it is drowsiness and nodding of the head, and his awareness is not gone — rather he senses those around him from reciters and others — then his prayer is valid."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, audio lesson on *Sharh al-Muntaqa*

### Summary of the Criteria:

| Scholar | Criterion for wudu being broken |
|---------|--------------------------------|
| **Ibn Uthaymeen** | If you would NOT feel it if you passed wind → wudu broken |
| **Ibn Baz** | If your awareness is completely gone → wudu broken |
| **Al-Albani** | If you can no longer hear sounds around you → wudu broken |
| **Permanent Committee** | If it is deep/overwhelming sleep (mustaghriq) → wudu broken |

**All four criteria point to the same reality:** if you were still somewhat aware of your surroundings (could hear the khutbah, would have noticed if you passed wind), your wudu is intact. If you were completely "out" — no awareness, no hearing, no sensation — your wudu is broken.

## The Two Main Salafi Positions on Sleep and Wudu {#two-main-positions}

There are two main positions among the contemporary Salafi scholars:

### Position 1: The criterion is loss of awareness/sensation (Majority)

This is the position of **Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, and the Permanent Committee**. They hold:
- Sleep is **mazhanna al-hadath** (occasion for hadath), not hadath itself.
- The criterion is whether the person would **feel it if he passed wind** (Ibn Uthaymeen) or whether **awareness is completely lost** (Ibn Baz).
- **Drowsiness (nu'as)** and light nodding do not break wudu.
- **Deep/overwhelming sleep (nawm mustaghriq)** breaks wudu, regardless of position.
- The Companions' nodding of heads was drowsiness, not deep sleep.
- The hadith "wudu is only on the one who sleeps lying down" is **weak (da'if)**.
- In case of doubt, the original assumption is the intactness of wudu.

### Position 2: Sleep absolutely breaks wudu (al-Albani, stricter)

This is the position of **Shaykh al-Albani** (and Ibn Hazm, al-San'ani). He holds:
- Sleep (al-nawm) absolutely breaks wudu, whether little or much, whether sitting or lying down — **as long as it is actual sleep (not drowsiness)**.
- The hadith "من نام فليتوضأ" (whoever sleeps, let him perform wudu) is absolute (mutlaq) and not restricted.
- The Companions' practice of sleeping then praying was in the **early period of Islam** before the ruling was established.
- The Prophet's sleep not breaking his wudu is from his **special characteristics (khasa'is)**.
- However, **drowsiness (nu'as)** — where one can still hear sounds but not understand them — does not break wudu.
- The difference is: if one can still **hear sounds**, it is drowsiness (no break); if one **cannot hear sounds at all**, it is sleep (wudu breaks), even if for moments.

### Practical difference between the two positions:

The practical difference arises in the case of **light sleep while seated** where the person loses awareness briefly but might still feel it if he passed wind:
- **Ibn Baz/Ibn Uthaymeen:** If he would feel it if he passed wind, wudu is not broken — even if he briefly lost awareness of his surroundings.
- **Al-Albani:** If he can no longer hear sounds, wudu is broken — even if brief and while seated.

However, **both positions agree** that:
- **Drowsiness (nu'as)** where one can still hear sounds does **not** break wudu.
- **Deep sleep** where awareness is completely lost **does** break wudu.
- The hadith "wudu is only on the one who sleeps lying down" is **weak**.
- Position (sitting vs. lying) is **not the determining factor**.

## Shaykh Ibn Baz's Position {#ibn-baz-position}

Shaykh 'Abd al-'Aziz Ibn Baz (may Allah have mercy on him) holds that **deep sleep (al-nawm al-mustaghriq) that removes awareness breaks wudu**, while **drowsiness (al-nu'as) does not**. Position does not matter — even if seated, if the sleep is deep, wudu is broken.

### Fatwa on deep sleep breaking wudu:

> "النوم ينقض الوضوء إذا كان مستغرقًا قد أزال الشعور... أما النعاس فلا ينقض الوضوء؛ لأنه لا يذهب معه الشعور، وبذلك تجتمع الأحاديث الواردة في هذا الباب."

**Translation:** "Sleep breaks wudu if it is overwhelming and removes awareness... As for drowsiness (nu'as), it does not break wudu because awareness is not removed with it, and thereby the hadiths reported in this chapter are reconciled."

**Source:** *Majmu' Fatawa Ibn Baz* 10/144, Fatwa no. 3775

### Fatwa on drowsiness not breaking wudu:

> "النعاس لا ينتقض به الوضوء، وإنما ينتقض بالنوم الذي لا يبقى مع صاحبه شعور بمن حوله، فقد كان الصحابة ينتظرون العشاء على عهد رسول الله ﷺ فتخفق رؤوسهم من النعاس ثم يصلون ولا يتوضؤون، أما النوم الثقيل الذي يذهب فيه الشعور فهذا ينتقض الوضوء به."

**Translation:** "Drowsiness does not break wudu; rather, what breaks it is the sleep in which the person no longer has awareness of those around him. The Companions used to wait for 'Isha during the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and their heads would nod from drowsiness, then they would pray without performing wudu. As for heavy sleep in which awareness is lost, this does break wudu."

**Source:** *Majmu' Fatawa Ibn Baz*

### On position not mattering:

> "أما حديث: لا ينقض الوضوء إلا إذا نام مضطجعًا فهذا حديث ضعيف؛ إنما النوم إذا استغرق، ولو ما هو مضطجع، ولو جالسًا استغرق في نومه، أو نام وهو ساجد، نام نومًا ذهب به شعوره؛ ينتقض الوضوء."

**Translation:** "As for the hadith 'wudu is not broken except if one sleeps lying down,' this is a weak hadith. Rather, sleep — if it is deep, even if not lying down, even if seated and overwhelmed by sleep, or sleeps while prostrating, a sleep that removes his awareness — breaks wudu."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, audio lesson on *Sharh al-Muntaqa*

### On the criterion (ضابط):

> "الضابط هو ذهاب الشّعور، إذا عرف من نفسه أنه ذهب شعوره ولا بقي له إحساس ينتقض الوضوء، أما ما دام يعتقد أنه نعاس وخفقان الرأس وليس شعوره ذاهبًا، بل يحسّ بمَن حوله من قُرَّاء وغيرهم؛ فصلاته صحيحة."

**Translation:** "The criterion is the loss of awareness. If he knows from himself that his awareness is gone and he has no sensation, his wudu is broken. As long as he believes it is drowsiness and nodding of the head, and his awareness is not gone, rather he senses those around him from reciters and others — then his prayer is valid."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, audio lesson on *Sharh al-Muntaqa*

### On doubt:

> "الأصل اليسير، الأصل عدم انتقاض الوضوء، الأصل سلامة الوضوء حتى يعلم ويطمئن أنه نام نومًا يُزيل شعوره."

**Translation:** "The original assumption is that it was light; the original assumption is non-nullification of wudu; the original assumption is the soundness of wudu until he knows and is certain that he slept a sleep that removes his awareness."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, audio lesson on *Sharh al-Muntaqa*

## Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen's Position {#ibn-uthaymeen-position}

Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him) holds the view attributed to Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah: **the criterion is whether the person would feel it if he passed wind**.

### From *al-Sharh al-Mumti'*:

> "والنَّوم النَّاقض على المذهب: كُلُّ نوم إلا يسير نوم من قائم، أو قاعد. والصَّحيح أن المدار في نقض الوُضُوء على الإِحساس، فما دام الإِنسان يحسُّ بنفسه لو أحدث فإِن نومه لا يَنْقُضُ وضوءَه، وإِذا كان لا يحسُّ بنفسه لو أحدث فإِن نومَه يَنْقُضُ وضوءَه."

**Translation:** "The nullifying sleep according to the madhhab [Hanbali] is: all sleep except light sleep of one who is standing or seated. **The correct view is that the basis for nullification of wudu is sensation (ihsas)**: as long as the person senses himself — such that if he passed wind he would know — then his sleep does not break his wudu. But if he does not sense himself — such that if he passed wind he would not know — then his sleep breaks his wudu."

**Source:** *al-Sharh al-Mumti' 'ala Zad al-Mustaqni'* 1/170

### From his audio fatwa (Fatwa al-Haram al-Makki):

> "ومن نواقض الوضوء النوم إذا نام الإنسان نوما مستغرقا وضابط النوم المستغرق هو الذي لو أحدث الإنسان فيه لم يحس بنفسه سواء كان مضطجعاً أو جالساً أو ساجداً هذا هو النوم المستغرق وما دون ذلك فلا ينقض الوضوء... المدار ليس على هيئة الإنسان بل المدار على عقل الإنسان فما دام الرجل لو أحدث لأحس بنفسه فإن نومه لا ينقض الوضوء وإذا كان لو أحدث لم يحس بنفسه فإن نومه ينقض الوضوء."

**Translation:** "Among the nullifiers of wudu is sleep, if a person sleeps a deep sleep. The criterion for deep sleep is: if the person were to pass wind during it, he would not sense himself — whether he is lying down, seated, or prostrating. This is deep sleep. Anything less than that does not break wudu... **The basis is not the person's posture, but the person's mind.** As long as the man — if he passed wind — would sense himself, then his sleep does not break wudu. And if he would not sense himself, then his sleep breaks wudu."

**Source:** Audio fatwa, *Fatawa al-Haram al-Makki*

## Shaykh al-Albani's Position {#al-albani-position}

Shaykh Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani (may Allah have mercy on him) holds a **stricter view**: sleep (al-nawm) absolutely breaks wudu, even if slight, whether sitting or lying down — but he distinguishes between **sleep (nawm)** and **drowsiness (nu'as)**.

### From his written works:

In *Irwa' al-Ghalil* (1/141), on the hadith of Safwan:

> "ومن فوائد هذه الزيادة أنها تدل على أن النوم مطلقا ناقض للوضوء كالغائط والبول... وهو الصواب"

**Translation:** "And from the benefits of this addition is that it indicates that sleep absolutely nullifies wudu like defecation and urine... and it is the correct view."

**Source:** *Irwa' al-Ghalil* 1/141

### On the Companions' practice:

> "النوم ينقض الوضوء مطلقا وحمل ما ورد عن الصحابة أنهم كانوا ينامون ثم يصلون بلا وضوء على أنه كان في أول الإسلام"

**Translation:** "Sleep absolutely breaks wudu, and what is reported about the Companions that they used to sleep then pray without wudu is carried on the basis that it was in the early period of Islam [before the ruling was established]."

**Source:** *Jami' Turath al-Albani fi al-Fiqh* 1/222

### From his audio fatwa — the clearest criterion:

> "النعاس ما بغيب الناعس عن الوجود مطلقًا بيبدأ بصير بيسمع الصوت لكن ما بيميز كلام شو عم يحكي هذا زيد أو بكر... لكنه لا يزال يسمع صوتًا... فإذا صار بحيث لا يسمع فهو النائم... فهذا النوم إن كان دقائق بل لحظات خلاص انتقض الوضوء... لأنه غاب عن وجوده أما اللي بيضل بيسمع الصوت لكن ما بيفهم فهذا ناعس فلا ينتقض وضوؤه."

**Translation:** "Drowsiness does not make the drowsy person lose consciousness entirely. He begins to hear sounds but does not distinguish the speech... but he still hears a sound... But if it reaches the point where he does not hear, then he is asleep (nā'im)... And this sleep, even if minutes or moments, wudu is broken... because he lost consciousness. As for the one who still hears sounds but does not understand, he is drowsy (nā'is) and his wudu is not broken."

**Source:** Audio fatwa, *Fatawa 'abra al-Hatif wa al-Sayyara*, Tape 156

### Al-Albani's position summarized:
- **Sleep (al-nawm) absolutely breaks wudu**, whether little or much, whether sitting or lying down.
- **Drowsiness (al-nu'as) does not break wudu.**
- The criterion: if the person can still **hear sounds** around him (even if he does not understand them), he is drowsy and his wudu is intact. If he **can no longer hear sounds**, he is asleep and his wudu is broken.
- The Companions' practice of sleeping then praying without wudu was **in the early period of Islam** before the ruling was established.
- The hadith "wudu is only on the one who sleeps lying down" is **weak (da'if)** and the addition is **munkar (rejected)**.
- The Prophet's sleep not breaking his wudu is from his **special characteristics (khasa'is)**.

## Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah's Position {#ibn-taymiyyah-position}

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) holds that **sleep is not a hadath in itself, but is a mazhanna (occasion) for hadath**. His key principle is:

> "فَأَمَّا النَّوْمُ الْيَسِيرُ مِنْ الْمُتَمَكِّنِ بِمَقْعَدَتِهِ فَهَذَا لَا يَنْقُضُ الْوُضُوءَ عِنْدَ جُمَاهِيرِ الْعُلَمَاءِ مِنْ الْأَئِمَّةِ الْأَرْبَعَةِ وَغَيْرِهِمْ، فَإِنَّ النَّوْمَ عِنْدَهُمْ لَيْسَ بِحَدَثٍ فِي نَفْسِهِ لَكِنَّهُ مَظِنَّةُ الْحَدَثِ"

**Translation:** "As for light sleep of the one who is firmly seated, this does not break wudu according to the majority of scholars among the four Imams and others. For sleep, according to them, is not a hadath in itself, but it is an occasion (mazhanna) for hadath."

**Source:** *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 21/228-230

### On the Prophet's sleep not breaking his wudu:

> "وَيَدُلُّ عَلَى هَذَا مَا فِي الصَّحِيحَيْنِ: أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ كَانَ يَنَامُ حَتَّى يَنْفُخَ ثُمَّ يَقُومُ فَيُصَلِّي وَلَا يَتَوَضَّأُ، لِأَنَّهُ كَانَ تَنَامُ عَيْنَاهُ وَلَا يَنَامُ قَلْبُهُ فَكَانَ يَقْظَانَ، فَلَوْ خَرَجَ مِنْهُ شَيْءٌ لَشَعَرَ بِهِ. وَهَذَا يُبَيِّنُ أَنَّ النَّوْمَ لَيْسَ بِحَدَثٍ فِي نَفْسِهِ؛ إذْ لَوْ كَانَ حَدَثًا لَمْ يَكُنْ فِيهِ فَرْقٌ بَيْنَ النَّبِيِّ وَغَيْرِهِ"

**Translation:** "And this is indicated by what is in the two Sahihs: that the Prophet used to sleep until he snored, then stand and pray without performing wudu, because his eyes would sleep but his heart would not, so he was awake. Had anything exited from him, he would have been aware of it. This shows that sleep is not a hadath in itself; for if it were a hadath, there would be no difference between the Prophet and others."

**Source:** *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 21/228-230

### On the Companions nodding their heads:

> "وَأَيْضًا فَإِنَّهُ ثَبَتَ فِي الصَّحِيحِ: أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ كَانَ يُؤَخِّرُ الْعِشَاءَ حَتَّى كَانَ أَصْحَابُ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ يَخْفُقُونَ بِرُءُوسِهِمْ ثُمَّ يُصَلُّونَ وَلَا يَتَوَضَّئُونَ. فَهَذَا يُبَيِّنُ أَنَّ جِنْسَ النَّوْمِ لَيْسَ بِنَاقِضٍ؛ إذْ لَوْ كَانَ نَاقِضًا لَانْتَقَضَ بِهَذَا النَّوْمِ الَّذِي تَخْفُقُ فِيهِ رُءُوسُهُمْ"

**Translation:** "And also, it is established in the Sahih that the Prophet would delay 'Isha until the Companions of the Messenger of Allah would nod their heads, then they would pray without performing wudu. This shows that the genus of sleep is not a nullifier; for if it were a nullifier, this sleep in which their heads nod would have nullified [their wudu]."

**Source:** *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 21/228-230

### On doubt:

> "وَالْأَظْهَرُ فِي هَذَا الْبَابِ أَنَّهُ إِذَا شَكَّ الْمُتَوَضِّئُ: هَلْ نَوْمُهُ مِمَّا يَنْقُضُ أَوْ لَيْسَ مِمَّا يَنْقُضُ؟ فَإِنَّهُ لَا يَحْكُمُ بِنَقْضِ الْوُضُوءِ؛ لِأَنَّ الطَّهَارَةَ ثَابِتَةٌ بِيَقِينٍ فَلَا تَزُولُ بِالشَّكِّ"

**Translation:** "And the most apparent in this chapter is that if the one with wudu doubts: is his sleep of the type that breaks [wudu] or not? Then he does not rule that wudu is broken; because purity is established with certainty and is not removed by doubt."

**Source:** *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 21/228-230

## The Permanent Committee's Position {#permanent-committee-position}

The Permanent Committee for Scholarly Research and Ifta' (al-Lajna al-Da'imah) holds that **deep/overwhelming sleep is a mazhanna for nullification of wudu**, and it breaks wudu **regardless of position** (standing, seated, or lying down).

### On deep sleep breaking wudu:

> "النوم المستغرق مظنة لنقض الوضوء فمن نام نوما مستغرقا في المسجد أو غيره وجب عليه إعادة وضوئه، سواء كان قائما أو قاعدا أو مضطجعا، وسواء كان في يده سبحة أم لا. أما إن كان غير مستغرق، كالنعاس الذي لا يفقد معه الشعور فلا تجب عليه إعادة الوضوء لما ورد في ذلك من الأحاديث الصحيحة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم الدالة على التفصيل المذكور."

**Translation:** "Deep/overwhelming sleep is an occasion for nullification of wudu. So whoever sleeps a deep sleep in the mosque or elsewhere, he must repeat his wudu, whether he is standing, seated, or lying down, and whether he has prayer beads in his hand or not. As for sleep that is not deep, such as light drowsiness (al-nu'as) during which consciousness is not lost, it is not obligatory to renew wudu, due to the authentic hadiths from the Prophet ﷺ that indicate the detail mentioned."

**Source:** Permanent Committee, Fatwa No. 3030, *Majmu' Fatawa al-Lajna al-Da'imah*, Vol. 5, p. 283. Members: `Abdullah ibn Qu'ud, `Abdullah ibn Ghudayyan, `Abd al-Razzaq `Afifi, `Abd al-`Aziz ibn `Abdullah ibn Baz.

### On sleeping during the Friday khutbah specifically:

> "إن النوم الخفيف الذي لا يزول معه الشعور لا ينقض الوضوء. فقد ثبت أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم كان يؤخر صلاة العشاء بعض الأحيان حتى كان أصحاب رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم تخفق رءوسهم ثم يصلون ولا يتوضؤون."

**Translation:** "Light sleep during which consciousness is not lost does not break wudu. It has been established that the Prophet ﷺ used to delay the `Isha' prayer sometimes until the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would have their heads nodding [in drowsiness], then they would pray and not make wudu."

**Source:** Permanent Committee, Fatwa No. 12885, *Majmu' Fatawa al-Lajna al-Da'imah*, Vol. 5, p. 284. Members: `Abdullah ibn Ghudayyan, `Abd al-Razzaq `Afifi, `Abd al-`Aziz ibn `Abdullah ibn Baz.

## Does the Position of Sleeping Matter? (Sitting vs. Lying Down) {#does-position-matter}

**No.** All the contemporary Salafi scholars (Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, al-Albani, Permanent Committee) agree that position is **NOT the determining factor**. The hadith "wudu is only on the one who sleeps lying down" is graded **weak (da'if)** by al-Albani and Ibn Baz.

### The weak hadith:

> "إِنَّمَا الْوُضُوءُ عَلَى مَنْ نَامَ مُضْطَجِعًا، فَإِنَّهُ إِذَا اضْطَجَعَ اسْتَرْخَتْ مَفَاصِلُهُ"

**Translation:** "Wudu is only obligatory upon the one who sleeps lying down, for when he lies down, his joints relax."

**Al-Albani's grading:** **ضعيف (Da'if)** in *Da'if al-Jami'* (no. 2051 and 1808). He also graded it da'if in *Da'if Abi Dawud* (no. 25), noting: "وفيه زيادة منكرة" (it contains a rejected addition).

**Abu Dawud's own comment:** "قوله: الوضوء على من نام مضطجعا، هو حديث منكر لم يروه إلا يزيد الدالاني عن قتادة" — "This wording is a munkar hadith; none reported it except Yazid al-Dalani from Qatadah."

**Ibn Baz's comment:** "أما حديث: لا ينقض الوضوء إلا إذا نام مضطجعًا فهذا حديث ضعيف" — "As for the hadith 'wudu is not nullified except if one sleeps lying down,' this is a weak hadith."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, audio lesson on *Sharh al-Muntaqa*

### Ibn Baz on position:

> "سواء، لكن المضطجع أو المتَّكئ أقرب إلى خروج الحدث منه، ولكن بكل حالٍ ما دام ينعس فلا يضرّه، إنما تضره إذا ذهب عقله بالكلية."

**Translation:** "They are the same [whether leaning or not]. However, the one lying down or leaning is closer to having hadath exit. But in any case, as long as he is drowsy, it does not harm him. It only harms him if his mind goes entirely."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, audio lesson on *Sharh al-Muntaqa*

| Scholar/School | Does position matter? | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Abu Hanifah** | Yes | Only lying down breaks wudu; prayer postures do not |
| **Al-Shafi'i** | Yes | Firmly seated = no break; any other position = break |
| **Malik** | No (quantity matters) | Deep sleep breaks regardless of position; light sleep does not |
| **Ahmad (madhhab)** | Partially | Light sleep of standing/seated does not break; other positions do |
| **Ibn Taymiyyah** | No | The criterion is sensation (ihsas), not position |
| **Ibn Baz** | No | The criterion is loss of awareness, not position |
| **Ibn Uthaymeen** | No | The criterion is sensation, not position |
| **Al-Albani** | No | Sleep breaks wudu absolutely regardless of position |
| **Permanent Committee** | No | Deep sleep breaks wudu regardless of position |

## The Key Hadiths and Their Gradings {#key-hadiths}

### 1. The Hadith of Safwan ibn 'Asal (wiping over socks and sleep)

> "كَانَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ يَأْمُرُنَا إِذَا كُنَّا سُفَّرًا أَلَّا نَنْزِعَ خِفَافَنَا ثَلَاثَةَ أَيَّامٍ وَلَيَالِيَهُنَّ إِلَّا مِنْ جَنَابَةٍ، لَكِنْ مِنْ غَائِطٍ وَبَوْلٍ وَنَوْمٍ"

**Translation:** "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to command us, when we were travelers, not to remove our leather socks for three days and nights, except from sexual impurity, but [we may remove them] from defecation, urine, and sleep."

**Sources:** Reported by al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi, and authenticated by Ibn Khuzaymah.
**Al-Albani's grading:** **Hasan** in *Irwa' al-Ghalil* (no. 112).
**Ibn Baz's grading:** Called it "حديث صحيح" (an authentic hadith).

### 2. The Hadith of 'Ali — "The eye is the tie of the anus"

> "الْعَيْنُ وِكَاءُ السَّهِ، فَمَنْ نَامَ فَلْيَتَوَضَّأْ"

**Translation:** "The eye is the tie (wikā') of the anus (al-suh); so whoever sleeps, let him perform wudu."

**Sources:** Reported by Ahmad, Abu Dawud (no. 202), Ibn Majah (no. 516), al-Daraqutni, al-Hakim, and al-Tabarani.
**Al-Albani's grading:** **Hasan** in *Irwa' al-Ghalil* (no. 113); **Sahih** in *al-Jami' al-Saghir* (no. 7598).
**Ibn Baz's grading:** "It was reported by Ahmad and al-Tabarani, and in its chain there is weakness, but it has supporting witnesses (shawāhid), such as the hadith of Safwan mentioned, and thereby it becomes a hasan hadith."

**Note on meaning:** "السَّه" (al-suh) means the ring of the anus. The "wikā'" is the string that ties a waterskin. The metaphor means: wakefulness is to the anus what the tie is to a waterskin — just as the tie prevents contents from spilling, wakefulness prevents involuntary exit of wind. When one sleeps, the "tie" is loosened.

### 3. The Hadith of the Companions nodding their heads (drowsiness doesn't break wudu)

> "كَانَ أَصْحَابُ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ يَخْفُقُونَ بِرُءُوسِهِمْ ثُمَّ يُصَلُّونَ وَلَا يَتَوَضَّئُونَ"

**Translation:** "The Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would nod their heads [from drowsiness] and then pray without performing wudu."

**Sources:** *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*. Imam Muslim titled a chapter: "باب الدليل على أن نوم الجالس لا ينقض الوضوء" (Chapter: The evidence that the sleep of the seated one does not break wudu).
**Grading:** **Muttafaq 'alayh** (agreed upon — Bukhari & Muslim).

### 4. The Hadith of the Prophet's eyes sleeping but not his heart

> "إِنَّ عَيْنَيَّ تَنَامَانِ وَلَا يَنَامُ قَلْبِي"

**Translation:** "Indeed, my eyes sleep but my heart does not sleep."

**Sources:** *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*.
**Al-Albani's note:** "صريحة في أن النوم لا ينقض وضوءه صلى الله عليه وسلم، وأن ذلك من خصوصياته" — "Explicit that sleep does not break his (the Prophet's) wudu, and that this is from his special characteristics."

### 5. The Hadith "Allah does not accept prayer without purification"

> "لاَ تُقْبَلُ صَلاَةٌ بِغَيْرِ طُهُورٍ، وَلاَ صَدَقَةٌ مِنْ غُلُولٍ"

**Translation:** "No prayer is accepted without purification, and no charity from stolen goods."

**Narrator:** `Abdullah ibn `Umar (may Allah be pleased with them both)
**Sources:** Reported by Muslim, al-Tirmidhi (no. 1), Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Ahmad.
**Al-Albani's grading:** **Sahih** — graded Sahih in *Sahih al-Tirmidhi* (no. 1).
**Note:** Al-Tirmidhi said: "This is the most authentic and best hadith on this topic."

### 6. The Hadith "If one of you breaks wind in his prayer, let him hold his nose and leave"

> "إِذَا أَحْدَثَ أَحَدُكُمْ فِي صَلَاتِهِ فَلْيَأْخُذْ بِأَنْفِهِ، ثُمَّ لِيَنْصَرِفْ"

**Translation:** "If one of you breaks wind in his prayer, let him hold his nose and leave."

**Narrator:** `A'ishah (may Allah be pleased with her)
**Sources:** Abu Dawud (no. 1111/1114), Ibn Majah.
**Al-Albani's grading:** **Sahih** — graded Sahih in *Sahih Abi Dawud* (no. 1114) and *Silsilat al-Ahadith al-Sahihah* (no. 2976).

**Significance:** This hadith directly addresses the scenario of wudu breaking during prayer — the person should hold their nose (to give the impression of a nosebleed, preserving their dignity) and leave the prayer. This confirms that the prayer is invalidated by the breaking of wudu.

## Sleeping During the Friday Khutbah Specifically {#sleeping-during-khutbah}

Now let us address the specific scenario you asked about: sleeping during the Friday khutbah while sitting.

### The Ruling on Sleeping During the Khutbah

**Deliberately sleeping during the khutbah is haram (prohibited)** because it violates the obligation of listening attentively (al-insat). The Permanent Committee explicitly listed sleeping alongside talking as distractions that must be avoided:

> "It is obligatory for a Muslim to listen to the Khutbah and stay away from distractions like talking or sleeping."

**Source:** Permanent Committee, Fatwa no. 12827, Part No. 7, Page No. 136. Scholars: Shaykh `Abdul-`Aziz Bin Baz, Shaykh `Abdullah bin Ghudayyan, Shaykh `Abdur-Razzaq al-`Afeefi.

However, **unintentional dozing off (ghalabahu al-nawm) is not sinful** — the person is not held accountable for what was beyond their control. The IslamWeb fatwa (citing Salafi positions) states:

> "لا يجوز تعمد النوم أثناء خطبة الجمعة لأن الإنصات لها واجب، أما من غلبه النوم في هذا الوقت فعليه أن يدفع ذلك ما استطاع، ولا يأثم بما كان غلبة"

**Translation:** "It is **not permissible** to deliberately sleep during the Friday khutbah because listening attentively to it is **obligatory**. As for the one whom sleep overtakes (ghalabahu al-nawm) at this time, he must repel it as much as he is able, and **he is not sinful for what was beyond his control**."

**Source:** IslamWeb Fatwa no. 104129

### The Prophetic Guidance: Change Your Place If You Doze Off

The Prophet ﷺ gave specific guidance for the one who dozes off during Jumu'ah:

> "إِذَا نَعَسَ أَحَدُكُمْ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ فَلْيَتَحَوَّلْ مِنْ مَجْلِسِهِ ذَلِكَ"

**Translation:** "If one of you dozes off on the day of Jumu'ah, let him move from his sitting place to another."

**Source:** Jami' al-Tirmidhi, Hadith no. 526; Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith no. 1119.
**Grading by al-Tirmidhi:** "Hadith hasan sahih" (good and authentic).
**Grading by al-Albani:** **Sahih** in *Hidayat al-Ruwat* (no. 1339) and *Sahih Sunan al-Tirmidhi*.

The wisdom behind this command, as explained by the commentators, is that movement dispels drowsiness. It may also be that the wisdom is in leaving the place where heedlessness overtook him.

### The Practice of the Salaf Regarding Sleep During the Khutbah

The early generations took this matter very seriously:

**Ibn 'Umar (may Allah be pleased with them both)** used to instruct a man who dozed off during the khutbah to move:

> "كَانَ ابْنُ عُمَرَ يَقُولُ لِلرَّجُلِ إِذَا نَعَسَ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ وَالْإِمَامُ يَخْطُبُ أَنْ يَتَحَوَّلَ مِنْهُ"

**Translation:** "Ibn 'Umar used to tell a man, if he dozed off on Friday while the imam was giving the khutbah, to move from his place."

**Source:** Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah; also reported in Musnad al-Shafi'i.

A Tabi'i expressed the gravity of sleeping during the khutbah:

> "لَأَنْ تَخْتَلِفَ السِّيَاطُ عَلَى ظَهْرِي أَحَبُّ إِلَيَّ مِنْ أَنْ أَنَامَ وَالْإِمَامُ يَخْطُبُ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ"

**Translation:** "I would rather have whips strike my back than sleep while the imam is giving the khutbah on Friday."

**Source:** Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah, graded sahih in chain.

## Does Sleeping During the Khutbah Invalidate the Jumu'ah Prayer? {#does-sleeping-invalidate-jumuah}

This is a crucial distinction that must be understood clearly:

### The Jumu'ah prayer itself is NOT invalidated by sleeping during the khutbah

The prayer obligation is still discharged if one prays with the imam. However, the person may **lose the reward and special virtue (fadilah)** of Jumu'ah.

Based on the hadith:

> "مَنِ اغْتَسَلَ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ وَمَسَّ مِنْ طِيبِ امْرَأَتِهِ إِنْ كَانَ لَهَا، وَلَبِسَ مِنْ صَالِحِ ثِيَابِهِ ، ثُمَّ لَمْ يَتَخَّطَّ رِقَابَ النَّاسِ وَلَمْ يَلْغُ عِنْدَ الْمَوْعِظَةِ كَانَتْ كَفَّارَةً لِمَا بَيْنَهُمَا ، وَمَنْ لَغَا وَتَخَّطَّى رِقَابَ النَّاسِ كَانَتْ لَهُ ظُهْرًا"

**Translation:** "Whoever performs ghusl on Friday, applies perfume if his wife has it, wears his good clothes, then does not step over people's necks and does not engage in idle talk (yalghu) during the admonition — it (the Jumu'ah) becomes an expiation for what is between it (this Friday and the previous one). And whoever engages in idle talk (lagha) and steps over people's necks — it becomes Dhuhr for him."

**Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud, Hadith no. 347; also Ibn Khuzaymah, al-Tahawi, al-Bayhaqi.
**Grading:** Al-Nawawi said its chain is hasan. Al-Albani confirmed its meaning: "It is understood from it that the virtue of Jumu'ah prayer is reversed to the virtue of Dhuhr prayer, and that's it."

**Note on the meaning of "became Dhuhr for him":** Ibn Wahb (one of its narrators) explained: "Its meaning is that the prayer suffices him (ajza'at 'anhu al-salat) but he is deprived of the virtue (fadilah) of Jumu'ah." So the prayer still counts as fulfilling the obligation, but without the special reward of Jumu'ah.

### The Key Distinction: Loss of Reward vs. Invalidity of Prayer

| Issue | Ruling |
|-------|--------|
| Speaking (laghw) during the khutbah | Loses the reward/fadilah of Jumu'ah; prayer still counts as obligation |
| Deliberate sleep during the khutbah | Haram (sinful); loses the reward of the khutbah |
| Unintentional dozing (ghalabahu al-nawm) | Not sinful; should change place and continue listening |
| Heavy sleep that breaks wudu, then praying without renewal | **Prayer is INVALID** — must repeat as Dhuhr |
| Light sleep (wudu intact), then praying | **Prayer is VALID** — but may lose some reward |

**The critical point:** Sleeping during the khutbah does NOT automatically invalidate the Jumu'ah prayer. What could invalidate it is if the sleep was deep enough to break wudu AND the person prayed without renewing wudu. The issue is not the sleep itself but the **state of taharah (purity)** when the prayer is performed.

## The Obligation of Listening Attentively to the Khutbah {#obligation-of-listening}

Allah commanded the believers in the Quran:

> **يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِن يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَى ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَذَرُوا الْبَيْعَ ذَلِكُمْ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ**

**Translation:** "O you who believe! When the call is made for the prayer on Friday (Jumu'ah), hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade. That is better for you, if you only knew."

(Quran, Surah al-Jumu'ah 62:9)

### Shaykh as-Sa'di's Tafsir:

> "يأمر تعالى عباده المؤمنين بالحضور لصلاة الجمعة والمبادرة إليها من حين يُنادى لها والسعي إليها، والمراد بالسَّعْي هنا المبادرة [إليها] والاهتمام لها وجعلها أهمَّ الأشغال... وفي الآية دليل على وجوب حضور الجمعة واستماع الخطبة"

**Translation:** "Allah commands His believing servants to attend the Jumu'ah prayer and to hasten to it from the time the call is made... And in the verse is evidence for the **obligation (wujub)** of attending Jumu'ah and listening to the khutbah."

**Source:** Shaykh `Abd al-Rahman as-Sa'di, *Taysir al-Karim al-Rahman fi Tafsir Kalam al-Mannan*, Tafsir of Surah al-Jumu'ah, Ayah 9.

### Shaykh Ibn Baz on the obligation:

> "لا يجوز الكلام والإمام يخطب مع الناس، لا مع العاطس ولا مع غيره، الواجب الإنصات لسماع الخطيب"

**Translation:** "It is not permissible to speak while the imam is giving the khutbah to the people — neither to the one who sneezes nor to anyone else. It is **obligatory (al-wajib)** to listen attentively to the khatib."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة"

### The Hadith on Speaking During the Khutbah:

> "إِذَا قُلْتَ لِصَاحِبِكَ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ أَنْصِتْ وَالْإِمَامُ يَخْطُبُ فَقَدْ لَغَوْتَ"

**Translation:** "If you say to your companion 'be quiet' on the day of Jumu'ah while the imam is giving the khutbah, you have engaged in idle talk (laghw)."

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith no. 934; Sahih Muslim, Hadith no. 851.
**Grading:** **Sahih (authentic)** — agreed upon (muttafaq 'alayh).

Shaykh Ibn Baz explained:

> "مع أنه أمره بالمعروف، سماه لاغياً وهو آمر بالمعروف... هذا يدل على أن الجمعة تلغو ويفوته ثوابها بسبب كلامه حال الخطبة، ولو أن كلامه في أمر طيب، في أمر بمعروف، أو نهي عن منكر، أو تشميت عاطس."

**Translation:** "Even though he was commanding the good, the Prophet ﷺ called him 'one who engages in idle talk (laghi)' while he was commanding the good... This indicates that the Jumu'ah is nullified (tulghu) and its reward is lost due to his speaking during the khutbah, even if his speech is about something good — commanding good, forbidding evil, or saying 'yarhamukullah' to a sneezer."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, "حكم الكلام أثناء خطبة الجمعة"

## What to Do If You Doze Off During the Khutbah {#what-to-do-if-you-doze-off}

Based on the authentic hadith and the guidance of the scholars:

1. **Change your sitting place** — The Prophet ﷺ said: "If one of you dozes off on the day of Jumu'ah, let him move from his sitting place to another." (al-Tirmidhi 526, sahih by al-Albani)

2. **Try to repel the sleep** as much as you are able — The scholars stated: "he must repel it as much as he is able."

3. **If the sleep becomes heavy** (deep sleep that breaks wudu) — you should leave, make wudu, and return for the prayer.

4. **If someone next to you is sleeping** — it is recommended to wake them **by action, not by speech** (since speaking during the khutbah is itself prohibited). Shaykh Ibn Baz ruled:

> "يستحب إيقاظهم بالفعل لا بالكلام، لأن الكلام في وقت الخطبة لا يجوز"

**Translation:** "It is **recommended (mustahabb)** to wake them **by action, not by speech**, because speaking during the khutbah is not permissible."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Baz, *Majmu' Fatawa wa Maqalat Mutanawwi'ah*, vol. 30, no. 182.

This is supported by the practice of Ibn 'Umar (may Allah be pleased with them both), who threw pebbles at two men talking during the khutbah to tell them to be quiet — using action rather than speech.

## The Prohibition of Ihtiba' (Sitting Cross-Legged) During the Khutbah {#prohibition-of-ihtiba}

The Prophet ﷺ specifically forbade a sitting posture that induces sleep during the khutbah:

> "أَنَّهُ نَهَى عَنِ الْحُبْوَةِ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ وَالْإِمَامُ يَخْطُبُ"

**Translation:** "He [the Prophet ﷺ] forbade ihtiba' (sitting hugging one's knees / sitting cross-legged) on the day of Jumu'ah while the imam is giving the khutbah."

**Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud, Sunan al-Tirmidhi, from Sahl ibn Mu'adh from his father.
**Grading by al-Albani:** **Hasan**.

**The reason for the prohibition**, as explained by al-Khattabi:

> "نهى عنها لأنها تجلب النوم فتعرض طهارته للنقض، ويمنع من استماع الخطبة"

**Translation:** "He forbade it because it **brings on sleep**, thereby exposing one's wudu to nullification, and it **prevents listening to the khutbah**."

**Source:** al-Khattabi in *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi*, cited by al-Nawawi in *al-Majmu'*.

This is significant because it shows that the scholars understood that **sleep during the khutbah could break wudu** — which is one of the reasons this posture was discouraged.

## Wudu Breaking During the Khutbah: What Should You Do? {#wudu-breaks-during-khutbah}

If your wudu breaks during the khutbah — whether by passing wind, deep sleep, or any other nullifier — you should:

1. **Leave the masjid** (quietly, without stepping over people unnecessarily)
2. **Make wudu**
3. **Return** to catch the prayer

The Permanent Committee confirmed this is permissible and addressed what to do upon returning:

> "Whoever leaves the Masjid to perform Wudu' because he invalidated his Wudu' then comes back, it is a Sunnah for him to offer two Rak'ahs before he sits as Tahiyyat-ul-Masjid (two-unit Prayer to salute the mosque) because he is included in the general saying of the Prophet (peace be upon him): 'If anyone of you enters a Mosque, he should not sit until he has offered a two-Rak'ahs prayer.'"

**Source:** Permanent Committee, Fatwa No. 20123, Part No. 6, Page No. 140. Members: Bakr Abu Zayd, Salih Al-Fawzan, `Abdullah ibn Ghudayyan, `Abdul-`Aziz Al-Shaykh, `Abdul-`Aziz ibn `Abdullah ibn Baz.

### Key Principle: Taharah for Khutbah vs. Taharah for Salah

A crucial distinction made by the scholars:

- **Taharah is NOT a condition for the khutbah** — one can listen to (or deliver) the khutbah without wudu.
- **Taharah IS a condition for the salah** — one cannot pray without wudu.

Shaykh Ibn Baz stated:

> "الخطبة لا يشترط لها الطهارة"

**Translation:** "The khutbah does not require taharah."

The Permanent Committee confirmed:

> "الوضوء ليس شرطا لها [الخطبة]، وإنما الوضوء شرط لكل صلاة قبل الدخول فيها"

**Translation:** "Wudu is not a condition for it [the khutbah]; rather, wudu is a condition for every prayer before entering into it."

**Source:** Permanent Committee, Fatwa No. 16863

This means: if your wudu breaks during the khutbah, you can continue listening to the khutbah without wudu — but you MUST renew your wudu before the prayer begins.

## What If You Prayed Jumu'ah Without Realizing Your Wudu Broke? {#prayed-without-wudu}

This is the most critical scenario for your question. If you slept deeply during the khutbah (which broke your wudu), did not realize it, and then prayed Jumu'ah without renewing wudu:

### The prayer is INVALID (batil) by consensus (ijma')

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah stated:

> "وَكَذَلِكَ ‌مَنْ ‌نَسِيَ ‌طَهَارَةَ ‌الْحَدَثِ، ‌وَصَلَّى ‌نَاسِيًا: فَعَلَيْهِ أَنْ يُعِيدَ الصَّلَاةَ بِطَهَارَةِ، بِلَا نِزَاعٍ"

**Translation:** "Likewise, **whoever forgets purification from hadath (minor impurity) and prays out of forgetfulness: it is upon him to repeat the prayer with purification, without dispute (bila niza')**."

**Source:** Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, *Majmu' al-Fatawa*

Imam al-Nawawi stated the consensus:

> "أجمعت الأمة على أن من صلى محدثًا مع إمكان الوضوء فصلاته باطلة، وتجب إعادتها بالإجماع، سواء أتعمد ذلك أم نسيه أم جهله"

**Translation:** "The Ummah has agreed by consensus that whoever prays while in a state of impurity, while being able to make wudu, his prayer is invalid (batilah), and its repetition is obligatory by consensus — whether he did so deliberately, forgetfully, or out of ignorance."

**Source:** Imam al-Nawawi, cited in *Mawsu'at Ahkam al-Salawat al-Khams*, p. 33

### Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen's fatwa on a similar case:

A person asked: "On Friday, I made ghusl for Jumu'ah and intended to make wudu after this ghusl, but I forgot the wudu and prayed Jumu'ah. What is upon me?"

Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen answered:

> "نعم... إذا لم يرتفع الحدث به وصلى الإنسان الجمعة بغير وضوء فإنه يعيدها ظهراً، لأنه ما نوى رفع الحدث... وعلى هذا نقول للأخ السائل : الأحوط في حقك أن تعيد صلاة الجمعة ظهراً، وأنت إن شاء الله قد حصَّلت الأجر، لأنك لم تتعمد الصلاة بغير وضوء."

**Translation:** "Yes... if the hadath is not lifted by it and the person prayed Jumu'ah without wudu, he must repeat it as Dhuhr, because he did not intend lifting the hadath... Based on this, we say to the questioner: The safest course for you is to repeat the Jumu'ah prayer as Dhuhr, and you, if Allah wills, have obtained the reward, because you did not deliberately pray without wudu."

**Source:** Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen, Liqa' al-Shahri (Monthly Meeting) No. 71.

### Shaykh al-Fawzan on praying without wudu:

> "صلاتك غير صحيحة على كل حال؛ لأنه ما دام أنك: على غير وضوء فلا تصح منك الصلاة... وما فعلته من الاستمرار في الصلاة بعد علمك أنك لست على طهارة هذا خطأ كبير، كان الواجب عليك أن تنصرف، وأن تخرج من المسجد وأن تتوضأ وترجع لإدراك ما بقي من الصلاة مع الجماعة"

**Translation:** "Your prayer is invalid in any case; because as long as you are without wudu, no prayer is valid from you... and what you did by continuing in the prayer after knowing you were not in a state of purity is a grave mistake. It was obligatory upon you to leave, to exit the masjid, to make wudu, and to return to catch what remains of the prayer with the congregation."

**Source:** Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan, "الصلاة على غير وضوء"

### Important: No sin if you were ignorant or forgetful

Imam al-Nawawi added:

> "لكنه إن صلى جاهلًا أو ناسيًا فلا إثم عليه"

**Translation:** "However, if he prayed out of ignorance or forgetfulness, there is no sin upon him."

So even if your prayer was invalid, you are not sinful if you genuinely didn't realize your wudu had broken. But you must still repeat the prayer as Dhuhr (4 rak'ahs), since the time for Jumu'ah has passed and Jumu'ah cannot be "made up."

## Practical Scenarios Answered {#practical-scenarios}

Let me now lay out the different scenarios clearly so you can determine which one applies to your situation:

### Scenario 1: You dozed off briefly but could still hear the khutbah

- **Type of sleep:** Drowsiness (al-nu'as)
- **Wudu status:** **INTACT** — drowsiness does not break wudu
- **Jumu'ah validity:** **VALID** — you can pray with your existing wudu
- **Evidence:** The Companions' heads would nod in drowsiness while waiting for 'Isha', yet they would pray without renewing wudu. (Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim)
- **Action needed:** None — your prayer is valid. However, you should try to stay alert and change your sitting place if you feel drowsy, following the Prophetic guidance.

### Scenario 2: You slept more deeply but would have felt it if you passed wind

- **Type of sleep:** Light sleep (according to Ibn Uthaymeen's criterion)
- **Wudu status:** **INTACT** — according to Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, and the Permanent Committee, if you would have felt it if you passed wind, your wudu is not broken
- **Jumu'ah validity:** **VALID**
- **Evidence:** Shaykh Ibn Uthaymeen: "as long as the person senses himself — such that if he passed wind he would know — then his sleep does not break his wudu." (*al-Sharh al-Mumti'* 1/170)
- **Note:** According to Shaykh al-Albani's stricter view, if you could no longer hear sounds at all, your wudu would be broken even in this scenario. But the majority position is that your wudu is intact.
- **Action needed:** None according to the majority. To be safe, you could renew your wudu before the prayer if you have any doubt.

### Scenario 3: You slept deeply and completely lost awareness (couldn't hear anything, wouldn't have felt passing wind)

- **Type of sleep:** Deep sleep (al-nawm al-mustaghriq)
- **Wudu status:** **BROKEN** — by consensus of all scholars
- **Jumu'ah validity if you prayed without renewing wudu:** **INVALID** — must be repeated as Dhuhr (4 rak'ahs)
- **Jumu'ah validity if you renewed wudu before praying:** **VALID**
- **Evidence:** The Permanent Committee: "Deep/overwhelming sleep is an occasion for nullification of wudu. So whoever sleeps a deep sleep in the mosque or elsewhere, he must repeat his wudu." (Fatwa No. 3030)
- **Action needed:** If you realized before the prayer, leave and make wudu. If you already prayed without realizing, you must repeat the prayer as Dhuhr (4 rak'ahs). There is no sin on you if you were genuinely ignorant.

### Scenario 4: You're not sure which type of sleep it was

- **Type of sleep:** Uncertain
- **Wudu status:** **INTACT** (original assumption) — according to Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn Baz
- **Jumu'ah validity:** **VALID** — but to be safe, renew your wudu if possible
- **Evidence:** Ibn Taymiyyah: "if the one with wudu doubts: is his sleep of the type that breaks [wudu] or not? Then he does not rule that wudu is broken; because purity is established with certainty and is not removed by doubt." (*Majmu' al-Fatawa* 21/228-230)
- **Evidence:** Ibn Baz: "The original assumption is non-nullification of wudu; the original assumption is the soundness of wudu until he knows and is certain that he slept a sleep that removes his awareness."
- **Action needed:** Your prayer is valid based on the principle of certainty. However, renewing wudu is always safer if you have any doubt.

### Scenario 5: You know you didn't pass wind, touch private parts, or do anything to break wudu — but you did sleep

This is essentially your described situation. The answer depends on the type of sleep:

- **If it was drowsiness/light sleep (you could still hear sounds, would have felt it if you passed wind):** Your wudu is intact and your Jumu'ah is valid. The fact that you didn't pass wind, touch private parts, etc. confirms that no nullifier occurred. Sleep itself is not a nullifier — it is only an *occasion* for hadath. If no hadath occurred (and you would have felt it if it did), your wudu is intact.

- **If it was deep sleep (you completely lost awareness):** The majority of scholars (Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, Permanent Committee) say wudu is broken because deep sleep is a mazhanna for hadath — the assumption is that wind may have escaped without you knowing. Even if you're sure you didn't fart, the deep sleep itself is treated as breaking wudu by all the scholars (the disagreement is only about what counts as "deep"). Al-Albani is even stricter — any sleep where you can't hear sounds breaks wudu.

### Summary Table of Scenarios

| Scenario | Sleep Type | Wudu Status | Jumu'ah Valid? | Action Required |
|----------|-----------|-------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Dozed briefly, could hear khutbah | Drowsiness (nu'as) | Intact | **YES** | None |

## Summary and Practical Guidance {#summary}

### Key Takeaways:

1. **Sleep is not hadath itself** — it is a *mazhanna* (occasion) for hadath. The concern is that wind might escape without you knowing.

2. **Drowsiness (nu'as) does NOT break wudu** — all scholars agree on this. If you nodded off briefly but could still hear the khutbah, your wudu is intact and your Jumu'ah is valid.

3. **Deep sleep (nawm mustaghriq) DOES break wudu** — all scholars agree on this. If you completely lost awareness, you must renew your wudu before praying.

4. **The criterion (according to the majority):** Would you have felt it if you passed wind? If yes, your wudu is intact. If no, your wudu is broken.

5. **The criterion (according to al-Albani):** Could you still hear sounds? If yes, it's drowsiness (wudu intact). If no, it's sleep (wudu broken).

6. **Position does NOT matter** — sitting, leaning, or lying down are all the same. The hadith restricting wudu-breaking sleep to lying down is weak (da'if).

7. **Sleeping during the khutbah does not automatically invalidate the Jumu'ah prayer** — what matters is whether your wudu was intact when you prayed.

8. **If you prayed without wudu (because deep sleep broke it and you didn't realize):** The prayer is invalid by consensus (ijma') and must be repeated as Dhuhr (4 rak'ahs). However, there is no sin on you if you were genuinely ignorant or forgetful.

9. **In case of doubt:** The original assumption is that your wudu is intact (principle of certainty). Purity is established with certainty and is not removed by doubt.

10. **If your wudu breaks during the khutbah:** Leave quietly, make wudu, and return. The khutbah does not require taharah, but the prayer does.

### For Your Specific Situation:

You mentioned:
- You slept during the khutbah while sitting
- You know you didn't fart, touch private parts, or do anything to break wudu
- You're sure you did sleep "like real"

Based on this, ask yourself: **During that sleep, would you have felt it if you passed wind? Could you still hear the khutbah (even if you didn't understand the words)?**

- **If yes** → Your sleep was drowsiness/light sleep → **Your wudu is intact and your Jumu'ah is valid.** (This is the majority position of Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, and the Permanent Committee.)

- **If no** (you were completely "out," couldn't hear anything, wouldn't have felt anything) → Your sleep was deep sleep → **Your wudu broke, and if you prayed without renewing it, you must repeat the prayer as Dhuhr (4 rak'ahs).** There is no sin on you if you didn't realize.

- **If you're not sure** → The original assumption is that your wudu is intact (by the principle of certainty), and your Jumu'ah is valid. But to be safe, you may want to repeat it as Dhuhr if you have any doubt.

And Allah knows best.
