# Using Windows Without Activation: Is It Halal to Use Third-Party Tools for Dark Mode and Personalization?

## The Question

You want to use Windows without activating it (avoiding cracks/KMS/activators, which are clearly haram). Microsoft allows installation and use of unactivated Windows, but locks personalization features (dark mode, light mode, wallpapers, themes, colors) behind activation. You ask: **Can I use third-party tools (not cracks, not activation bypasses) to change settings like dark mode that Microsoft has locked behind activation?**

## Short Answer

**No direct fatwa from a Salafi scholar was found addressing this exact scenario.** However, based on the general principles established by the scholars (Ibn Baz, al-Uthaymin, al-Albani, al-Munajjid, and the Islamic Fiqh Council), **using third-party tools to bypass Microsoft's personalization lockout appears to be impermissible (haram)**, because it violates the terms and conditions you agreed to when installing Windows.

This is a matter of caution — when in doubt, leave it. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Leave that which makes you doubt for that which does not make you doubt."
> (Narrated by al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i; graded **sahih** by al-Albani)

---

## The Two Scenarios — A Critical Distinction

### A) Piracy / Cracking (KMS, activators, cracks) — CLEARLY HARAM

This is explicitly prohibited by the scholars and the Islamic Fiqh Council. No ambiguity here.

### B) Third-party tools to change settings without activating — LIKELY HARAM (by general principle)

No direct fatwa exists on this exact case, but the established principles point to it being impermissible because it breaches the agreed terms of use.

---

## The Evidence — Why It Appears to Be Haram

### 1. The Quran Commands Fulfilling Contracts

Allah (سبحانه وتعالى) says:

> **{يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَوْفُوا بِالْعُقُودِ}**
>
> "O you who have believed, fulfill [all] contracts."
> (Quran, Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:1)

And He says:

> **{وَأَوْفُوا بِالْعَهْدِ ۖ إِنَّ الْعَهْدَ كَانَ مَسْئُولًا}**
>
> "And fulfill the covenant. Indeed, the covenant will be questioned about."
> (Quran, Surah Al-Isra 17:34)

When you install Windows, you agree to Microsoft's Software License Terms (EULA). Those terms restrict personalization to activated copies. Bypassing that restriction with third-party tools is a breach of the contract you agreed to.

### 2. The Prophet ﷺ Bound Muslims to Their Conditions

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> **"المسلمون على شروطهم إلا شرطًا حرم حلالًا أو أحل حرامًا"**
>
> "The Muslims are bound by their conditions, except a condition that makes the lawful unlawful or the unlawful lawful."
> (Narrated by al-Tirmidhi 1352 and Abu Dawud 3594; **graded sahih** by al-Albani in Sahih al-Tirmidhi)

The restriction of personalization to activated copies does **not** make a halal thing haram or a haram thing halal — it is a valid business condition. Therefore, the Muslim is bound by it.

### 3. Violating Software Terms Is a Breach of Intellectual Rights

IslamWeb's Fatwa Center ruled:

> "With regard to the violation of the terms and conditions of the owner of the software program, according to the preponderant opinion, **it is an obligation to abide by those terms and it is not permissible to violate them**. These conditions imply the possession of a utility to utilize it according to a determined extent and a specific manner. Based on such conditions, the contract has been concluded and agreed upon. So, violating such conditions constitutes a breach of the intellectual rights."
> (IslamWeb, Fatwa No. 264511 — https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/264511)

### 4. The Islamic Fiqh Council Affirmed Copyright Protection

The Islamic Fiqh Council (5th session, Kuwait, 1405 AH) stated:

> "Copyrights belong to their holders and have, in contemporary times, financial value that can be traded. These rights are recognized by the sharee'ah, and it is impermissible to violate them."
> (Cited in IslamWeb Fatwa No. 327097 — https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/327097)

### 5. Shaykh Ibn 'Uthaymin (may Allah have mercy on him) on Conditions

> "The ruling on stipulated conditions in contracts is that if the conditions are valid and permissible, they must be fulfilled... because of the general meaning of the verse in which Allah, may He be exalted, says: **{O you who have believed, fulfill [all] contracts}** [al-Ma'idah 5:1]. Fulfilling contracts includes fulfilling all the conditions and clauses thereof, because all of that is part of the contract."
> (Shaykh Ibn 'Uthaymin, *Ash-Sharh al-Mumti'* 12/164; cited in IslamQA Fatwa 308096)

### 6. IslamWeb on Breaching Software Terms

> "One must adhere to the conditions that he accepts relating to the permission of the owner of the software program for downloading it and the like, **unless these are conditions for something unlawful, because unlawful conditions are void** and it is not an obligation to fulfill them."
> (IslamWeb, Fatwa No. 283420 — https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/283420)

The key exception: conditions are only void if they require something **haram**. Restricting personalization to activated copies is not a haram condition — it is a permissible business term. So the exception does not apply here.

### 7. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him)

> "Rights are defined by stipulated conditions."
> (Narrated by al-Bayhaqi 14826; **graded sahih** by al-Albani in *al-Irwa'* 6/303)

## The Counter-Argument (and Why It Is Weak)

One might argue: "Microsoft allows me to use unactivated Windows for free, so I'm not stealing. I'm just changing a setting on my own computer."

**Why this is weak:**
- The issue is not theft of the software — it is **breach of the agreed terms**. You accepted a contract that says personalization requires activation. Using a third-party tool to circumvent that is breaking your word.
- The hadith "Muslims are bound by their conditions" applies to **all valid conditions**, not just conditions about money.
- IslamWeb explicitly ruled that violating software terms "constitutes a breach of the intellectual rights" (Fatwa 264511).

---

## What IS Halal — Your Permissible Options

### Option 1: Use Linux (Best Option — Fully Free and Open)

Linux distributions (Ubuntu, Linux Mint, Fedora, etc.) are:
- Completely free and open source
- Require no activation
- Allow full personalization (dark mode, themes, wallpapers) without restriction
- Permissible to use — IslamQA confirmed there is nothing wrong with downloading free programs available on the internet (IslamQA Fatwa 116782)

This is the cleanest option Islamically. You get full customization with zero contractual concerns.

### Option 2: Use Windows Unactivated As-Is

Microsoft permits use of unactivated Windows. You can:
- Use it for all your normal tasks
- Accept the personalization limitations (default wallpaper, no theme switching via Settings)
- Live with the "Activate Windows" watermark in the corner

This is permissible — you are using the software exactly as the owner permits.

### Option 3: Buy a Legitimate Windows License

If you need Windows with personalization:
- Purchase a genuine license key
- This fulfills the contract and unlocks all features lawfully
- This is the most straightforward halal solution if you must use Windows

**Note:** Be cautious of suspiciously cheap keys sold on grey-market sites — many are stolen, fraudulently obtained, or violate Microsoft's terms. Buy from Microsoft or an authorized retailer.

| Using third-party tools to change dark mode/personalization on unactivated Windows | **Likely Haram** (no direct fatwa, but violates agreed terms) | Breach of contract/conditions |
| Using Linux with full customization | **Halal** | Free, open source, no restrictions |

## Important Disclaimer

**No Salafi scholar was found to have issued a direct fatwa on this specific scenario** (third-party tools to change Windows personalization without activation). The conclusion above is based on applying the general principles established by the scholars to this case.

For a definitive ruling, it is strongly recommended to **consult a knowledgeable Salafi scholar directly** with this specific question. Until then, the safest path is to avoid the doubtful matter, as the Prophet ﷺ advised.
