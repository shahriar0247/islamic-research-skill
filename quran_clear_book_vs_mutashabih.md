# Is the Quran a "Clear Book" If It Contains Ambiguous Verses?

## A Salafi Scholarly Answer to a Common Doubt

A common question arises from a sincere reading of the Quran: **Allah repeatedly describes the Quran as a "clear book" (Kitab Mubeen), as "guidance" (Huda), as "light" (Nur), and as "clear proof" (Bayyinah/Burhan) — yet Surah Al Imran 3:7 explicitly states that some verses are *muhkam* (clear) and others are *mutashabih* (unclear/ambiguous).** Does the existence of ambiguous verses — verses with deeper meanings, multiple possible interpretations, or meanings known only to Allah — make the Quran an "unclear book"? Is there a contradiction between the Quran's self-description as clear and its own admission that parts of it are not entirely clear?

This article answers that question using verbatim quotes from the major Salafi scholars — Ibn Kathir, As-Sa'di, Ibn Uthaymeen, Ibn Baz, and others (may Allah have mercy on them) — to show that there is no contradiction, and that the scholars have a clear, well-established reconciliation.

---

## 1. The Verse at the Heart of the Question

The foundational verse addressing this is **Surah Al Imran 3:7**:

> **{هُوَ الَّذِي أَنزَلَ عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ مِنْهُ آيَاتٌ مُّحْكَمَاتٌ هُنَّ أُمُّ الْكِتَابِ وَأُخَرُ مُتَشَابِهَاتٌ ۖ فَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ زَيْغٌ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ مَا تَشَابَهَ مِنْهُ ابْتِغَاءَ الْفِتْنَةِ وَابْتِغَاءَ تَأْوِيلِهِ ۖ وَمَا يَعْلَمُ تَأْوِيلَهُ إِلَّا اللَّهُ ۗ وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ يَقُولُونَ آمَنَّا بِهِ كُلٌّ مِّنْ عِندِ رَبِّنَا ۗ وَمَا يَذَّكَّرُ إِلَّا أُولُو الْأَلْبَابِ}**

> *"It is He who has sent down to you the Book; in it are verses [that are] precise - they are the foundation of the Book - and others unspecific. As for those in whose hearts is deviation, they will follow that of it which is unspecific, seeking discord and seeking an interpretation [suitable to them]. And no one knows its [true] interpretation except Allah. But those firm in knowledge say, 'We believe in it. All [of it] is from our Lord.' And no one will be reminded except those of understanding."*
> — (Quran, Surah Al Imran [3]:7)

This verse is the key to understanding the entire question. It explicitly divides the Quran into two categories:
1. **Muhkam** (محكم) — precise, clear, unambiguous verses; "the foundation of the Book" (Umm al-Kitab)
2. **Mutashabih** (متشابه) — unspecific, ambiguous verses whose meaning is not entirely clear to everyone

## 2. The Quran's Self-Description as "Clear"

Before addressing the reconciliation, it is important to establish that Allah **does** describe the Quran as a clear book in numerous verses. This is not a minor or incidental description — it is one of the most repeated attributes of the Quran:

### As "Kitab Mubeen" (Clear Book):
- (Quran, Surah Yusuf [12]:1) — *"Alif, Lam, Ra. These are the verses of the clear Book."*
- (Quran, Surah Al-Ma'idah [5]:15) — *"There has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book."*
- (Quran, Surah Ash-Shu'ara [26]:2) — *"These are the verses of the clear Book."*
- (Quran, Surah An-Naml [27]:1) — *"Ta, Sin. These are the verses of the Quran and a clear Book."*
- (Quran, Surah Al-Qasas [28]:2) — *"These are the verses of the clear Book."*
- (Quran, Surah Az-Zukhruf [43]:2) — *"By the clear Book."*
- (Quran, Surah Ad-Dukhan [44]:2) — *"By the clear Book."*

### As "Huda" (Guidance):
- (Quran, Surah Al-Baqarah [2]:2) — *"This is the Book about which there is no doubt, a guidance for those conscious of Allah."*
- (Quran, Surah Al-Baqarah [2]:185) — *"The Quran is guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion."*

### As "Nur" (Light):
- (Quran, Surah An-Nisa [4]:174) — *"There has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book."*
- (Quran, Surah Ash-Shura [42]:52) — *"...but We have made it a light by which We guide whom We will of Our servants."*

### As "Bayyinah/Burhan" (Clear Proof):
- (Quran, Surah Al-Baqarah [2]:159) — *"Indeed, those who conceal what We sent down of clear proofs and guidance..."*
- (Quran, Surah Al-Bayyinah [98]:1) — *"...until there came to them clear evidence."*

### As "Clear Arabic":
- (Quran, Surah An-Nahl [16]:103) — *"...and this Quran is [in] a clear Arabic language."*
- (Quran, Surah Ash-Shu'ara [26]:195) — *"In a clear Arabic language."*

### As Made Easy to Understand:
- (Quran, Surah Al-Qamar [54]:17) — *"And We have certainly made the Quran easy for remembrance, so is there any who will remember?"* (repeated in 54:22, 54:32, 54:40)

So the question is legitimate: **if Allah describes the Quran as clear in so many places, how can the same Book contain unclear verses?**

## 3. The Scholars' Definition of Muhkam and Mutashabih

### Ibn Kathir (d. 774H) (may Allah have mercy on him)

Ibn Kathir explains in his Tafsir on 3:7:

> *"Allah states that in the Qur'an, there are Ayat that are Muhkamat, entirely clear and plain, and these are the foundations of the Book which are plain for everyone. And there are Ayat in the Qur'an that are Mutashabihat not entirely clear for many, or some people. So those who refer to the Muhkam Ayat to understand the Mutashabih Ayat, will have acquired the correct guidance, and vice versa."*
> — **Ibn Kathir**, Tafsir Ibn Kathir ([source](https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/3/7/))

He further clarifies what each category contains:

> *"The Muhkamat are the Ayat that explain the abrogating rulings, the allowed, prohibited, laws, limits, obligations and rulings that should be believed in and implemented. As for the Mutashabihat Ayat, they include the abrogated Ayat, parables, oaths, and what should be believed in, but not implemented."*
> — **Ibn Kathir**, Tafsir Ibn Kathir ([source](https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/3/7/))

**Key point**: The *muhkam* verses contain the **rulings, laws, and obligations** — the practical guidance that people must implement. The *mutashabih* verses are things to be *believed in* but not necessarily implemented as rulings.

### Ibn Baz (d. 1420H) (may Allah have mercy on him)

Shaykh Ibn Baz gave a concise definition:

> *"أحسن ما قيل في ذلك: أن المحكم هو ما وضح معناه، والمتشابه: ما يخفى معناه على العالم، والواجب ردّ المشتبه إلى المحكم، وألا يُفسّر بشيءٍ يُخالف المحكم... فالمحكم من القرآن: ما وضح معناه، كآيات الصلاة، وما حرَّم الله من المحرَّمات، وغير ذلك، وما اشتبه معناه يُردّ إلى المحكم، ويُفسّر بالمحكم، ولا يجوز أبدًا أن يُفسّر بخلاف المحكم؛ لأن القرآن لا يتناقض، بل يصدق بعضُه بعضًا، ويُشبه بعضه بعضًا."*

> *"The best that has been said concerning this is: the muhkam is that whose meaning is clear, and the mutashabih is that whose meaning is hidden from the scholar. The obligation is to refer the ambiguous back to the clear, and not to interpret it in a way that contradicts the clear... The muhkam of the Quran is that whose meaning is clear, such as the verses of prayer, and what Allah has forbidden of prohibitions, and so on. Whatever meaning is ambiguous is referred back to the muhkam and interpreted by the muhkam. It is never permissible to interpret it contrary to the muhkam, because the Quran does not contradict itself; rather, part of it confirms part of it, and part of it resembles part of it."*
> — **Shaykh Abdul-Aziz ibn Baz** ([source](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/21213/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%82-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%87-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86))

### IslamQA (citing the scholars)

> *"The entirely clear verses (muhkam) are those which are clear and concerning which there is no confusion; this applies to most of the verses of the Qur'aan. These verses are the 'foundations of the Book'. As for the verses which are not entirely clear (mutashaabih) these are verses which may not be clear to some people, in exclusion to others. The scholars know them but the ignorant do not, and there are some which only Allaah knows."*
> — **IslamQA**, Fatwa 103146 ([source](https://islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/103146))

**Critical point**: The muhkam verses are **"most of the verses of the Qur'aan."** The mutashabih are a minority. The Quran is predominantly clear.

## 4. The Reconciliation: How the Quran Is Both "Clear" and Contains "Unclear" Verses

### 4.1 The Quran Is Clear in Its Overall Purpose and Guidance

IslamWeb explains the intent behind Allah describing the Quran as clear:

> *"You should know that, by this revelation, Allah, The Exalted, intended to establish the irrefutable proof beyond the excuse of ignorance or reasonable doubt for people (so that mankind would have no argument against Allah). This cannot be done by means of incomprehensible, unclear, and ambiguous evidence but rather requires evidence that is clear-cut and unambiguous. Therefore, Allah, The Exalted, described His Book as Bayyin (clear) in terms of its wording and meanings."*
> — **IslamWeb**, Fatwa 432235 ([source](https://islamweb.net/en/fatwa/432235/the-quran-is-clear-cut-evidence-but-scholars-may-differ-in-their-understanding-as-it-includes-muhkamaat-and-mutashaabihaat-verses))

The "clarity" of the Quran refers to its **overall purpose**: to establish irrefutable proof and guidance for humanity. The core message — Tawheed (oneness of Allah), prophethood, the Hereafter, the basic moral and legal rulings — is crystal clear and repeated throughout the Book. The mutashabih verses do not obscure this core message.

### 4.2 The Muhkam Verses Are the Foundation (Umm al-Kitab)

Allah calls the muhkam verses **"Umm al-Kitab"** (the foundation of the Book). Ibn Kathir explains:

> *"Allah states that in the Qur'an, there are Ayat that are Muhkamat, entirely clear and plain, and these are the foundations of the Book which are plain for everyone."*
> — **Ibn Kathir**, Tafsir Ibn Kathir ([source](https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/3/7/))

As-Sa'di (d. 1376H) (may Allah have mercy on him) explains how the firm in knowledge handle the mutashabih:

> *"فلعلمهم أن المحكمات معناها في غاية الصراحة والبيان، يردون إليها المشتبه الذي تحصل فيه الحيرة لناقص العلم وناقص المعرفة، فيردون المتشابه إلى المحكم فيعود كله محكما ويقولون: {آمنا به كل من عند ربنا}"*

> *"Due to their knowledge that the muhkamat are extremely clear and plain in meaning, they refer to them the ambiguous verses that cause confusion for those deficient in knowledge and understanding. So they refer the mutashabih back to the muhkam, and thus all of it becomes muhkam (clear), and they say: 'We believe in it; all of it is from our Lord.'"*
> — **As-Sa'di**, Tafsir As-Sa'di ([source](https://quranenc.com/aa/browse/arabic_saadi/3))

**This is the key reconciliation**: When the mutashabih is referred back to the muhkam, **"all of it becomes muhkam (clear)."** The mutashabih verses are not permanently unclear — they become clear when interpreted in light of the muhkam verses.

### 4.3 The Mutashabih Verses Are Not Unclear to Everyone

IslamWeb clarifies an important point:

> *"The soundest scholarly opinion regarding verse 3:7 is that the Mutashabih verses refers to those whose indications are not clear to other than the scholars. Only the scholars understand them clearly. Hence, there are no Quranic verses whose meaning is unknown to all people."*
> — **IslamWeb**, Fatwa 276151 ([source](https://islamweb.net/en/fatwa/276151/the-obscure-verses-in-the-quran-are-not-obscure-to-everyone))

This is a crucial distinction: the mutashabih verses are not "unclear" in an absolute sense. They are unclear **to those who lack knowledge**, but the scholars (the Raasikhoon fil-Ilm — those firm in knowledge) understand them. There is **no verse in the Quran whose meaning is unknown to all people** — except for matters of the unseen whose reality only Allah knows (such as the exact nature of His attributes, or the exact reality of the Hereafter).

### 4.4 The Quran Explains Itself, and the Sunnah Explains the Quran

IslamWeb further explains:

> *"Some verses of the Quran may denote more than one meaning; these are called Mutashaabihaat (verses with unclear indications). Meanwhile, other verses with clear indications (Muhkamaat) adequately explain the Mutashaabihaat ones. Hence, some verses of the Quran explain others, and the Sunnah also clarifies the unclear indications of the Mutashaabihaat verses. The Sunnah as well explains and clarifies the meanings of the verses of the Quran. Thus, both the Quran and the Sunnah provide explanations for the verses whose specific intended meanings need to be identified. The Quran is not a book of mysteries..."*
> — **IslamWeb**, Fatwa 432235 ([source](https://islamweb.net/en/fatwa/432235/the-quran-is-clear-cut-evidence-but-scholars-may-differ-in-their-understanding-as-it-includes-muhkamaat-and-mutashaabihaat-verses))

**"The Quran is not a book of mysteries."** The mutashabih verses are not puzzles left unsolved — they are explained by:
1. Other (muhkam) verses of the Quran
2. The Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ

## 5. The Wisdom Behind Mutashabih Verses

If the Quran could have been entirely muhkam, why did Allah include mutashabih verses? The scholars explain the wisdom:

### Ibn Uthaymeen (d. 1421H) (may Allah have mercy on him)

> *"Allaah, The Exalted, divided the Holy Quran into two categories: the verses that are precise (muhkam) and those that are not entirely clear (mutashaabih)... The wisdom behind revealing the Quran in these two types is that this is a test, because those in whose hearts is deviation will follow that which is not entirely clear and will thus remain confused. Those who are well-versed in knowledge, however, believe in all of it, both that which is not entirely clear and that which is precise; they know that it is from Allaah and that there is no contradiction in it."*
> — **Shaykh Muhammad ibn Saalih al-'Uthaymeen** ([source](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/322657/wisdom-behind-verses-with-unclear-indication-mutashaabih))

### Ibn Kathir (d. 774H) (may Allah have mercy on him)

> *"As for the unclear Ayat, they can (but must not) be altered and changed, and this is a test from Allah to the servants, just as He tested them with the allowed and prohibited things. So these Ayat must not be altered to imply a false meaning or be distorted from the truth."*
> — **Ibn Kathir** ([source](https://abdurrahman.org/2016/08/26/clear-verses-muhkam-and-unclear-verses-mutashabihat-in-quran/))

The wisdom includes:
1. **A test** — to distinguish those who sincerely believe and refer to scholars from those with deviation in their hearts who seek to exploit ambiguity.
2. **Humility** — to remind the intellect that there are matters beyond its reach, and that one must submit to Allah's knowledge.
3. **Elevation of the scholars** — by giving them a role in explaining the mutashabih, Allah elevates their status.

## 6. The Danger of Following the Mutashabih

The verse in 3:7 contains a stern warning. Ibn Kathir explains:

> *"They follow that which is not entirely clear thereof, meaning, they refer to the Mutashabih, because they are able to alter its meanings to conform with their false interpretation since the wordings of the Mutashabihat encompass such a wide area of meanings. As for the Muhkam Ayat, they cannot be altered because they are clear and, thus, constitute unequivocal proof against the misguided people."*
> — **Ibn Kathir**, Tafsir Ibn Kathir ([source](https://livingnoor.com/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/215))

The Prophet ﷺ himself warned against such people, as reported by A'ishah (may Allah be pleased with her):

> *"The Messenger of Allah ﷺ recited this Ayah: 'It is He Who has sent down to you the Book. In it are verses that are entirely clear, they are the foundations of the Book; and others not entirely clear...' until '...men of understanding.' He then said: 'When you see those who argue in it (using the Mutashabihat), then they are those whom Allah meant. Therefore, beware of them.'"*
> — Reported by **A'ishah** (may Allah be pleased with her); collected in **Sahih al-Bukhari** (Book of Tafsir), **Sahih Muslim** (Book of Qadar), and **Abu Dawud** (Book of Sunnah) ([source](https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/3/7/))

Shaykh Ibn Baz quoted the Prophet ﷺ as saying:

> *"إذا رأيتم الذين يتَّبعون ما تشابه منه فأولئك الذين سمَّى الله فاحذروهم"*

> *"When you see those who follow that which is ambiguous of it, then those are the ones whom Allah has named [as having deviation], so beware of them."*
> — Quoted by **Shaykh Ibn Baz** ([source](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/21213/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%82-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%87-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86))

> **Note on hadith grading**: The al-Albani gradings for these specific narrations were not found in the approved Salafi sources searched. The narration is well-known and is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. (NOT FOUND FROM SALAFI SOURCE for the specific al-Albani grading.)

## 7. The Example of Allah's Names and Attributes

A practical example of muhkam vs. mutashabih is found in Allah's names and attributes. Shaykh Ibn Baz explained:

> *"هي من المُحْكَم، أسماء الله الحسنى من المُحْكَم من جهة المعنى، ولكن من جهة الكيفية لا يعلمها إلا الله، ولهذا قال السلف ورحمهم في الصفات قالوا فيها: معلومة والكيف مجهول، الاستواء معلوم، الرحمة معلومة، الغضب معلوم، الرضا معلوم، الضحك معلوم، والكيف غير معلوم، الكيف مجهول."*

> *"They are from the muhkam. The beautiful names of Allah are from the muhkam in terms of meaning, but in terms of the 'how' (kayfiyyah), no one knows it except Allah. This is why the Salaf (may Allah have mercy on them) said regarding the attributes: 'The meaning is known, and the how is unknown. The Istiwa (rising above the Throne) is known, the mercy is known, the anger is known, the pleasure is known, the laughter is known — but the how is not known; the how is unknown.'"*
> — **Shaykh Abdul-Aziz ibn Baz** ([source](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/30979/%D9%87%D9%84-%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D9%87-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%87))

This is a beautiful illustration: the **meaning** of Allah's attributes is clear (muhkam) — we know Allah hears, sees, speaks, rises above the Throne, etc. But the **how** (kayfiyyah) — the exact manner — is mutashabih, known only to Allah. This does not make the Quran "unclear"; rather, it teaches us to believe in what Allah has affirmed for Himself without delving into what He has kept hidden.

## 8. The Statement of Al-Hasan al-Basri (d. 110H)

From the early Salaf, Al-Hasan al-Basri (may Allah have mercy on him) summarized the believer's approach:

> *"They act according to and upon its clear and manifest verses (whose meanings are clear and apparent) and they believe in its allegorical verses (whose meanings are unclear) and they entrust whatever is difficult for them (to know) to its Knower."*
> — **Al-Hasan al-Basri** (d. 110H), via Salafi Publications ([source](https://www.salafipublications.com/sps/sp.cfm?subsecID=SCL05&articleID=SCL050003&articlePages=1))

This is the methodology of the Salaf: **act on the muhkam, believe in the mutashabih, and entrust the unknown to Allah.**

## 9. As-Sa'di on Who Knows the Ta'weel (Interpretation)

As-Sa'di (may Allah have mercy on him) addresses the famous grammatical question of 3:7 — whether "and no one knows its interpretation except Allah" stops at "Allah" or continues to "and those firm in knowledge":

> *"فإن التأويل إن أريد به علم حقيقة الشيء وكنهه كان الصواب الوقوف على { إلا الله } لأن المتشابه الذي استأثر الله بعلم كنهه وحقيقته، نحو حقائق صفات الله وكيفيتها، وحقائق أوصاف ما يكون في اليوم الآخر ونحو ذلك، فهذه لا يعلمها إلا الله، ولا يجوز التعرض للوقوف عليها... وإن أريد بالتأويل التفسير والكشف والإيضاح، كان الصواب عطف { الراسخون } على { الله } فيكون الله قد أخبر أن تفسير المتشابه ورده إلى المحكم وإزالة ما فيه من الشبهة لا يعلمها إلا هو تعالى والراسخون في العلم يعلمون أيضا، فيؤمنون بها ويردونها للمحكم ويقولون { كل } من المحكم والمتشابه { من عند ربنا }"*

> *"If by 'ta'weel' is meant knowledge of the true reality and essence of a thing, then the correct view is to stop at 'except Allah,' because the mutashabih whose reality and essence Allah has kept to Himself — such as the realities of Allah's attributes and their 'how,' and the realities of what will occur in the Hereafter, and similar things — these none knows except Allah, and it is not permissible to seek to know them... But if by 'ta'weel' is meant explanation, uncovering, and clarification, then the correct view is to connect 'those firm in knowledge' with 'Allah,' meaning that Allah has informed that the explanation of the mutashabih, referring it back to the muhkam, and removing the confusion in it — none knows it except Him, and those firm in knowledge also know it; so they believe in it, refer it to the muhkam, and say: 'All of it — the muhkam and the mutashabih — is from our Lord.'"*
> — **As-Sa'di**, Tafsir As-Sa'di ([source](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/saadi/sura3-aya7.html); [English translation](https://tulayhah.wordpress.com/2016/10/31/who-knows-the-taweel-imam-al-sadi/))

This is a profound point: there are **two types of mutashabih**:
1. **Mutashabih whose reality only Allah knows** (e.g., the essence of His attributes, the exact reality of the Hereafter) — stop at "except Allah."
2. **Mutashabih whose interpretation can be known by the scholars** (by referring it back to the muhkam) — "those firm in knowledge" are connected with Allah.

## 10. The Quran Describes Itself as Both Muhkam and Mutashabih (in Different Senses)

IslamWeb (Arabic) points out an important linguistic observation:

> *"والإحكام والتشابه من حيث اللغة يطلقان على القرآن الكريم عامة، فيوصف بأنه محكم وبأنه متشابه، كما في قوله سبحانه وتعالى: كِتَابٌ أُحْكِمَتْ آيَاتُهُ ثُمَّ فُصِّلَتْ مِنْ لَدُنْ حَكِيمٍ خَبِيرٍ {هود: 1}. وكما في قوله سبحانه وتعالى: كِتَابًا مُّتَشَابِهاً مَّثَانِيَ {الزمر: 23}. فهو في غاية الإحكام والإتقان اللفظي والسمو المعنوي والدقة في التعبير والمعلومة، وهو كذلك متشابه ومتماثل في ألفاظه ومعانيه ويصدق بعضه بعضاً ولا يوجد فيه اختلاف أو تناقض"*

> *"Linguistically, both 'ihkam' (precision) and 'tashabuh' (similarity) are used to describe the entire Quran. It is described as muhkam, as in His statement: 'A Book whose verses have been perfected and then detailed...' (Hud 11:1). And it is described as mutashabih, as in His statement: 'A Book, consistent and paired...' (Az-Zumar 39:23). So it is in the utmost of precision, linguistic perfection, and meaningful elevation, and it is likewise mutashabih (consistent/similar) in its words and meanings — part of it confirms part of it, and there is no difference or contradiction in it."*
> — **IslamWeb (Arabic)**, Fatwa 55396 ([source](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/55396/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%82-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%87))

This shows that the Quran uses the terms "muhkam" and "mutashabih" in **different senses** depending on context:
- In Surah Hud 11:1, the **entire Quran** is described as "muhkam" (perfected/precise).
- In Surah Az-Zumar 39:23, the **entire Quran** is described as "mutashabih" (consistent/paired — its parts resemble and confirm each other).
- In Surah Al Imran 3:7, the verses are **divided** into muhkam (clear) and mutashabih (unclear).

So when Allah describes the Quran as a "clear book" (Kitab Mubeen), He is referring to the **overall clarity of its guidance, its perfected wording, and its self-consistency** — not denying that some specific verses have meanings that require scholarly interpretation.

---

## 11. Direct Answer to the Question

**Does the existence of ambiguous (mutashabih) verses make the Quran an "unclear book"? No.** Here is the summary of the scholars' reconciliation:

1. **The Quran is clear in its overall guidance and purpose.** Allah describes it as clear to establish irrefutable proof against humanity. The core message — Tawheed, prophethood, the Hereafter, the basic rulings — is crystal clear and repeated throughout.

2. **The muhkam verses are the majority and the foundation.** They contain the rulings, laws, and obligations that people must implement. They are clear to everyone.

3. **The mutashabih verses are a minority.** They are not unclear to everyone — the scholars understand them. There is **no verse whose meaning is unknown to all people** (except matters of the unseen whose reality only Allah knows).

4. **The mutashabih is explained by the muhkam.** When the mutashabih is referred back to the muhkam, "all of it becomes muhkam (clear)" — as As-Sa'di said. The Quran explains itself, and the Sunnah explains the Quran.

5. **The wisdom behind mutashabih verses is a test.** They distinguish those who sincerely believe and refer to scholars from those with deviation in their hearts who exploit ambiguity to create fitnah.

6. **There is no contradiction.** The Quran describes itself as "clear" in a general sense (overall guidance, perfected wording, self-consistency) and acknowledges that some specific verses require scholarly interpretation. Both are true. A book can be clear in its overall message while containing some passages that require deeper study — this is true of any profound text, and it does not make the book "unclear."

7. **The believer's methodology**: Act on the muhkam, believe in the mutashabih, and entrust the unknown to Allah — as Al-Hasan al-Basri (d. 110H) said.

The Quran is a clear book. The existence of mutashabih verses does not negate that — it is part of Allah's wisdom, and those verses are explained by the clear verses and by the scholars.
